# Frictionless recipient capture: occasion reminders, recipient share cards and EU consent limits

*Research notes compiled 1 November 2026. Evidence from 2024–2026 is preferred; older material is marked "historical".*

*How the sources were read: this session's network allowlist blocked direct page fetches from moonpig.group, help.partiful.com, reedsmith.com, MDN, webkit.org, EUR-Lex, edpb.europa.eu, ico.org.uk and aepd.es. Findings attributed to those pages come from search-engine extracts, and the cited URL is the original page. Apple developer documentation (developer.apple.com) was read directly. Check any figure that carries a decision against its primary source before relying on it. None of this is legal advice.*

## 1. How the leaders capture recipients (Partiful, Moonpig, Paperless Post, Evite, Facebook, Hallmark, Funky Pigeon)

### Takeaway
Moonpig built its 113 million reminders on the sender's side. Logged-in buyers save dates themselves, either by hand or by importing them from their phone calendar, with a voucher as the incentive. Each reminder then reaches the buyer when they are ready to buy: nearly 40% of orders arrive within seven days of one. Partiful is the only leader found that captures the recipient (the guest) directly. Its texted-code phone check is the price of an RSVP the guest already wants to send. No leader was found asking the recipient of a card or gift for their own occasion dates.

### Cited Findings

**Moonpig Group (Moonpig UK/US, Greetz NL)**
- Size of the reminder database over time:
  - 84m at April 2023 and 90m at 30 April 2024 — [Moonpig Annual Report 2024](https://moonpig.group/media/1nrnq4jc/annual-report-and-accounts-2024.pdf)
  - 101m at 30 April 2025 — [FY25 investor factsheet](https://www.moonpig.group/media/dfnfliw1/moonpig-group-plc-fy25-full-year-results-investor-factsheet.pdf)
  - 107m at 31 October 2025 — [H1 FY26 factsheet](https://www.moonpig.group/media/53uonjys/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-half-year-results-investor-factsheet.pdf)
  - "113m Moonpig and Greetz customer occasion reminders set as at 30 April 2026" — [FY26 full-year investor factsheet](https://www.moonpig.group/media/0tqhyd35/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-full-year-results-investor-factsheet.pdf)
- Conversion: "nearly 40% of Moonpig orders are placed within seven days of a customer receiving the relevant occasion reminder." The company calls reminders "a core part of how we retain customers and drive purchase frequency… at moments of high purchase intent" — [FY25 results announcement, 26 June 2025](https://www.moonpig.group/media/ylcfmqgj/moonpig-group-plc-fy25-full-year-results-announcementpdf.pdf); [FY25 Annual Report CEO review](https://www.moonpig.group/media/pciaffpq/moonpig-group-plc-annual-report-2025-ceo-review.pdf)
- Repeat revenue: "nearly nine tenths of Moonpig and Greetz revenue comes from existing customers" — [FY25 results announcement](https://www.moonpig.group/media/ylcfmqgj/moonpig-group-plc-fy25-full-year-results-announcementpdf.pdf)
- Membership: Moonpig Plus members set 2.5x more occasion reminders than active customers who are not members — [FY25 results announcement](https://www.moonpig.group/media/ylcfmqgj/moonpig-group-plc-fy25-full-year-results-announcementpdf.pdf)
- Checkout and timing: the FY24 report describes a "date first" checkout that "clearly informs customers about the possibility of scheduling their orders for cards and gifts in advance". It also says the first reminder now goes out 14 days before each occasion — [Annual Report 2024](https://moonpig.group/media/1nrnq4jc/annual-report-and-accounts-2024.pdf)
- Where dates are captured (help centre): reminders live in the "My Reminders" section of the customer's account. The customer saves the date plus the recipient's name, "connection to you" and occasion, which Moonpig uses to suggest cards. Dates can be imported from the phone's calendar or entered by hand, and an email goes out before each one — [Moonpig help: How do reminders work](https://help.moonpig.com/en/articles/318961-how-do-reminders-work)
- US timing and incentive: emails arrive "a week before each occasion, and again 3 days before". The offer is "Set 3 reminders for an instant 40% off voucher, plus get an extra 20% off when each date approaches" — [Moonpig US add-reminder page](https://www.moonpig.com/us/add-reminder)
  - An older UK version described a 20% discount after three reminders, reusable the following year — [TopCashback Moonpig promotion page](https://www.topcashback.co.uk/moonpig-promotion)
  - The UK annual report (14 days) and the US page (7 and 3 days) disagree on timing. The likely reasons are different markets and dates, and possibly postal lead times.
- App: "Set birthday reminders – use it as a countdown… Make sure you enable notifications!" — [Moonpig App Store listing (GB)](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/moonpig-birthday-cards/id393730279)

**Partiful (event invites)**
- RSVP flow: the guest picks a response such as "Going", then enters their name and phone number. A texted code is entered in a popup, and the RSVP is complete once the code is accepted — [Partiful help: How do I RSVP](https://help.partiful.com/hc/en-us/articles/34230743189787-How-do-I-RSVP-to-an-event-on-Partiful)
- Guests invited by email must still log in with a phone number to RSVP. Troubleshooting offers a WhatsApp code as a fallback and a "Retry" after 30 seconds. These come from help-centre search extracts; the exact article could not be fetched — [Partiful help centre](https://help.partiful.com/hc/en-us/articles/11974255411355)
- Partiful also has an opt-in for "verified iMessage notifications" (article title only; content not read) — [Partiful help](https://help.partiful.com/hc/en-us/articles/34049238292891-How-can-I-opt-into-verified-iMessage-notifications)
- Hosts share one link through texts, Instagram, WhatsApp and group chats, "without guests needing to download an app or check email first" — [Sacra profile](https://sacra.com/chat/h/47abcb5f-90c8-462d-8ee9-09bbf4909254/)
- Growth: roughly 500,000 monthly users in Q1 2025, up about 400% year on year, and about 2 million users added in 2025. These are Sensor Tower estimates plus company statements; Partiful does not disclose monthly users — [CNBC via NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/?amp=1); [NBC Philadelphia](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/4164362/?amp=1)
- Screenshots of Partiful's celebrity-lookalike contest pages spread the Partiful link to large audiences — [NoGood analysis](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)

**Paperless Post and Evite (address books belong to the host)**
- Paperless Post adds anyone you have sent to into your address book automatically. Bulk CSV import is available on desktop through a template — [Paperless Post help: Managing contacts](https://paperlesspost.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005890986-Managing-Contacts-Using-the-Address-Book)
- Paperless Post can "send event reminders and messages to groups of guests by RSVP status, or with helpful Guest Tags" — [Paperless Post features](https://paperlesspost.com/features)
- Evite: "guests added to your event guest list will automatically be added to your address book" — [Evite support: Manage address book](https://support.evite.com/account/account-questions/manage-address-book)
- Evite sends one automatic reminder two days before the event or RSVP deadline, and hosts can schedule up to five. The wording cannot be changed. This comes from a third-party comparison blog run by an event-tool vendor — [Carly blog: Evite vs Paperless Post](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/evite-vs-paperless-post/)

**Hallmark and Funky Pigeon**
- In the Hallmark Cards Now app, Hallmark Reminders notify the user two weeks before an occasion — [App Store: Hallmark Cards Now](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1673592071)
- The Funky Pigeon app lets users add dates to their calendar and has "built in reminders for milestones" — [App Store: Funky Pigeon](https://apps.apple.com/app/id441509555)
- Card Factory acquired Funky Pigeon in July 2025 — [CB Insights comparison](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/funky-pigeon-vs-hallmark-cards)
- No evidence was found that either brand sends occasion reminders by email. Searches surfaced only their marketing emails — [Milled: Funky Pigeon emails](https://milled.com/funky-pigeon/dont-miss-out-get-fathers-day-sorted-dA4dgL1CAYXFAqlZ)

### Inferences
- Moonpig's growth rate (my arithmetic from the cited figures): +6m reminders in FY24, +11m in FY25 and +12m in FY26, about 12% a year. The increase coincides with the date-first checkout and the reminder vouchers. The reports do not separate the causes.
- Every leader except Partiful captures dates from the buyer, inside an account, often paying for them with a discount. Asking a card's or gift's recipient for a date without an account was not found at any leader, so no conversion benchmark exists for it.
- Partiful attaches verification to the action the guest came to do (RSVP), so the phone number arrives as a by-product. Ethos's comparable actions are "Keep this gift" and "send one back".
- The 14-day lead time (Moonpig UK, Hallmark) reflects postage. A digital gift arrives instantly, so the 7-day reminder Moonpig US uses, plus an optional 3-day one, fits a link-based product.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm an explicit "add a reminder" step at Moonpig checkout, or whether reminders are created automatically from a delivery date, pre-ticked, or prompted after the order. Only the "date first" scheduling checkout is documented. The source breakdown of the 113m reminders (manual entry, calendar import, checkout, app) is not published.
- Facebook birthdays: I found no reliable 2024–2026 source on changes to birthday notifications. Results were SEO how-to pages.
- Partiful publishes no figures for SMS-verification completion, opt-outs, or how many guests go on to host.

## 2. Zero-account reminder mechanisms and their reliability (calendar links and .ics files, Wallet passes, iOS web push, SMS vs email, passkeys)

### Takeaway
Without an account, only email or SMS let a brand reach someone again on its own initiative, and both need consent in the EU (see section 5). The "user-held" options put the reminder on the person's own device. A calendar event works well from iOS Safari but less reliably in the Google Calendar app and iOS Chrome. A Wallet pass shows passively on the Lock Screen and needs Apple signing certificates and a server. iOS web push requires a Home Screen install, which few people do. Passkeys sign in very reliably (93% success) but still create an account.

### Cited Findings

**Calendar links and .ics files**
- On iPhone, opening a calendar link in Safari prompted the user to add the event to iOS Calendar. The same .ics download offered no calendar option in iOS Chrome, even with the Google Calendar app installed. This is a community thread of uncertain date; treat it as historical — [Marketo Nation thread](https://nation.marketo.com/thread/27884)
- The Google Calendar mobile app cannot import .ics files directly; import goes through the web version. Outlook.com accepts an .ics upload through its calendar settings — [UNISON guide](https://learning.unison.org.uk/how-to-add-an-icalendar-file-to-your-calendar/)
- Google needs its own link format and does not treat iCalendar links the same way — [Marketo Nation thread](https://nation.marketo.com/thread/27884)
- A vendor claims bulk emails with attachments are filtered more aggressively, and that teams switching to links see about 3x more sign-ups. This is unverified marketing copy — [AddCal ICS generator](https://addcal.co/tools/ics-generator)

**Apple Wallet and Google Wallet passes**
- A pass can show on the Lock Screen when it is relevant ("A pass can be relevant on a date, at a location, or both").
  - A date is set with the `relevantDates` key for boarding passes, event tickets and generic passes.
  - Passes without locations display on the relevant date.
  - The Simulator does not show Lock Screen passes.
  — [Apple: Showing a pass on the Lock Screen](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/showing-a-pass-on-the-lock-screen)
- `relevantDate` is deprecated. The replacement, `relevantDates`, is "an array of objects that represent date intervals that the system uses to show a relevant pass" — [Apple: Pass object reference](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/pass)
- Distribution: as an email attachment, a web-page download or an "Add to Apple Wallet" button. An optional web service can update a pass later, for example to change an event time — [Apple: Distributing and updating a pass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/distributing-and-updating-a-pass)
- Signing: each pass needs a signing certificate for a pass type ID, generated in the Apple Developer portal, and a PKCS #7 detached signature of the manifest — [Apple: Building a pass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/building-a-pass)
- Safari can import pass files straight into Wallet (archived guide; historical) — [Apple PassKit Programming Guide (archive)](https://developer.apple.com/library/content/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/PassKit_PG/DistributingPasses.html)
- For certain passes, such as event tickets, the system can also start a Live Activity — [Apple HIG: Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet)
- Google Wallet began reading .pkpass files in spring 2024, but inconsistently. In testing, one ticket site blocked the attempt because it "required Safari" — [9to5Google, 4 April 2024](https://9to5google.com/2024/04/04/google-wallet-apple-wallet-pass-files-how/)
- A Google Wallet "save" link is a JWT signed by a server that holds Google service-account credentials, so a static link will not work. This is a third-party explainer; Google's own docs could not be fetched — [WalletWallet blog](https://walletwallet.alen.ro/blog/pkpass-to-google-wallet/)

**Web push on iOS**
- iOS/iPadOS 16.4 (released in 2023, historical but still the governing rule) added Web Push for Home Screen web apps only — [WebKit blog: Web Push for Web Apps on iOS and iPadOS](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/)
- Requirements:
  - A plain Safari page cannot request push permission; the site must first be added to the Home Screen.
  - The permission request must follow a direct user interaction.
  - The site needs a web app manifest with the correct `display` value.
  — [OneSignal docs: Web push for iOS](https://documentation.onesignal.com/docs/web-push-for-ios)
- A vendor guide in the same search reports that Chrome and Firefox on iOS do not support web push. Which vendor wrote it was not confirmed — [OneSignal docs: Mobile Web Push for iOS](https://documentation.onesignal.com/docs/ios-web-push)
- No measured PWA install rate was found. One developer wrote that they had never seen a non-developer iOS user add a website to the Home Screen (anecdote) — [steele.blue: Web push on iOS 17](https://www.steele.blue/web-push-ios17)

**SMS vs email**
- Klaviyo's 2026 SMS benchmarks, drawn from more than 183,000 customers:
  - Click rate: 5.6% for campaigns and 9.65% for flows.
  - Ecommerce order rate: 0.27% for campaigns and 1.9% for flows.
  — [Klaviyo SMS benchmarks](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/sms-marketing/benchmarks)
- Klaviyo's average email click rate is 1.69% — [Klaviyo email benchmarks](https://klaviyo.com/blog/ecommerce-benchmarks-2021)
- Omnisend's figures:
  - SMS campaigns: 12.39% click rate, 0.12% conversion.
  - Automated SMS: 20.34% click-through, about 0.77% conversion.
  - Email campaigns convert at 0.08%, automated email at 1.49%.
  - Omnisend's "98% SMS open rate" is an inferred figure, since texts send no read receipts.
  — [Omnisend SMS benchmarks](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/sms-marketing-benchmarks/); [Omnisend SMS statistics](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/sms-marketing-statistics/)
- Klaviyo and Omnisend define clicks and conversions differently, so their numbers cannot be compared directly — [Omnisend SMS statistics](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/sms-marketing-statistics/)

**One-tap identity (passkeys, Sign in with Apple)**
- FIDO Passkey Index, October 2025: passkeys succeed at sign-in 93% of the time, against 63% for other methods. The sample is a confidential survey of nine member organisations, including Amazon, Google, Microsoft and PayPal — [FIDO Passkey Index Oct 2025](https://fidoalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FIDO-Passkey-Index-October-2025.pdf)
  - A passkey sign-in takes about 8.5 seconds, against 31.2 seconds for MFA — [Mobile Ecosystem Forum, 12 Nov 2025](https://mobileecosystemforum.com/2025/11/12/whats-up-with-passkeys-the-verdict-is-in/)
- FIDO Passkey Index, October 2026: 93% for passkeys against 78% for passwords plus traditional MFA. Passkeys made up 36% of successful sign-ins, up from 26% in 2025, and are "nearly three times faster" — [FIDO Passkey Index Oct 2026](https://fidoalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Passkey-Index-October-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The best no-account reminder with nothing stored is "Add to calendar". It can be offered as a browser-generated .ics file for Apple devices and a Google Calendar template link for Android and Gmail users. The event can carry a 7-day alert and a link back to the brand, and the user's own calendar fires the reminder, so nothing is sent by the brand. The cost is that the brand cannot measure beyond the tap and cannot follow up.
- Wallet passes fit the use case technically: a generic pass can take a `relevantDates` interval that starts a week before the date. They need a lot of setup, though: an Apple pass-type certificate, server-side signing and a separate Google JWT backend. The Lock Screen display is passive (Apple describes it as showing the pass, not alerting). Repeating next year needs a pass-update web service. A pass works better as a keepsake than as a reminder channel.
- iOS web push is effectively unavailable to a one-time recipient because of the Home Screen requirement.
- Email is the most practical channel through which a brand can reach someone again. SMS gets higher click rates but costs more per message, and in the EU it needs the same consent (SMS counts as "electronic mail", see section 5). A passkey or Sign in with Apple adds an account step that is unnecessary if an email address is the only identity needed.

### Gaps
- I found no published PWA or Add-to-Home-Screen install rates.
- I found no tests of whether alarms (VALARM) inside .ics files survive import on Apple, Google and Outlook. This needs device testing.
- I found no conversion data for Sign in with Apple.
- Google's documentation on lock-screen relevance for generic passes could not be fetched.

## 3. Timing and form design (peak-end rule, one field vs several, post-experience capture benchmarks, date picker vs text)

### Takeaway
The peak-end rule is well established for how people remember experiences. No study was found showing that asking for data at the emotional peak raises opt-in. Popup benchmarks put cold email capture at about 2–5% of views, with no loss up to three fields and a drop at four. For a remembered date such as a birthday, government design systems recommend typed fields over a calendar date picker.

### Cited Findings
- Peak-end rule: in Kahneman et al. (1993, *Psychological Science*; historical), participants endured 60 seconds of 14°C water, or the same 60 seconds plus 30 more in slightly warmer water. More chose to repeat the longer trial, because retrospective ratings track the peak and the end. The rule applies to experiences with a clear start and end. A vacation study found remembered happiness was better predicted by the "most memorable or most unusual 24-h period" — [Wikipedia: Peak–end rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule)
- Popup capture: Omnisend's analysis of 1.24 billion popup displays found a 2.1% average conversion in 2025, down from 2.3% in 2024. Below 1.5% counts as underperforming and 5% or more as excellent — [Omnisend email popup statistics](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/email-popup-statistics/)
  - Field count: forms with one to three fields convert at about 2.1–2.2%, and four fields drop to 1.5% (same source).
  - Other Omnisend pages give a 3.77% average signup rate, apparently with a different denominator (same source).
- Wisepops reports 4.82% for email-signup popups (via an aggregator) — [Crazy Egg popup statistics](https://www.crazyegg.com/blog/popup-statistics/)
- Popupsmart reports 3.49%, but measures against interactions — [Popupsmart popup statistics](https://popupsmart.com/blog/popup-statistics)
- Klaviyo publishes no fixed average. It rates each form Poor, Fair, Good or Excellent against peers over the last three months. From 1 April 2025 it filters bot traffic out of form analytics, so earlier and later figures cannot be compared — [Klaviyo help: Signup form analytics](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015960712)
- Field-count evidence:
  - Imagescape (2008, historical) cut a contact form from 11 fields to 4 and saw 160% more submissions. Retellings cite "+120%, 5.4% to 11.9%" — [Imagescape case study](https://imagescape.com/filer/canonical/1455648270/152); [CXL: Should you really reduce form fields?](https://cxl.com/blog/reduce-form-fields/)
  - HubSpot's Dan Zarrella studied more than 40,000 landing pages (historical) and found conversion falls slightly as fields increase, "but not as steeply as I expected" — [HubSpot blog](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/6746/which-types-of-form-fields-lower-landing-page-conversions.aspx)
  - Counter-examples: an Unbounce test cutting nine fields to six lowered conversions. A B2B form cut from 8 fields to 3 raised conversions 40%, but 65% of the new leads were unqualified — [CXL](https://cxl.com/blog/reduce-form-fields/)
- Date entry:
  - The US Web Design System advises against date pickers for dates of birth. Its "memorable date" component uses text inputs and suggests a picker suits scheduling instead — [USWDS date-of-birth pattern](https://designsystem.digital.gov/patterns/create-a-user-profile/date-of-birth/); [USWDS memorable date](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/memorable-date/)
  - In eye-tracking, three text boxes gave "a much smoother scan path and lower fixation count" than dropdowns or date pickers. Zuko advises always offering text entry alongside a picker for past dates — [Zuko: Optimizing date fields](https://www.zuko.io/blog/optimizing-date-fields-on-forms)
  - GOV.UK found text fields "tested well", with the main hesitation being whether to type a leading zero (2013, historical) — [GOV.UK Design Notes](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2013/12/05/asking-for-a-date-of-birth/)
  - A 2010 WHATWG request for a native `type="month-day"` input shows the long-standing gap for year-less dates (historical) — [WHATWG mailing list, Aug 2010](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-whatwg-archive/2010Aug/0256.html)

### Inferences
- A birthday or anniversary reminder needs only the day and month. Leaving out the year meets data minimisation and removes the hardest part of date entry, scrolling back through years. Two short selects or text boxes (day, month), or a picker limited to month and day, follow the USWDS reasoning for memorable dates.
- Omnisend's data suggests a second field (email plus date) costs little against one field; the drop comes at four fields. This is cold-popup data, not post-experience data.
- The peak-end rule implies the end screen of a gift shapes how the whole gift is remembered. An optional ask there should be gentle and dismissible, never a gate. If it spoils the ending, it could lower the remembered quality of the gift, and with it the likelihood of a reply or share. This is extrapolation, not tested.
- A capture that offers clear value (keep the gift, be reminded of their day) should beat cold popups at 2–5%. That is my expectation, not a measured result.

### Gaps
- I found no study of asking for contact details right after an emotional peak.
- I found no published opt-in benchmark for post-experience prompts in gifting or e-cards, and no Typeform benchmark; the vendor sites returned nothing usable.
- I found no Baymard data on date-of-birth fields.

## 4. Share prompts for recipients (Web Share API Level 2, screenshot-first design, published share rates)

### Takeaway
Sharing image files from the mobile web works on iOS Safari (14/15 onwards) and Android Chrome (76 onwards) through the OS share sheet. No reliable way exists for the web to send an image straight into Instagram Stories; that route needs native app code. Spotify's own share count includes screenshots and downloads, so the card has to work as a screenshot. Duolingo and Strava publish no share rates.

### Cited Findings
- Browser support for `navigator.canShare()` with files:
  - Safari and iOS Safari 14, Chrome Android 76 — [web-features: Web Share](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/share.json)
  - Desktop Chrome 128; Firefox only behind a flag — [web.dev: Web Share API](https://web.dev/articles/web-share)
- Sharing must start from a user action such as a click, and only on HTTPS. Sites should check `canShare()` before sharing files — [web.dev: Web Share API](https://web.dev/articles/web-share)
- iOS quirks from developer reports (community sources, about 2021–2023):
  - File sharing was behind a flag in iOS 14 and worked in iOS 15.
  - Text and files cannot be shared together on iOS, so developers share the image alone.
  — [Stack Overflow archive 66661995](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/66661995/navigator-share-file-not-working-on-ios-14-safari); [Stack Overflow archive 76076190](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/76076190/sharing-an-image-using-the-webshare-api-in-ios-is-failing)
- On iOS 16, "Save to Photos" was missing from the Web Share sheet for images. A later reply says an update fixed it (unverified) — [Apple Developer Forums 729782](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/729782)
- Instagram Feed: media that met the Feed requirements failed to share from a web page on iPhone, while manual uploads worked. The thread shows no fix — [Meta developer community thread](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/community/threads/1094504835445267/)
- Instagram Stories needs native code. On iOS, assets go onto the pasteboard under `com.instagram.sharedSticker.*` keys and the app opens the `instagram-stories://` scheme, a native-only technique — [social_story_share Flutter plugin docs](https://pub.dev/documentation/social_story_share/0.2.1/)
- Even native Android libraries report a black screen when sharing to Instagram Stories, while Feed works — [react-native-share issue #1243](https://github.com/react-native-share/react-native-share/issues/1243)
- Spotify Wrapped 2025 (launched early December 2025):
  - More than 200m engaged users in the first 24 hours, up 19% year on year. In 2024 the same milestone took 62 hours. "Engaged" means viewing at least one story — [TechCrunch, 4 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)
  - More than 500m shares on day one, up 41%. "Shares" include native shares, screenshots and downloads — [AOL/CNET](https://www.aol.com/articles/spotify-wrapped-2025-wraps-more-213100251.html); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/record-breaking-spotify-wrapped-2025-sees-200m-engage-500m-shares-day-1760640)
- Duolingo Year in Review 2025 (rolled out December 2025) ends with a shareable summary card, and users get a bonus for sharing. No share metrics were published — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/duolingo-year-in-review-2025-3621782)
- Strava Year in Sport 2025 covered 180m users and 14bn kudos. The recap moved behind the Premium paywall, which upset users. No share statistics were published — [BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-year-in-sport-2025); [Ars Technica via Harvard TagTeam](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/3415/feed_items/17132945/about)
- Partiful: screenshots of its lookalike-contest pages carried the brand link — [NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)

### Inferences
- My arithmetic: Spotify's day-one figures work out to about 2.5 shares per engaged user (500m / 200m). Because screenshots count, a card that looks complete as a 9:16 screenshot, with the brand URL drawn into the image, captures shares that no button can track.
- A sound pattern for 2026 mobile web:
  - Generate a PNG.
  - Call `navigator.share({ files: [png] })` with no text on iOS.
  - Offer "Save image" as a fallback.
  Promising a one-tap "Share to Instagram Stories" from a web page would be unreliable; the user picks Instagram from the share sheet, or saves the image and posts it manually.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 device test matrix for Web Share targets (Instagram Stories vs Feed, TikTok, WhatsApp Status) on iOS 18/26 Safari and current Android Chrome. Meta's and TikTok's sharing docs could not be fetched. This needs real-device testing.
- Duolingo and Strava publish no share rates. Spotify publishes only totals, not a share rate per viewer.

## 5. EU law: reminders to non-customers, one-tap consent, storing a third party's birthday, Spain (LSSI) and UK (PECR), calendar files

### Takeaway
A gift recipient who has never bought anything is not a "customer", so the ePrivacy soft opt-in does not cover them. An email or SMS reminder that encourages them to send a gift counts as direct marketing and needs prior, specific, unticked-by-default consent. That consent can be one deliberate tap on a clearly worded control; scrolling, swiping through a page or pre-ticked boxes do not count. Saving the sender's birthday makes the company a controller of third-party data, which brings duties to have a lawful basis, to minimise, and to give notice under Article 14. A calendar file generated in the user's browser and never sent to the server involves no storage or sending by the company. That is an inference; no authority has said so.

### Cited Findings

**ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC)**
- Article 13(1) requires prior consent for direct marketing by email. Article 13(2), the "soft opt-in", is a narrow exception. It covers contact details obtained from a customer "in the context of the sale of a product or a service", used only for the company's own similar products, with a clear chance to object both at collection and in every message — [Presencis: ePrivacy Article 13](https://presencis.com/regulations/eprivacy-directive/article-13/); [iubenda: soft opt-in](https://www.iubenda.com/en/?p=231548)
  - Leads and contacts who have never bought do not qualify — [Presencis: Soft opt-in definition](https://presencis.com/regulations/eprivacy-directive/definitions/soft-opt-in/)
- SMS counts as "electronic mail":
  - Article 2(h) defines electronic mail as any text, voice, sound or image message stored in the network or on the recipient's device until collected — [ePrivacy Directive Art 2 (legislation.gov.uk)](https://legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2002/58/article/2/data.pdf)
  - Recital 40 covers "e-mails, including SMS messages", and the Article 29 Working Party's WP90 confirms it — [Article 29 WP opinion WP90](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2004/wp90_fr.pdf)
  - Recital 67 of Directive 2009/136 extends the same safeguards to SMS and MMS — [CJEU referral excerpt (vLex)](https://eu.vlex.com/vid/stwl-stadtische-werke-lauf-878569736)
  - One vendor source says some member states read "electronic mail" narrowly, which conflicts with Recital 40 — [compliance-ai summary (low reliability)](https://compliance-ai-h.pages.dev/regulations/eu/eprivacy-directive/direct-marketing)
- CJEU C-654/23 *Inteligo Media*, 13 November 2025:
  - The Court read "in the context of the sale" broadly, so a free account in a freemium model where the company earns indirectly can qualify.
  - Where the soft opt-in conditions are met, no separate GDPR Article 6 legal basis is needed.
  — [Reed Smith Viewpoints](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102luvt/ecj-rules-on-soft-opt-in-for-freemium-newsletters-no-separate-gdpr-legal-basis-r/); [Chambers (Legalink)](https://chambers.com/articles/relaxation-of-the-rules-on-direct-marketing-the-court-of-justice-of-the-eu-is-changing-the-view-of)

**What valid consent looks like (EDPB Guidelines 05/2020)**
- The EDPB adopted the guidelines on 4 May 2020.
  - Scrolling, swiping through a page or similar activity is not a "clear affirmative action".
  - Pre-ticked boxes, silence and inactivity are never valid consent.
  - Different purposes need separate ("granular") consent.
  - A service must not be made conditional on consent to something unrelated.
  — [EDPB consent summary (PDF, 2026)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-04/edpb-summary-consent_en.pdf); [Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/ip-technology-and-data/the-european-data-protection-board-edpb-has-adopted-revised-guidelines-on-consent-under-gdpr/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/edpb-guidelines-for-consent/)
- The CJEU's *Planet49* ruling (C-673/17) held that pre-ticked boxes are not valid consent — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/edpb-guidelines-for-consent/)

**Service messages vs marketing (UK ICO view, persuasive in EU practice)**
- A service message is sent "for administrative or customer service purposes only". If a service message contains any promotional element, "the whole message would amount to direct marketing". Phrasing, tone and context decide, and a message that "actively promotes or encourages" use of a service is likely to be marketing — [DPO Centre](https://www.dpocentre.com/marketing-emails-the-fine-line-between-a-service-and-sales-message/); [Lewis Silkin](https://lewissilkin.com/en/insights/blurred-lines-in-the-thicke-of-the-difference)
- The ICO fined EE £100,000 (2019, historical) for "service" texts that promoted the My EE app to people who had opted out — [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/100000-fine-sending-marketing-text-messages-consent-withdrawn/)

**Refer-a-friend and messages sent on a user's behalf**
- The ICO's 2020 draft code said refer-a-friend schemes were hard to square with PECR and UK GDPR, because the company cannot verify a friend's consent — [Bird & Bird, 2020](https://twobirds.com/insights/2020/uk/information-commissioner-consults-on-draft-code-of-practice-on-direct-marketing)
- A 2023 ICO update: encouraging referrers "will not be a breach if the referrer controls how the message is sent to referees". The advice is "ensure that you are not the instigator of the messages" — [Foot Anstey](https://www.footanstey.com/our-insights/articles-news/what-do-you-need-to-know-about-the-icos-latest-guidance-on-refer-a-friend-schemes/)
- France (CNIL): data collected about a referred friend must not be kept after the referral "sauf consentement exprès", and the consent box must not be pre-ticked (2016 fiche, historical) — [CNIL fiche Parrainage](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/typo/document/Parrainage_Fiches_Commerce.pdf)
  - A secondary source adds that the address serves only that one suggestion, and one invitation does not authorise repeated follow-ups — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://donneespersonnelles.fr/parrainage-email-prospection-rgpd)

**UK PECR after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025**
- The PECR changes took effect on 5 February 2026 through Commencement No. 6 Regulations.
  - The maximum PECR fine rose from £500,000 to £17.5m or 4% of global turnover, whichever is higher.
  - Charities gained a soft opt-in.
  — [MFMac](https://www.mfmac.com/insights/data-protection/data-use-and-access-act-2025-increased-maximum-fines-for-cookies-and-direct-marketing-practices/); [Russell-Cooke](https://www.russell-cooke.co.uk/news-and-insights/news/the-data-use-and-access-act-changes-to-the-soft-opt-in-rules-for-charities-explained); [Blake Morgan](https://www.blakemorgan.co.uk/data-use-and-access-act-2025-privacy-and-electronic-communications-regulations/)
  - An older Brabners article expected June 2026, which conflicts with the February 2026 date; the later sources are preferred — [Brabners](https://www.brabners.com/insights/data-protection/data-use-and-access-act-2025-how-opt-in-rules-are-changing-for-charities)

**Spain (LSSI 34/2002, enforced by the AEPD)**
- Article 21.1 bans advertising or promotional messages by email "u otro medio equivalente" (or other equivalent means) unless previously requested or expressly authorised. Article 21.2 allows them where a prior contract exists, the contact details were obtained lawfully, the products are similar to those originally bought, and an opt-out is offered — [AEPD resolution A/00255/2015](https://www.aepd.es/documento/a-00255-2015.pdf); [AEPD A/00081/2015](https://www.aepd.es/documento/a-00081-2015.pdf)
- Article 22.1 lets people withdraw consent at any time by simple notice — [AEPD E/07180/2019](https://www.aepd.es/documento/e-07180-2019.pdf)
- The AEPD says "el artículo 21 LSSI no admite un consentimiento tácito" (Article 21 does not allow tacit consent) — [AEPD report 2018-0164](https://www.aepd.es/es/documento/2018-0164.pdf)
- The AEPD applies Article 21 to SMS — [AEPD A/00291/2014](https://www.aepd.es/documento/a-00291-2014.pdf)
- Breaching Article 21 is a minor infringement under Article 38.4.d unless it qualifies as serious. Most cited resolutions date from 2011–2020 — [AEPD resolutions via search](https://www.aepd.es/documento/00004-2019.pdf)

**Storing a third party's data (the sender's birthday entered by the recipient)**
- GDPR Article 14 applies when personal data is not obtained from the person it describes. The controller must tell them the purposes, the legal basis and, under Article 6(1)(f), the legitimate interests pursued — [GDPR Art 14 (legislation.gov.uk)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/14/data.htm)
  - Timing: within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month, or at the first communication with the person — [Presencis: GDPR Article 14](https://presencis.com/regulations/gdpr/article-14/); [isms.online](https://isms.online/general-data-protection-regulation-gdpr/gdpr-article-14-compliance)
  - The Article 14(5)(b) exemption (impossible or disproportionate effort) is read restrictively by the EDPB, per secondary commentary — [Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/gdpr-article-14-third-party.html)

### Inferences (interpretation, not legal advice)
- Reminder to a recipient who has not bought anything:
  - The soft opt-in does not apply, and "Their birthday is next week, send them something" is promotional by ICO standards. Email or SMS therefore needs prior consent, both under ePrivacy Article 13(1) and in Spain under LSSI 21.1, where tacit consent is excluded.
  - A dedicated, clearly labelled control would plausibly count as one-tap consent: a button whose only effect is "Email me a reminder 7 days before Anna's birthday", or an unticked box beside an email field. The wording must name the purpose, the timing and the brand, and link to the privacy notice.
  - Bundling it into "Keep this gift" (a service) without a separate choice would fail the granularity rule.
- *Inteligo Media* (C-654/23) might let a recipient who takes a free but indirectly paid-for action ("send one back" free, funded by the paid gift) count as a "customer" for soft opt-in on similar products. That is untested for this model, and consent is the safer basis.
- The gift-delivery email itself is sent at the sender's instigation, which resembles referral logic. The ICO and CNIL positions suggest it must not turn into an ongoing marketing relationship with the recipient unless the recipient opts in separately.
- Saving the sender's birthday:
  - The company becomes a controller of data obtained from the recipient rather than the sender.
  - Plausible lawful basis: legitimate interests (Art 6(1)(f)) in providing a reminder the recipient asked for, supported by a documented balancing test.
  - Minimisation: day and month only, first name only, deleted after a set time or when the recipient withdraws consent.
  - Article 14 notice: probably satisfied through the sender's own privacy notice or receipt, since the sender is a customer whose contact details are already held.
  - The recipient's own household exemption does not extend to the company.
- Calendar file:
  - If the .ics file or Google Calendar link is built entirely in the browser from data already on the page, the date never reaches the server and the company sends nothing later. That plausibly involves no further processing of the date by the company and no "electronic mail" by it, so no consent question arises for the reminder itself.
  - If the file is generated on the server from URL parameters (name, date), request logs become processing and need a lawful basis.
  - No regulator guidance on this point was found.

### Gaps
- I could not read primary texts on EUR-Lex, the full EDPB 05/2020 PDF, ICO pages or AEPD guidance because the network blocked them. Paragraph numbers and exact wording are unverified.
- I did not research Latvia's ePrivacy implementation (Law on Information Society Services, art. 9) or the Latvian DPA's (DVI) practice. These matter because tryethos.io is Latvia-based.

## Implications for Ethos

*This section is interpretation built on the facts above and the brief's context about Ethos. It is not part of the cited findings.*

1. **Lead with the option that stores nothing.** At the gift's end, or on the "Keep this gift" page, offer a single "Add [Sender]'s birthday to my calendar" button.
   - Build it in the browser: an .ics file on Apple devices, a Google Calendar template link elsewhere.
   - Set a 7-day alert and include a link back to Ethos ("send one back").
   - Nothing is stored and nothing is sent, so no consent question arises, and it is genuinely one tap.
   - The cost is that Ethos cannot follow up or measure beyond the tap. Test on iOS Chrome and the Google Calendar app, the known weak spots.
2. **Make the email reminder a separate, explicit opt-in on the page that already asks for email.** "Keep this gift" already collects an email to deliver the gift link, which is a service. Add one optional, unticked control: "Also email me 7 days before [Sender]'s birthday on [day month]".
   - If the sender has already set the date, show it; otherwise ask for day and month only, with no year and no calendar picker.
   - Keep it unbundled from keeping the gift.
   - Record the consent: timestamp, exact wording, page version.
   - Treat the reminder as marketing: unsubscribe link, Ethos identified, no reminders without the box ticked. Spain's no-tacit-consent rule and the UK's new maximum fine (£17.5m or 4% of turnover) make the safe default worthwhile.
3. **Never gate the gift.** The peak-end rule implies the ending of the gift is the part people remember. Any ask should come after the recipient has reacted or chosen an action, be dismissible, and never sit before replay or react. The "one step at most" constraint fits the data: up to about three fields cost little, four cost a lot.
4. **Copy Moonpig's timing, not its mechanics.** A reminder a week before matches Moonpig's 7-day window (nearly 40% of orders within seven days of a reminder) and suits an instant digital product. An optional nudge 3 days before matches Moonpig US. Moonpig's vouchers and account dependence don't fit "no friction"; the free first reply is already Ethos's incentive.
5. **Minimise the sender's data.**
   - Store the occasion as day and month, the sender's first name and a gift reference; delete after the reminder is sent or consent is withdrawn.
   - Cover the sender through Ethos's privacy notice and receipt: recipients may save the occasion date for a reminder.
   - Write a short legitimate-interests assessment.
   - Have counsel check the Latvian implementing law, since Ethos is established in Latvia.
6. **Defer Wallet passes, web push, SMS and passkeys.** Wallet needs Apple certificates, server signing and a separate Google backend, and it only shows passively on the Lock Screen; it may suit a later "gift keepsake" pass. iOS web push needs a Home Screen install. SMS needs the same consent at higher cost, and a Partiful-style texted code adds a step. Passkeys and Sign in with Apple add an account the flow doesn't need.
7. **Design the "I got one" card for screenshots first and the share button second.**
   - Render a 1080×1920 image with the tryethos.io address drawn into it.
   - Share it with `navigator.share({ files: [png] })` and no text payload on iOS, with a "Save image" fallback.
   - Don't promise a direct "Share to Instagram Stories" from the web; the user picks Instagram in the share sheet.
   - Default the card to exclude the sender's private message and name unless the recipient chooses to show them.
   - Spotify's count includes screenshots, so screenshots are a primary path.
8. **What to measure.**
   - Tap rate for calendar adds compared with email-reminder opt-ins, against generic popup benchmarks (about 2.1% average, 5% or more excellent).
   - Reminder-to-gift conversion within 7 days, against Moonpig's roughly 40% share of orders.
   - Share-sheet opens compared with image saves.
   - Run real-device tests of Instagram, WhatsApp and TikTok share targets before launch; no current public test matrix exists.
