# Frictionless brand marks inside shared and gifted digital artifacts

Scope note: research done 1 November 2026. Material from before 2023 is marked **[historical]**. A methods caveat applies throughout: WebFetch could not reach several primary hosts from this session (developer.chrome.com, developer.mozilla.org, webkit.org, growthunhinged.com, alignify.co, onlinelibrary.wiley.com, assets.super.so). ## 1. Default signatures users keep ("Sent from my iPhone", "Sent via Superhuman", Hotmail)

### Takeaway
No public data says what share of users keep default signatures. The evidence is about effects: a 2012 experiment found "Sent from my iPhone" softens readers' judgement of typos (an excuse function), and Superhuman's CEO said in 2024 that its default "Sent via Superhuman" line still drove more than 30% of site traffic. Default-on, removable-in-settings is the common pattern. Nobody has published why users keep the line (status, inertia or excuse). Only commentary exists.

### Cited Findings
- **[historical] Carr & Stefaniak (2012), *Journal of Applied Communication Research*, "Sent from My iPhone: The Medium and Message as Cues of Sender Professionalism in Mobile Telephony".** 111 undergraduates read an email said to come from the HR director of a large accounting firm. The design was 2×2: with or without grammar errors, and with or without a "Sent from my iPhone" line. The mobile signature reduced the negative judgements caused by the errors. The abstract says "negative attributions are attenuated by cues reflecting the transmission medium (i.e., a message's mobile signature block)". Co-author Stefaniak called the mechanism "technological empathy". The authors also warned that some professionals might fake a mobile signature on desktop to get the same leniency. — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257828481_Sent_from_My_iPhone_The_Medium_and_Message_as_Cues_of_Sender_Professionalism_in_Mobile_Telephony); [Redmond Pie coverage](https://www.redmondpie.com/the-sent-from-my-iphone-signature-has-evolved-past-crass-bragging-according-to-research/); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/sent-from-my-iphone-is-fine,news-23291.html). The paper is paywalled, so these details come from the abstract and press coverage.
- **[historical] People add the line on purpose as an excuse.** A 2013 HuffPost essay is titled "Why I Include 'Sent From My iPhone' — Even When It's Not". Clive Thompson wrote in 2013 about "why people forgive your bad spelling in email 'sent from my iPhone'". — [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/sent-from-my-iphone-signature_b_3516549); [collision detection](http://www.collisiondetection.net/mt/archives/2013/06/_-_kind_of_like.php)
- **It is a default.** Apple Mail on iPhone adds "Sent from my iPhone" until the user changes it in Settings. — [Bybrand guide](https://www.bybrand.io/blog/iphone-remove-default-signature/)
- **No survey of how many keep it.** A targeted search found no poll or study measuring the share of users who keep the iPhone default. A lifetips.alibaba.com article cites precise figures (e.g. "83% of Fortune 500 companies updated mobile signature clauses 2022–2024") with no traceable source, and it should not be used. — [lifetips.alibaba.com (unreliable)](https://lifetips.alibaba.com/tech-efficiency/are-sent-from-my-phone-signatures-useful-or-annoying)
- **Superhuman, 2024 podcast, Rahul Vohra:** "More than 30% of our site traffic still comes from the sent via superhuman viral signature and more than 30% of our new users still comes from our invitations and referrals." — [Deciphr podcast summary](https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/how-i-built-825m-email-revolution-called-superhuman--rahul-vohra). Starter Story gives a higher estimate (50–60% of web traffic), but that page has visible errors. — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/superhuman-breakdown)
- **[historical] Superhuman's default-on line, 2019:** TechCrunch noted that users were expected to keep the line in their signature and never disable it, which implies it was on by default and could be switched off. — [TechCrunch, 27 Jun 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/27/my-six-months-with-30-month-email-service-superhuman/)
- **Superhuman later paid users for keeping it:** Vohra's LinkedIn post says users "earn referral credits when folks sign up through this signature, too!" (post from about mid-2023, judging by the activity ID). — [Rahul Vohra on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rahulvohra_new-2-new-ways-to-get-superhuman-for-free-activity-7070098451165839360-KBBs)
- **[historical] Hotmail (1996–97), the original footer loop:** A TechCrunch book excerpt says investor Tim Draper suggested adding "PS: I love you" to the bottom of every message. The founders agreed to a footer but dropped the "PS: I love you" part, and the line became "Get your free email at Hotmail". — [TechCrunch, 18 Oct 2009](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail/). Growth figures conflict. Marketing blogs repeat "12 million users in 18 months", while other accounts put registered users at about 8.5 million at acquisition. Microsoft bought Hotmail for about $400M in December 1997. — [The Marketing Millennials](https://themarketingmillennials.com/articles/2023-01-09/the-unknown-story-of-how-hotmail-grew-to-12-million-users-in-1-5-years/); [TechSpot history](https://www.techspot.com/article/2376-hotmail/)

### Inferences
- Default-on, quiet and removable is the shared design of iPhone, Hotmail and Superhuman. The user never has to take a step to carry the brand, and the brand line also gives the sender something: leniency for typos (iPhone), a status signal (Superhuman's $30/month exclusivity) or referral credits (Superhuman after 2023).
- The Carr & Stefaniak result suggests a brand line works best when it also tells the reader something useful about the message, such as how or where it was made. A line that is only an ad gives the sender no reason to keep it.

### Gaps
- No data was found on the share of iPhone, Outlook ("Get Outlook for iOS") or Superhuman users who keep the default line, or on their reasons. Status, inertia and excuse are all argued in commentary but not measured.
- Nothing was found on "Get Outlook for iOS" specifically.

## 2. "Made with" / "Edit with" badges on user-made pages

### Takeaway
Across the category the same pattern holds: the badge is on by default for free users, and removal is part of the paid plan (Lovable, v0, Framer, Webflow, Carrd, Typeform). Shopify is the exception and lets any store remove "Powered by Shopify" in the theme editor. No company publishes what share of paying users remove the badge, and I found no published A/B test of the badge's effect on paying users' satisfaction or conversion. Growth attribution is mostly secondary. The best figures are Typeform's "80% of new customers via word of mouth or product virality" and Superhuman's 30%+ above.

### Cited Findings
**Lovable (2025–2026)**
- Lovable's own January 2025 pricing post put "the option to remove 'Edit with Lovable' badge" on the Starter plan ($20/month). — [Lovable blog, 13 Jan 2025](https://lovable.dev/blog/2025-01-13-targeted-edits-supabase-integration-improvements-and-pricing-updates)
- Later third-party guides (2025–2026) describe a "Hide Lovable badge" toggle under Project settings → Publishing that needs Pro or Business. The tier names have probably changed since January 2025. — [RapidDevelopers](https://www.rapidevelopers.com/lovable-issues/removing-the-lovable-badge); [Opsily (2026)](https://opsily.com/hosting/ship/remove-lovable-badge)
- One guide says visitors can dismiss the badge with a close button. It also says the hide setting can switch itself back off on a later publish, so the badge returns, and that hiding it with CSS on the free plan breaks Lovable's terms. I could not check this against Lovable's terms. — [parkstatic](https://parkstatic.site/blog/remove-lovable-badge)
- Several guides show how to hide it for free with CSS (`#lovable-badge`) or by exporting the code to host elsewhere. — [Medium, Eli Front](https://medium.com/@elifront/how-to-hide-the-edit-with-lovable-badge-for-free-a685266c08d8); [Capacity](https://capacity.so/blog/how-to-hide-the-lovable-badge-a-complete-guide-for-app-builders)
- Growth attribution: CEO Anton Osika posted on 11 March 2025 that growth "increased by almost 50% last week, now adding 1,500 customers per day". He credited people sharing what they build ("when they share it it goes viral") and did not name the badge. — [Anton Osika on X](https://x.com/antonosika/status/1899572640966447549). Only a third-party growth analysis lists "Badge on every app" as part of Lovable's growth playbook (unverified). — [treg.to REA for Growth](https://treg.to/rea-growth)

**v0 (Vercel)**
- Community threads from 2025–2026 say free users cannot turn off the "Built with v0" badge and paid users can, in Project settings. — [Vercel Community: request to remove](https://community.vercel.com/t/request-to-remove-built-with-v0-watermark/15306/5); [How to remove](https://community.vercel.com/t/how-to-remove-built-with-v0-button/22458)
- Paying users complain when the badge stays on: "Built with v0 badge showing in Premium" ("even though the 'Show v0 Branding' option is deactivated the 'Built with v0' badge won't disappear"). — [Vercel Community thread](https://community.vercel.com/t/built-with-v0-badge-showing-in-premium/33610); [badge between plan tiers](https://community.vercel.com/t/built-with-v0-badge-between-v0-and-vercel-plan-tiers/44767)
- An undated v0 changelog entry reads "Removed the Built with v0 badge from published apps and generated repositories". Another says the badge "respects branding settings on deployed apps". — [v0 changelog](https://v0.app/changelog)

**Framer, Webflow, Carrd**
- Framer: the "Made in Framer" badge disappears once you upgrade to a paid plan or connect a custom domain, and you must republish after doing so. — [Framer Help](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/how-do-i-remove-the-made-in-framer-badge-from-my-website/). A secondary blog says Framer reported 10.5 million new sites created in its 2025 year in review and calls each free site "a live ad". That is opinion, not measured attribution. — [vibhe](https://vibhe.com/blog/how-framers-free-plan-turns-every-site-into-a-growth-loop)
- Webflow: the "Made in Webflow" badge shows by default. It can be hidden only on a Growth, Agency or Enterprise Workspace plan or a paid site plan. — [Webflow Help: branding](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961378233235-Webflow-branding); a user asking to remove it on the Core plan: [Webflow forum](https://discourse.webflow.com/t/remove-made-in-webflow-from-core-plan/217196)
- Carrd: Pro Lite, Pro Standard and Pro Plus all publish without "Made with Carrd" branding. — [Carrd docs: Pro plans](https://carrd.com/docs/pro/plans)

**Typeform, Calendly, Tally (form and scheduling badges)**
- Typeform: Kyle Poyar (Growth Unhinged / OpenView) reports that 80% of new customers came through word of mouth or product virality. He notes that much of the viral loop "wasn't immediately trackable" and showed up as direct or organic-search traffic. The badge is removable on paid plans. — [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/typeforms-viral-growth-and-its-disruption); [OpenView](https://openviewpartners.com/blog/typeforms-viral-growth-and-its-disruption/) (from search summaries; I could not open the full text)
- Calendly: one teardown claims 25% of new users sign up after seeing the badge and cites no source. Another page notes Calendly has not published its viral coefficient. — [nativeviralloop (unverified)](https://nativeviralloop.com/knowledge/calendly-viral-loop.html)

**Shopify, CapCut**
- Shopify: "Powered by Shopify" can be removed on any plan through Online Store → Themes → Edit default theme content (search "powered", clear the field) or by editing footer.liquid. Theme vendors describe this as allowed, and it depends on theme access, not subscription tier. — [Pixel Union support](https://support.pixelunion.net/hc/en-us/articles/25206667974803-How-do-I-remove-Powered-by-Shopify-from-the-footer); [Archetype Themes help](https://help.archetypethemes.co/hc/en-us/articles/45629169348755-How-do-I-remove-powered-by-shopify-from-the-footer)
- CapCut: the end-card watermark is a separate "ending" clip added by default. Guides point to an "Add default ending" toggle in Settings, or deleting the clip on the timeline. Sources disagree on whether a fully clean export needs CapCut Pro. — [Maestra](https://maestra.ai/blogs/how-to-remove-capcut-watermark-for-free); [Wink](https://wink.ai/blog/how-to-remove-capcut-watermark)

### Inferences
- Charging to remove the badge is the category default, so paying users expect a clean result. The v0 "badge showing in Premium" complaints and Lovable's republish bug show that a brand mark staying on after payment causes support friction and loses trust.
- Shopify's free removal shows a second model: when every user already pays, the badge is goodwill, not something to sell.
- CapCut's end card is the closest analogue to Ethos's end screen: the brand sits after the content as a separate, removable segment, not on top of it.
- Badge growth is under-measured even inside these companies (Typeform's loop showed up as "direct" traffic), so claims that badges "drive X% of signups" should be read as rough.

### Gaps
- No company publishes the share of paying users who keep or remove a badge. Lovable, Framer, Webflow, Carrd and Typeform were all searched.
- No published A/B test of a badge's effect on paying users' satisfaction, churn or conversion was found.
- A figure of about 15% of signups for "Powered by Tally" circulates in secondary SaaS blogs but could not be traced to Tally.
- Not covered for lack of tool budget: Notion Sites, Substack and Canva badge policies.

## 3. Branding and packaging as a quality signal in gifts

### Takeaway
Wrapping lifts recipients' attitudes through mood (Howard 1992). But neat or elaborate presentation raises expectations and can backfire with close friends (Rixom et al. 2020). Overpackaging reads as thoughtless to recipients even though givers think it signals care (Shi, Chen & Yin 2024). Handmade gifts are valued because they seem to "contain love", especially for loved ones (Fuchs et al. 2015). No study tests whether a visible maker's brand raises or lowers the value of a personal, handmade-feeling gift. Together these studies point toward restraint for close relationships.

### Cited Findings
- **[historical] Howard (1992), "Gift-Wrapping Effects on Product Attitudes: A Mood-Biasing Explanation", *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 1(3):197–223.** All four experiments found wrapping improved attitudes toward owning the item. The proposed mechanism is that wrapping, through repeated pairing with joyful events, cues a happy mood that biases attitudes. Plain wrapping without bows also worked. The effect appeared only when participants believed the gift was for them. — [ScienceBlogs summary](https://scienceblogs.com/grrlscientist/2009/12/15/the-psychology-behind-wrapping-2); [Indiana Public Media](https://indianapublicmedia.org/amomentofscience/the-psychology-of-gift-wrap.php) (secondary summaries; I could not open the paper)
- **Rixom, Mas & Rixom (2020), "Presentation Matters: The Effect of Wrapping Neatness on Gift Attitudes", *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 30(2):329–338.** Neat wrapping raises expectations, so the gift is more likely to disappoint. Sloppy wrapping lowers expectations, so the gift is more likely to beat them. That holds for friends (expectation disconfirmation). For acquaintances, neatness is read as a signal about the relationship, so neatly wrapped gifts are liked more. — [Wiley abstract](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jcpy.1140); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3462836); [Vanderbilt News, 5 Dec 2019](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2019/12/05/no-it-doesnt-matter-how-nicely-you-wrap-that-gift-except-when-it-does/)
- **Shi, Chen & Yin (2024), "Thoughtful or Thoughtless? Asymmetric Attitudes of Gift-givers and Gift-recipients toward Overpackaged Gifts", *Journal of Retailing* 100(4):656–672.** Five studies plus four supplementary studies. Givers prefer and rate overpackaged gifts more favourably and see the packaging as a sign of thoughtfulness. Recipients read overpackaging as the giver lacking thoughtfulness, and lower perceived thoughtfulness lowers their evaluation of the gift. The gap is larger on obligatory occasions and between more distant givers and recipients. The authors ruled out face concerns and gift expectations as explanations. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002243592400068X); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jouret:v:100:y:2024:i:4:p:656-672); [Cal Poly press](https://orfalea.calpoly.edu/?p=7807)
- **Fuchs, Schreier & van Osselaer (2015), "The Handmade Effect: What's Love Got to Do with It?", *Journal of Marketing* 79(2):98–110 [historical].** Across four studies, handmade products were more attractive, largely because they are seen as symbolically "containing love". Purchase intent for handmade goods was higher when buying gifts for loved ones, but not for more distant recipients. The effect held after controlling for effort, quality, uniqueness and authenticity. The authors say the handmade effect "is not omnipresent". — [WU Vienna PDF](https://www.wu.ac.at/fileadmin/wu/d/i/mm/paper/2015_CF_MS_SO_The_Handmade_Effect_Whats_Love_Got_to_Do_with_It.pdf); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2015/03/150324111544.htm)
- **Givers and recipients want different things.** Teigen et al. (2005) found givers favour exclusive luxury items while recipients prefer practical ones. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7840993_Giver-receiver_asymmetries_in_gift_preferences). Baskin et al. (2014, *JCR*) found givers favour more desirable gifts and recipients more feasible ones. — [JCR PDF](https://msbfile03.usc.edu/digitalmeasures/wakslak/intellcont/baskin%20wakslak%20trope%20novemsky%20(2014)-1.pdf). A meta-analysis pools 153 effects from 114 studies on giver–recipient asymmetries. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378335507_Exploring_gift_gaps_A_meta-analysis_of_giver-recipient_asymmetries)
- **Vendor statistics to avoid.** Figures that circulate on packaging-vendor blogs could not be traced to primary studies: "20–30% more valuable" (attributed to Deloitte), "76% more attractive" (Pregis / University of Wisconsin 2019), and Sleeknova's means for the Rixom paper. — [The Reward Store](https://www.therewardstore.com/blogs/how-packaging-and-presentation-affects-gift-perceived-value); [Sleeknova](https://sleeknova.com/guides/gift-wrapping-psychology-research/)

### Inferences
- Ethos's envelope, wax seal and tap-to-open work as wrapping. Howard suggests the wrapping ritual itself lifts mood. Rixom and Shi suggest that between close partners, extra polish or extra packaging can raise expectations or look like the giver cared more about presentation than content.
- A corporate brand mark on the gift itself could undercut the "contains love" effect Fuchs et al. describe, because it reminds the recipient that a company and a template were involved. This is an inference. No study tests it directly.
- The Tiffany-style case, where the brand box is the value signal, fits luxury goods bought for status. It fits less well a €3–11 personal page whose value is the sender's effort. No research was found to settle this.

### Gaps
- No academic study was found that tests a visible maker's brand on a personal or handmade-feeling gift, or the "Tiffany box effect" in a controlled design. The Tiffany box claim is folklore in these results.
- No research on branding inside digital gifts (e-cards, gift pages) was found.

## 4. Sonic branding inside a product without annoying users

### Takeaway
Successful product sound logos are short. Netflix's ta-dum runs about 3 seconds, and practitioners recommend 1–3 seconds. The strongest audio-brand data (Ipsos 2020) shows sonic cues are underused and linked to high-performing ads. Evidence on annoyance is thin. Old earcon guidelines blame loudness more than length, and a 2025 study found shorter cues distract less while longer ones are easier to identify. Browsers block sound until a user gesture, and a "tap to open" press counts as that gesture if audio starts inside the tap handler.

### Cited Findings
- **Netflix "ta-dum" [historical origin, ~2015].** About 3 seconds long. Sound designer Lon Bender built it after a call from Todd Yellin, who wanted tension and release, "not too electronic", with quirky qualities. It layers Bender's wedding ring knocking on a cabinet, a slowed anvil and a reversed-guitar "blossom". Hans Zimmer later made a 16–17-second theatrical version. — [Marketplace, 3 Jan 2022](https://marketplace.org/2022/01/03/the-strategy-behind-sonic-logos-like-netflixs-startup-chime); [Twenty Thousand Hertz](https://www.20k.org/episodes/netflix); [CBR on the Zimmer version](https://www.cbr.com/netflix-replaces-ta-dum-sound-theatrical-releases-hans-zimmer-intro/)
- **Mac startup chime [historical].** Removed with the 2016 Touch Bar MacBook Pros. Restored in macOS Big Sur (June 2020), on by default, with a "Play sound on startup" toggle in System Preferences → Sound. — [MacRumors, 23 Jun 2020](https://www.macrumors.com/2020/06/23/macos-big-sur-brings-back-startup-chime/); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/234380/macos-11-big-sur-startup-chime.html)
- **[historical] Ipsos, 2020.** Across 2,015 video ads, audio assets were about 3.44× more likely to appear in high-performing ads than visual assets, and sonic brand cues about 8.53×. Sonic devices appeared in only 6% of ads. — [Sixième Son on the Ipsos study](https://www.sixiemeson.com/news/ipsos-brand-audio-cues/); [the brandgym](https://thebrandgym.com/distinctive-brand-assets-marketers-are-missing-a-trick/)
- **Silas et al. (2026, Cambridge repository preprint).** 1,860 participants and 90 commercial sonic logos of 2–9 seconds. More complex structure improved recognition but hurt free recall. The authors suggest effective logos are "easy to hum, of moderate length, and feature simple structure". Peer-review status is unverified. — [Cambridge repository](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/eb68da1f-6ea7-4829-be41-d7da46f26aff/content)
- **Pramana et al. (2024), *Journal of Consumer Sciences* (IPB).** 300 participants heard a sonic logo 1, 3 or 7 times. Per a secondary summary, most of the gain came between the first and third exposure. — [IPB journal PDF](https://journal.ipb.ac.id/jcs/article/download/57484/29475); [tadadam summary](https://tadadam.com/en/blog/how-many-repetitions-sonic-logo-needs)
- **Length guidance is mostly from practitioners.** Vendors and agencies suggest 1–3 seconds, with about 6 seconds as a ceiling. A "2–3 seconds for optimal memorability" figure is attributed to SoundOut but could not be verified. — [oakgen](https://oakgen.ai/blog/sonic-logo-guide); [Sonic Minds](https://sonicmindsagency.com/what-defines-a-great-sonic-logo/)
- **Earcons and annoyance [historical guidelines].** Brewster-style guidelines say not to use notes shorter than 0.0825 s and name intensity, not length, as "the main cause of annoyance due to sound", so users should control volume. — [ResearchGate: Experimentally derived guidelines for earcons](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228607856_Experimentally_derived_guidelines_for_the_creation_of_earcons)
- **2025 cue-length test.** A wearable feedback study tested 0.5 s, 1 s and 2 s cues. Shorter cues were less distracting and longer ones easier to identify, and the authors chose 1 s. — [arXiv 2507.04238](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04238)
- **A 2024 narrative review exists** (Spence, "Sonic branding: A narrative review at the intersection of art and science", *Psychology & Marketing*), but I could not open it. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mar.21995)
- **Browser autoplay rules.** In Chrome, an AudioContext created before any user gesture starts "suspended" and has to be resumed after an interaction. Chrome's own example binds the resume to interaction events including `touchend`. — [Chrome for Developers: Autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay/); [Chrome: Web Audio, Autoplay Policy and Games](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/web-audio-autoplay). p5.js unlocks audio on mouseUp/touchEnd by default. — [p5.js userStartAudio](https://p5js.org/reference/p5/userStartAudio). iOS Safari is stricter: sound may need to be played, not just resumed, inside the gesture callback. — [bugnet.io](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-construct3-audio-not-playing-on-first-touch); [Chromium issue 41387757](https://issues.chromium.org/issues/41387757). These come from search summaries. I could not open the Chrome, MDN or WebKit pages to quote their current text.

### Inferences
- A 2-second sound sits inside the practitioner range and is shorter than Netflix's 3 seconds. The 2025 cue study suggests 1–2 seconds balances distraction against recognisability.
- A recipient usually opens a given gift once or twice, so a sound heard per gift wears out slowly. The sender, who previews many times in the editor, carries the real repetition risk.
- The Mac chime came back on by default but with a toggle. Respected product sounds tend to come with an off switch, even if few people use it (no usage data found).
- A tap-to-open screen already gives the browser the gesture it needs. Starting playback synchronously in that handler (especially on iOS) adds no new step.

### Gaps
- No data was found on Duolingo's, Apple Pay's or iMessage's sound design or on user annoyance with them.
- No study compares a spoken brand name ("Ethos") with a non-verbal tone in an intimate or emotional context.
- No research was found on an ideal length below 2 seconds specifically, and nothing on repetition fatigue for in-product (non-ad) sounds.

## 5. Branded opening and reveal moments people enjoy rather than skip

### Takeaway
Hard data on skipping comes from Netflix. Skip Intro is pressed about 136 million times a day (2022), and Netflix built it after seeing 15% of members manually skipping in the first five minutes. Long repeated intros get skipped. The ~3-second brand sting before them is not skippable and has become loved. Paperless Post's envelope animation is widely credited with making digital invites feel premium, but no skip or enjoyment data was found. Unboxing research links delight to surprise and anticipation, mostly in lower-tier venues.

### Cited Findings
- **Netflix Skip Intro.** Pressed about 136 million times a day, saving members 195 years of cumulative time daily (Netflix, March 2022). It was tested in 2017 on 250 series on web in the US, UK and Canada. Netflix research found 15% of members manually advanced within the first five minutes. An engineer said he was not sure a "free cupcake" button would get more clicks. — [Netflix: Looking back on the origin of Skip Intro](https://about.netflix.com/news/looking-back-on-the-origin-of-skip-intro-five-years-later); [TVBEurope](https://www.tvbeurope.com/media-consumption/netflixs-skip-intro-button-is-pressed-136-million-times-a-day)
- **Paperless Post.** Recipients see an envelope-opening animation. Alternative-review sites credit it with making digital invites feel like real stationery. Premium cards and add-ons such as liners cost Coins (e.g. 2 Coins per recipient for a premium card). — [Mixily](https://blog.mixily.com/evite-vs-paperless-post/); [InviteDrop](https://www.invitedrop.com/blog/paperless-post-alternatives) (competitor blogs)
- **Apple Invites.** Launched in early 2025. Creating an invite needs iCloud+, but anyone can RSVP without an Apple account. — [Wikipedia: Apple Invites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_Invites)
- **Partiful.** A competitor blog claims 2 million new users since January 2025. Not verified from a primary source. — [InviteDrop](https://www.invitedrop.com/blog/partiful-alternatives)
- **Unboxing research.** A University of Twente master's thesis finds that unboxing can trigger surprise, which can lift outcomes above expectation. — [Twente thesis](https://essay.utwente.nl/fileshare/file/81023/Berden_MA_BMS.pdf). Customer-delight work finds surprise raises delight. — [ScienceDirect, J. Business Research 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S014829632200371X). A 2025 *Journal of Informatics Education and Research* study (surveys, observation and EEG, smartphones in India) reports better unboxing correlates with delight and loyalty. The venue is low-tier. — [JIER](https://jier.org/index.php/journal/article/view/3324). Moderate uncertainty raises anticipation in blind-box buying. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/407305170_Psychology_of_the_Unboxing_Experience_How_Uncertainty_and_Surprise_Mechanisms_Drive_Happiness_in_Blind_Box_Consumption)

### Inferences
- What people skip is long, repeated material they have already seen (title sequences on episode 5). A short sting tied to the start of something they want (Netflix's ta-dum) is tolerated and liked. The difference looks like length and repetition, not branding itself.
- A reveal that is also the act of opening (envelope, seal) adds no time because it is part of the experience. A branded moment placed before the content adds time.

### Gaps
- No skip-rate data was found for Pixar, other studio logos, Paperless Post's envelope or Apple Invites' opening.
- No study gives a maximum length for a branded opening that people enjoy rather than skip.

## 6. Share cards and screenshots that carry a brand without platform penalties

### Takeaway
Platform penalties target video carrying another platform's watermark when it is recommended to new viewers. Instagram has done this since February 2021, and TikTok's guidelines make such reposts ineligible for For You. Shares to friends and Stories are not penalised. Spotify Wrapped shows that branded static cards built for Stories reach huge scale: 500M+ shares on day one in 2025, a count that includes screenshots.

### Cited Findings
- **Instagram, 10 February 2021:** Reels with visible watermarks and logos (e.g. TikTok's) would be recommended less often. — [GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/newscomm-47671.php). Later blogs say Instagram's originality guidance (dated about April 2024) lists "has no visible watermarks" as a condition for recommendation, and that in April 2026 the originality rules were extended to photos and carousels. Neither claim could be checked against Instagram's own pages. — [Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/instagram-wont-recommend-watermarked-reels); [Fastlane](https://www.usefastlane.ai/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026)
- **TikTok:** the Community Guidelines make reused content ineligible for the For You feed and name clips with another platform's watermark or logo as an example. Such posts stay on the profile and in search. — [Slidy Creator](https://slidycreator.com/blog/tiktok-not-eligible-for-recommendation/); [ContentIQ](https://contentiq.media/rules/tiktok) (secondary)
- **YouTube Shorts [historical, 2021]:** the Shorts Fund excluded videos with other platforms' watermarks. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/future-of-tv/youtubes-creator-fund-for-youtube-shorts-will-not-exclude-videos-posted-to-other-platforms/). YouTube adds its own watermark to Shorts downloaded from Studio. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/media/downloaded-youtube-shorts-will-be-watermarked/). No official YouTube statement on demoting watermarked Shorts in recommendations was found.
- **Spotify Wrapped 2025 (company-reported):** 200M users engaged in the first 24 hours (2024 took about 62 hours to reach 200M) and more than 500M shares on day one, up 41% year on year. Spotify counts native shares, downloads and screenshots as shares. By the end of the campaign there were more than 300M engaged users and more than 630M shares. — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-wrapped-campaign-hit-200m-engaged-users-in-24-hours-a-19-yoy-increase); [Music Week](https://www.musicweek.com/digital/read/spotify-wrapped-2025-was-biggest-ever-with-200-million-engaged-users/093178)
- **Wrapped card design:** each data story includes a share card, which can go to Spotify Messages or social apps. — [Spotify Newsroom, 3 Dec 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-12-03/2025-wrapped-user-experience/). Commentators note the cards are built at Instagram Stories dimensions. — [Medium, Aadil Syed](https://aadilsyed.medium.com/the-wrapped-phenomenon-how-we-learned-to-love-sharing-our-data-6392034f995f)
- **Strava and Letterboxd:** Bloomberg reported Strava monthly active users up 20% year on year in Q1 2024, as users sought refuge from mainstream social media. This says nothing about share-card design. — [Bloomberg, 31 Aug 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-31/strava-and-letterboxd-surge-as-users-crave-social-media-refuge)

### Inferences
- The penalties are about reach to strangers (recommendation), not about friends seeing a share. A gift share card posted to Stories or sent in a chat is not ranked by these systems in the same way.
- Wrapped cards carry the Spotify mark, yet users still share them by the hundreds of millions. That suggests a brand on a card is fine when the card is about the user, not the brand. The content is the hero and the logo is small.
- Because Spotify counts screenshots as shares, screenshot-friendly design (9:16, legible at a glance, mark built into the layout) is a deliberate growth tactic.
- The risk for Ethos is a recipient turning the animated gift into a screen recording and posting it as a Reel or TikTok. A visible logo burned into that video could hit the watermark rules.

### Gaps
- No primary design write-ups or share figures were found for Duolingo, Strava, Letterboxd, BeReal or Partiful share cards in this pass.
- No data was found on whether a brand mark on a share card changes share rates (no A/B test found).

## Implications for Ethos

*This section is interpretation, kept separate from the facts above. It uses the Ethos context from the assignment.*

1. **A permanent mark on paid gifts goes against both the category norm and Ethos's own promise.** Every badge product reviewed charges to remove the mark, and Shopify removes it for free. None keeps a brand mark on after payment. Paying v0 users complain publicly when the badge stays. Ethos's terms say paid gifts have no footer and never show the word "Ethos". A seal-and-name mark and a spoken "Ethos" would technically sidestep "footer", but buyers may see them as breaking the spirit of the promise. Before shipping, either (a) update the terms and the purchase-page wording so a buyer knows before paying, or (b) keep brand marks off paid gifts and limit the loop to free gifts plus elements the sender actively chooses.
2. **Put the brand on the wrapping, not the gift.** Howard (1992) supports the envelope and seal ritual as mood-lifting wrapping. Rixom (2020), Shi et al. (2024) and Fuchs et al. (2015) all warn that, between close partners, presentation that feels corporate, overdone or not from the sender can lower perceived thoughtfulness or "love". The lowest-risk places for a mark are the link preview, the unlabelled seal (perhaps a small mark on its rim) and the end screen, where CapCut and Netflix place theirs, after the content. Avoid any mark over the love letter or animation itself. A maker's mark in the craft sense (small, like a potter's stamp on the base) fits the handmade framing better than a logo.
3. **The 2-second spoken "Ethos" sound is technically frictionless but emotionally risky.** Playing it from the existing tap handler satisfies Chrome's and Safari's gesture rules with no new step. On iOS, start playback inside the handler, not after an await. Two seconds is in the recommended range. But the evidence base is ads and TV, not intimate messages, and no study covers a spoken brand name at the start of a love letter. To keep it frictionless: (a) overlap it with the seal-breaking animation rather than adding 2 seconds before the gift, (b) mix it quietly, since loudness drives annoyance, (c) mute it in the sender's editor previews, where repetition fatigue would build, and (d) consider a non-verbal seal-crack signature tone, which carries brand recognition without saying a company name over a personal moment.
4. **The named reply button is the closest analogue to the signature evidence.** "Send Leo an Ethos gift back" works like "Sent via Superhuman" and the Hotmail footer: it costs neither party a step, and it does something useful (reply in kind). The brand name inside the button is the least intrusive way to name Ethos because it sits next to an action the recipient may want. Test the named and unnamed wording, and track replies started per gift opened.
5. **Design the share card like Wrapped, not like a watermark.** Make it a 9:16 static image where the recipient's name, the sender's words or a reaction is the hero, with a small seal mark built into the composition. Expect most sharing to be screenshots or chat forwards, which no platform penalises. Do not burn a logo into any downloadable video of the gift, because Instagram and TikTok demote videos carrying visible logos and watermarks.
6. **Measure it, because the industry mostly doesn't.** Typeform found its loop showed up as untraceable "direct" traffic. Add referral parameters to the end-screen buttons, the share card URL and the free-gift footer pill. Run the sound and mark as A/B tests on paid-gift outcomes (completion, replay, reactions, "send one back" rate, refunds and complaints), so the no-friction promise is checked against data rather than assumed.
