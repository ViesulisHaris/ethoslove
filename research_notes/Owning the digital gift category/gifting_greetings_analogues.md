# How digital greeting-card, invitation and gift brands built, held or lost their category position

Research date: 1 November 2026. Anything dated before 2025 is historical unless marked "current".

**Method caveat (read first):** WebFetch was blocked in this session for every host tried (cnbc.com, moonpig.group, time.com: DNS not allowed). So every finding below comes from search-engine summaries of the linked pages, not from reading the pages in full. Numbers that matter should be checked against the linked primary page before anyone quotes them. Where a number came only from an aggregator or a competitor's blog, it is labelled as such.

## Q1. Brand case snapshots: how each became the category name, key numbers, and status on 1 Nov 2026

### Takeaway
The durable winners in this list are Hallmark, Moonpig, Evite and Paperless Post (all still operating) and Partiful (still growing). Each owns one clear job: sentimental cards, UK online cards, casual invites, design-led invites, Gen Z party invites. Each is also seen by recipients every time it is used. The two collapses had no recurring revenue engine. Blue Mountain lost about 95% of its sale price in roughly two years. Cameo kept about 10% of its peak valuation, as a pandemic novelty without repeat occasions.

### Inferences
- Every surviving brand ties its name to a single occasion-job: Hallmark to caring, Moonpig to UK online cards, Evite to casual invites, Paperless Post to stylish invites, Partiful to Gen Z parties. Blue Mountain owned a channel (free e-cards) rather than a job, and the channel was commoditised.
- Recent financial health correlates with paying behaviour, not with scale. In the most recent data, Evite with about 10–20% paying users and Moonpig with a 28% EBITDA margin look healthier than Blue Mountain did with 9M monthly visitors and no card revenue.

## Q2. What the recipient saw: branding on the artifact, and how it drove acquisition

### Takeaway
In every case the recipient opened the artifact on the brand's own domain or app, so each send was a product demo. The brands then split on whether branding was the cost of a free send (Evite ads, removed by paying) or a status signal the sender wanted (Hallmark's crown, Paperless Post's designer envelopes). Partiful went further: the recipient is converted into an identified user at RSVP, by phone number. No source in this session gave a quantified viral coefficient or the share of signups that came from recipients.

### Cited Findings
- **Blue Mountain (historical):** Excite@Home's president George Bell praised the site for creating viral growth around occasion-driven commerce — [InternetNews, Oct 1999](https://www.internetnews.com/it-management/excitehome-buys-blue-mountain-arts-for-780-million/). The free cards were "an instant hit" after an earlier paid e-commerce effort flopped — [Westword](https://www.westword.com/news/who-wants-to-be-a-billionaire-5060963/).
- **Jacquie Lawson:** the artist emailed one animated card to a few friends; "hundreds" wrote back. The artifact itself, a distinctive animated watercolour style, was the acquisition channel before any company existed — [Jacquie Lawson: About](https://www.jacquielawson.com/aboutus); [cs4fn](https://cs4fn.blog/2023/02/04/jacquie-lawson-the-multi-million-pound-greeting/).
- **Evite, recipient-side branding as monetization:**
  - Free invitations show banner ads to guests. Premium gives "an ad-free guest experience" plus digital envelopes, liners, backgrounds and stickers — [Evite FAQ (PDF)](https://evite.com/cms_proxy/61c4debfe6329744f06c60e1/648cd3dda08b27df921dc7b4_evite_faq.pdf).
  - Adding an envelope, sticker, background or ad removal upgrades a free design to Premium — [Carly blog (third party)](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/evite-pricing/).
  - Reported Premium prices (from a competitor page): $17.99 for up to 12 guests, up to $99.99 for up to 750 guests; Evite Pro is $249.99 a year — [Greenvelope comparison (competitor)](https://17hats.greenvelope.com/compare/greenvelope-vs-evite); [Carly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/evite-pricing/).
- **Paperless Post:**
  - TechCrunch described its online invitations as "skeuomorphic", attempting "to evoke the same emotions one might get from ripping open a real-life envelope" — [TechCrunch, 2014](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/15/paperless-post-20m-august-capital/).
  - Premium "Cards" are designed in-house and with fashion and design houses: Kate Spade, Oscar de la Renta, Jonathan Adler, The New Yorker. The company is ad-free — [YesPress summary](https://yespress.io/paperless-post); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paperless_Post).
  - Flyer launched in June 2018 as a free, casual product with six "vibes", with premium features possibly to be layered in later — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/06/19/paperless-post-introduces-flyer-for-more-casual-invitations/amp/).
- **Partiful:**
  - Guests RSVP through a link with no app needed, and hosts send "text blasts" — [partiful.com](https://partiful.com/).
  - Auto-reminders go out by SMS, iMessage or WhatsApp; guests with the app get push notifications — [Partiful Help: event reminders](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525436-what-event-reminders-do-you-send).
  - Email-invited guests get reminders by email until they RSVP, then switch to SMS or push — [Partiful Help](https://help.partiful.com/hc/en-us/articles/34834944631707-Do-guests-invited-via-email-receive-event-reminders-and-Text-Blasts).
  - RSVPing typically means entering a name and phone number and then a texted code — [Party.pro review](https://party.pro/partiful/); [JoinMyEvent (competitor)](https://joinmyevent.co/blog/partiful-alternative).
  - Hosts do not get direct access to attendees' contact information. Fortune notes this makes the platform incompatible with business marketing — [Fortune, 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/05/23/partiful-founders-startup-raises-series-a/).
- **Apple Invites:** guests can RSVP without an Apple account — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/04/apple-launches-app-for-party-invitations-in-recurring-revenue-push-.html). Partiful does not require attendees to enter an email address to confirm attendance — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/02/04/partiful-apple-invites-app-copycat/).
- **Hallmark:** the crown and script mark was a registered trademark from 1950. The slogan positions the brand as proof of the sender's care — [Company-Histories](https://www.company-histories.com/Hallmark-Cards-Inc-Company-History1.html).
- **Digital bouquets (2025–26):**
  - DigiBouquet (digibouquet.vercel.app), by indie maker "Pauline" (@pauline_makes), is credited by a competitor with making link-sent digital bouquets "a thing on TikTok" — [Attached blog (competitor)](https://www.attachedapp.com/attached-blog/link-bouquet-alternatives-6-websites-like-link-bouquet-to-send-a-bouquet-by-link).
  - The #digibouquet tag carries tutorials in several languages — [TikTok #digibouquet](https://www.tiktok.com/tag/digibouquet?lang=en).
  - No independent press coverage was found.

### Inferences
- There are two monetisation models for recipient-side branding:
  - Branding as tax: Evite shows ads or branding on free sends and sells their removal.
  - Branding as badge: Hallmark's crown and Paperless Post's designer cards carry branding the sender is proud of.
  - Evite is the only case with evidence that "remove branding" sells; about 10–20% of users pay. But Evite sells removal of third-party ads, not removal of its own name.
- Partiful's strongest acquisition move is collecting each recipient's phone number at RSVP. The recipient becomes a reachable user and receives Partiful-branded reminders and text blasts. Because the host never gets that contact, the network stays with Partiful.
- (General knowledge, not verified in this session.) In the dot-com era, Blue Mountain recipients typically got an email link and had to visit bluemountain.com to "pick up" their card. That made each card a site visit, which explains a portal paying for "eyeballs".

### Gaps
- No quantified viral coefficient, K-factor, or "% of new senders who were first recipients" was found for any brand: not Partiful, Evite, Paperless Post, Blue Mountain or Moonpig.
- No data on whether Paperless Post or Partiful show a "made with" mark on the artifact, or whether one can be removed.

## Q3. Did the brand name become a noun or verb, and was it deliberate?

### Takeaway
"An Evite" and "send a Partiful" became generic shorthand. The evidence suggests both happened organically through the product's own URL and send flow, not through copy telling people to use the name. Evite's own press kit actively discourages noun use, a trademark-protection move. Cameo's 2025–26 win against OpenAI shows that a common English word can still be defended as a brand when used in a suggestive way.

### Cited Findings
- Evite's press kit tells writers to say "an Evite invitation", not "an Evite" or "evites" — [Evite Press Kit 2019](https://partners.evitecdn.com/Press%20Kit/Press%20Kit/Evite_PressKit_02.2019.pdf).
- Partiful is described as the app that made "send a Partiful" the new verb for party invites — [BigGo Finance summary of the Prof G Pod](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e7c8f1d8d5a05e35). No primary Partiful copy pushing the phrase was found.
- Paperless Post was parodied on Saturday Night Live — [How I Built This](https://wondery.com/shows/how-i-built-this/episode/10386-paperless-post-james-and-alexa-hirschfeld/?queryID=6eecc1c8fd4cb3ddd1bfcecd6266d469).
- Hallmark's slogan dates to 1944 and the crown mark to 1950 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallmark_Cards); [Company-Histories](https://www.company-histories.com/Hallmark-Cards-Inc-Company-History1.html). No source in this session documented "Hallmark" being used as an adjective for sentimental; see Gaps.
- Moonpig's name was chosen as a distinctive, available two-syllable domain (the founder's school nickname) — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/man-mission-the-moonpigcom/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonpig). The Group CMO says the brand has a place in UK consumers' "head and heart" for cards and is now pushing to be known for gifting — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/moonpig-cmo-mission-cards/). An older third-party analysis cites 86% brand awareness (dated) — [Macro Ops](https://macro-ops.com/moonpig-moon-uks-leading-online-card-company/).
- **Cameo vs OpenAI (common-word trademark):**
  - Cameo sued OpenAI over Sora's "Cameo" feature — [California Lawyers Association](https://calawyers.org/intellectual-property-law/cameo-sues-openai-over-cameo-trademark-use-in-sora-app/).
  - A temporary restraining order in November 2025 barred OpenAI from using "cameo" or similar marks for Sora — [CNBC, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/24/openai-temporarily-blocked-from-using-cameo-after-trademark-lawsuit.html).
  - Judge Eumi Lee then granted a preliminary injunction (around February 2026). She found the mark suggestive, not merely descriptive, and the Sora feature was reportedly renamed "Characters" — [Android Headlines, Feb 2026](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/02/openai-lawsuit-bars-cameo-name-trademark-sora.html); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/federal-court-rules-that-openai-must-stop-using-the-term-cameo-124559072.html).
  - OpenAI said no one can claim exclusive ownership of the word "cameo".
  - A claim that the order is "permanent" is unverified — [Tekedia](https://www.tekedia.com/federal-court-sides-with-cameo-in-trademark-dispute-orders-openai-to-permanently-stop-using-cameo-name-for-sora-2-feature/).

### Inferences
- Noun adoption seems to follow from the artifact being a link on the brand's domain (evite.com, partiful.com) that people forward and talk about. Neither brand appears to have prompted it through copy, and Evite actively hedged against it for trademark reasons.
- Having a brand name that is a common English word (Cameo) was not fatal to protection. But it invites collisions with large platforms and forces costly enforcement. Cameo had to litigate against OpenAI while in a weak financial position.

### Gaps
- No primary source showing Partiful deliberately seeding "send a Partiful" in its copy or CTAs.
- No usage-frequency data (Google Trends or corpus) for "an Evite", "a Partiful" or "so Hallmark" was gathered.

## Q4. Bringing people back between occasions: reminders, calendars, subscriptions, and retention data

### Takeaway
Moonpig is the benchmark. It holds 113m occasion reminders, about 40% of orders come within seven days of a reminder, and more than 90% of revenue comes from existing customers. Its 1.2m Plus members place about a quarter of Moonpig orders. Subscription e-card brands (Jacquie Lawson, Blue Mountain) keep users through annual auto-renewal, and pay for it in complaints. Invitation apps retain through the guest graph rather than through calendars.

### Cited Findings
- **Moonpig FY26 (year to 30 April 2026):**
  - The reminders database grew 11.2% to 113m (from 101m).
  - "around 40% of orders placed within seven days of an occasion reminder"
  - Moonpig Plus and Greetz Plus memberships grew 29.3% to 1.2m, with members "now accounting for around a quarter of Moonpig orders"
  - Active customers (anyone who purchased in the last 12 months) rose to 12.3m from 12.0m.
  - Orders per active customer slipped to 2.92 from 2.94; total orders grew 2.1%.
  - "Order growth in FY26 was driven by new customer acquisition."
  - [FY26 results announcement](https://www.moonpig.group/media/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-results-announcement.html); [FY26 presentation script](https://www.moonpig.group/media/xllnveot/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-full-year-results-presentation-script-final.pdf); [Retail Technology Innovation Hub](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2026/6/25/moonpig-invests-in-ai-and-ms-becomes-a-data-company-presenting-the-retail-technology-week-in-numbers)
  - One news piece gives prior-year active customers as 11.7m, which conflicts with the company's 12.0m.
- The H1 FY26 script (December 2025) says Moonpig is "consistently able to generate over 90% of our revenue from existing customers" — [MarketScreener: H1 FY26 presentation script](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/moonpig-presentation-script-moonpig-group-plc-fy26-half-year-results-presentation-script-final-ce7d51d2da81f423); [H1 FY26 announcement](https://www.moonpig.group/media/t2lbafil/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-half-year-results-announcement.pdf).

There are 51m card buyers in the UK and NL, and only 15% of card purchases happen online — [Moonpig Group: Investment case](https://www.moonpig.group/investors/investment-case/) (some figures via third-party summaries).
- Plus had about 920,000 members by April 2025 (third-party) — [KoalaGains](https://koalagains.com/stocks/LSE/MOON/business-and-moat). This is consistent with +29% to 1.2m.
- **Jacquie Lawson:**
  - annual membership with automatic renewal; to stop it, users set Automatic Renewal to "No"
  - BBB grade F, 1.57/5 average rating, with complaints about unexpected renewals, double charges and refund difficulty
  - Trustpilot: most of 157 reviews are negative, though many praise the art
  - [USA Complaints](https://usacomplaints.com/company/jacquie-lawson/); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.jacquielawson.com?page=9); [PissedConsumer](https://jacquie-lawson.pissedconsumer.com/review.html)

- **Evite:** 100M+ annual users (2016, company-reported) — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/evite-announces-comprehensive-site-redesign-103038989.html). Its newer business includes subscriptions; Axios headlined "A brand new Evite invites subscribers" — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/retail-deals/2023/11/30/a-brand-new-evite-invites-subscribers).

Hosts can send text blasts, and auto-reminders run before events — [Partiful Help](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525436-what-event-reminders-do-you-send).

- Moonpig's flywheel works like this. A one-off card purchase captures the recipient's occasion date (the reminder database). The reminder then triggers the next purchase close to the date. The membership adds a subscription layer on top. The weak spot in FY26: frequency per customer is flat to slightly down (2.94 to 2.92), so growth now leans on acquisition.
- Subscription-only e-card brands show that annual auto-renewal retains revenue but builds a visible trust deficit (BBB F, Trustpilot), which is a reputational risk to the brand.

## Q5. When incumbents, platforms or copycats entered, what protected the leader?

### Takeaway
Apple's 2025 copy of Partiful did not visibly dent it, at least according to Partiful's CEO; no independent data exists. Partiful's protection was cross-platform reach, being free to hosts, no-app RSVP for guests, and a cultural identity. Apple's version requires iCloud+ to host and is iOS-centric. The incumbent greeting-card companies did not out-compete Blue Mountain; they waited and bought it for 4.5% of its peak price. Free, indie digital-bouquet sites spawn copycats within months, which suggests templates alone are not a moat.

### Cited Findings
- **Partiful's public response:** on 4 February 2025 Partiful posted a screenshot of App Store guideline 4.1 (copycats) captioned "just reviewing the apple developer guidelines". Marissa Mayer's Sunshine posted similarly ("Gen AI invites and integrated photo sharing, hmm…") — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/02/04/partiful-apple-invites-app-copycat/); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2598411/apples-new-app-invites-copycat-criticism.html).

- Murthy (April 2025): "We haven't really seen any users that have been leaving Partiful for Apple Invites." In Q1 2025 Partiful's estimated MAU was up 400% year on year — [CNBC via NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/?amp=1).

Reviewers praised its design but called setup "a chore" because it relies on many Apple services — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/inside/ios-18/tips/how-the-new-apple-invites-app-works-and-when-you-want-to-use-it); [Techlicious](https://www.techlicious.com/blog/apple-invites-app/); [Michael Tsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/02/04/apple-invites/).

- Apple tied creation to a paid subscription and its own ecosystem, which left Partiful's core users mostly untouched: Gen Z hosts with mixed iPhone/Android friend groups who want it free. A platform copy hurts most when it is free, preinstalled and cross-platform; Apple Invites was none of those for hosts.
- The leaders' protection was a mix of three things: (a) a recipient experience that works for anyone with a link, (b) a cultural or aesthetic identity that the copy doesn't have, and (c) owning the guest or recipient graph (Partiful's phone numbers, Moonpig's reminder dates).
- The "incumbent buys the fallen independent cheaply" pattern (American Greetings and Blue Mountain) suggests a cash-light digital leader can be absorbed rather than beaten.

- No data on whether Paperless Post lost share to Partiful or Apple.

## Q6. Why the losers failed (Blue Mountain, Cameo, and Moonpig's Experiences segment)

### Takeaway
Blue Mountain failed as a business, not as a product. It had huge free usage, "not one cent" of e-card revenue, and was bought at the peak of the bubble by a portal that wanted traffic. Cameo had a pandemic demand spike for a high-ticket novelty (about $70 AOV) with no built-in repeat occasion. It also had a cost base built for $1B expectations and a compliance problem in its push for enterprise revenue. Moonpig's Experiences segment shows that adding discretionary, cyclical gift products to an occasion engine can destroy value even inside a strong company.

### Cited Findings
- **Blue Mountain:**
  - The army of e-card users "produced not one cent of sales for the company's line of electronic greeting cards" — [Inc., Dec 1999](https://www.inc.com/magazine/19991201/15696.html) / [Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/20/Blue-Mountain-Arts-Inc.html) (attribution between pages not verified).
  - Excite@Home bought it partly to promote its broadband service — [Next TV](https://www.nexttv.com/news/excitehome-buys-blue-mt-eyeballs-382256).
  - It was resold for $35M in 2001; Wikipedia cites the deal as an example of dot-com excess — [Wikipedia: Blue Mountain Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mountain_Arts).
  - The CSMonitor attributes the broader e-card decline to the dot-com bust and the rise of social media — [CSMonitor, 2013](https://www.csmonitor.com/Technology/2013/1019/Inventions-that-were-going-to-change-the-world-but-didn-t/The-e-card).
- **Cameo:**
  - $1B valuation in March 2021, then a Series D cramdown about 90% lower — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/30/cameo-app-unicorn-silicon-valley-startup-celebrity-influencer-social-media/).
  - Layoffs: 87 (May 2022), 80 (November 2022), down to 33 staff (July 2023) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameo_(website)).
  - The Information headlined that "deep layoffs followed a dash for cash" — [The Information](https://feature.theinformation.com/articles/at-cameo-deep-layoffs-followed-a-dash-for-cash).
  - **July 2024 settlement:** a 30-state settlement led by the New York AG over celebrities in Cameo Business failing to disclose brand connections. The penalty was $600,000; Cameo said it could not pay, so it pays $100,000 with the rest suspended, and the full amount comes due if it goes bankrupt within 91 days — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/30/cameo-app-unicorn-silicon-valley-startup-celebrity-influencer-social-media/); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/cameo-once-a-1b-unicorn-is-broke-cant-afford-600k-fine/477563); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=4b6dfae7-6cd5-480a-b36a-48d96d777d9f).

  - It "never attracted many A-list celebrities" — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/cameo-once-a-1b-unicorn-is-broke-cant-afford-600k-fine/477563).
  - "The exact reason why Cameo has fallen so far isn't clear" — [Localogy](https://www.localogy.com/2024/07/d-list-platformcameo-on-shaky-financial-ground/).
  - **Pivot:** CameoX (December 2024) opened the platform to self-enrolled creators, with 31,000 creators and 155,000 Cameos over 18 months of pilot — [TechCrunch, Dec 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/03/celeb-greetings-app-cameo-pivots-to-creators).

It also claims TikTok creators delivered Cameo's "strongest year" in 2025 — [CreatorStack guide](https://creatorstack-io.pages.dev/guides/tiktok-cameo-personalized-video-sales-2026/) (low-reliability source; no primary release found).
- **Moonpig Experiences:**
  - Experiences revenue fell 19% in FY25 and was described as more exposed to cyclical pressures
  - £56.7m goodwill impairment; the CEO departure was announced alongside weak Experiences sales
  - [AJ Bell](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/articles/moonpig-ceo-depart-weak-experiences-sales-limits-revenue-growth); [Shares Magazine](https://sharesmagazine.co.uk/news/shares/moonpig-trots-into-the-red-as-experiences-arm-continues-to-struggle)

### Inferences
- **Blue Mountain:** commoditisation plus no monetisation. Once Yahoo, AOL and email clients offered free cards and social media made "happy birthday" free, the free e-card had no defensible value. The brand survived only as a small subscription product inside an incumbent.
- **Cameo:** no recurring use. Each video was a one-off, expensive gift tied to talent supply rather than to a calendar the brand controlled. Its 2020 GMV was driven by lockdown boredom and celebrities' spare time, and both faded. Its push into enterprise endorsements then created legal exposure.
- **Experiences:** at Moonpig, the occasion-and-reminder engine works for low-ticket, must-send items (cards). It did not rescue a high-ticket discretionary category (experience vouchers).
- **Jacquie Lawson** is the counter-example of a small, durable brand: a single distinctive art style, one occasion-rich format, and a subscription with no advertising. Its risk is billing practices, not demand.

### Gaps
- No contemporary data on Blue Mountain traffic decline after 2001, or on American Greetings' e-card revenue.
- No primary Cameo financials for 2023–2026. No Cameo repeat-purchase or cohort data.

## Implications for Ethos (interpretation only; not sourced facts)

1. **Recipient-side branding: be a badge, not a tax.** Evite shows people pay to remove clutter (ads). Hallmark and Paperless Post show branding can be the thing that signals effort.
   - Removing all Ethos branding from the paid end screen gives up the one recipient touchpoint that drove Partiful, Blue Mountain and Jacquie Lawson.
   - Test a small, premium "crown"-style mark on paid gifts, framed as a mark of quality rather than an ad. Keep the "send one back" CTA naming Ethos.
   - Instrument recipient-to-sender conversion, a number no analogue publishes. It would be a differentiating metric for investors.
2. **Capture the recipient as a known person, like Partiful.** Partiful's moat is that guests enter a phone number and become reachable, while the host never gets the list.
   - Ethos's "save the gift + reminder before the sender's next occasion" is the right mechanic. Collecting the recipient's own occasion dates (their partner's birthday, their anniversary) would turn each recipient into a Moonpig-style reminder record.
3. **Make reminders the core engine, Moonpig-style.** Moonpig's numbers point to the occasion-reminder database as the main retention asset:
   - 113m reminders
   - about 40% of orders within seven days of a reminder
   - more than 90% of revenue from existing customers
   - Plus members placing about a quarter of orders
   - "Ethos for Two" maps to Moonpig Plus. Watch Moonpig's flat orders per customer (2.92) as a warning that frequency is hard to raise.
4. **The name.** "Ethos" is a common word, so "send an Ethos" will not happen as easily as "send a Partiful".
   - Cameo shows a common word can be defended when used suggestively, but enforcement is costly.
   - Evite shows a market leader may even discourage genericisation.
   - Consider a consistent artifact noun used in all CTAs, previews and TikTok copy (for example the link preview, the end screen, and "send one back"). Then watch whether users adopt it.
   - Check EU and US trademark clearance against large platforms using "ethos".
5. **Platform-copy risk.** If Apple, TikTok, Instagram or an AI video tool ships "digital love letters", the Partiful playbook applies:
   - stay free to open on any phone with no app
   - keep a distinctive aesthetic and culture
   - own the reply/reminder graph
   - Apple's paywalled, ecosystem-bound copy did not visibly hurt Partiful, but a free, preinstalled feature would be more dangerous.
6. **Copycats.** The DigiBouquet wave shows free indie bouquet sites get cloned within months. Templates are not a moat; the brand, recipient capture and reminder loop are.
7. **Avoid the loser patterns:**
   - Blue Mountain: free volume with no revenue or retention. Keep the 2-free-template funnel tied to conversion and data capture.
   - Cameo: a one-off spike with no calendar. Tie every template to an annual occasion.
   - Jacquie Lawson and Blue Mountain: auto-renew complaints. For an EU company, make "Ethos for Two" renewal transparent and easy to cancel, both to protect the brand and to comply with consumer law.
