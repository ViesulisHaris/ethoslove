## Q1. Which tactics measurably shifted brand search or recall, versus views that never transferred to the brand?

### Takeaway
The best-documented brand transfer came from tactics where **the brand name or a proprietary brand asset was the joke or the format itself**: CeraVe's "Michael CeraVe", Duolingo's owl as the protagonist, e.l.f.'s song spelling out its name, Ryanair's talking plane. These were then amplified at scale by creators or paid boosts. Hard evidence of a lift in branded search or sales is scarce and mostly self-reported. Independent recall research (System1/Lumen on characters, SoundOut on sonic logos) consistently finds that assets **carrying the brand name or a recurring brand character** get attributed far more reliably than generic music or formats.

**Recall research: characters and sounds**
- System1 with Lumen: "fluent devices" (recurring brand characters) increased **spontaneous brand recall by up to 25%** versus the same ad without the character — [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/research-by-system1-shows-memorable-characters-in-ads-boost-chances-of-profit-by-30). US ads using memorable characters were about **50% more likely to score 3+ stars and 3x more likely to score 5 stars**. Only **4%** of US ads use them, and their share of IPA Effectiveness entries fell from **41% (1992) to 12%** — [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/analytics/behavioral-marketing/memorable-characters-ads-boost-chances-profit-30-just-4-us-brands-using-says-system1/). Study date not visible; verify recency.
- SoundOut Index 2025 (released ~13 Oct 2025; **174 brands, 70,000+ consumers**): Arby's ranked #1 with **86% recognition**, ahead of McDonald's, Netflix and TikTok — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/branding/this-is-the-most-famous-audio-logo-in-the-world-apparently). AutoZone and Liberty Mutual completed the top 3, all above 90% brand attribution — [Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/largest-ever-sonic-brand-tracking-study-reveals-the-best-performing-22657209). The 86% and 90% figures appear to measure different things; verify.
- SoundOut 2025: sonic logos that include the **brand name** are reported as **about 9x more effective at driving correct attribution** than purely musical cues — [Podcast News Daily](https://www.podcastnewsdaily.com/news/report-brand-name-audio-cues-outperform-music-only-logos/article_7325325b-9d2f-409e-839c-04378322daee.html); [MarComm News](https://marcommnews.com/largest-ever-sonic-brand-tracking-study-reveals-the-best-performing-sonic-logos-of-2025/). There is a "recall gap": 36% of consumers claim to recognise a given sonic logo but are right only 43% of the time, and **without a brand name accuracy falls to 18%** — [MarComm News](https://marcommnews.com/largest-ever-sonic-brand-tracking-study-reveals-the-best-performing-sonic-logos-of-2025/).
- Intel: SoundOut ranked Intel's classic "bong" #2 in the UK. Updated versions entered SoundOut's top 72 but performed "nowhere near" as well as the original (undated, likely **[historical]**) — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/sonic-branding-making-sound-count/en-GB/5374).
- Ipsos's widely quoted finding that sonic brand cues were **8.53x** more likely to appear in high-performing ads dates from **2020 [historical]**. Audio assets appeared in only about **8%** of video ads versus 92% for visual assets. I found it only second-hand — [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/From-Screen-to-Sound-Translating-Visual-Branding-into-Successful-Audio-Strategies); [Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/sonic-branding-statistics-2026).

**TikTok as a discovery and search engine**
- A TikTok-commissioned study with WARC (fielded Mar 2025, published Sep 2025) found **86% of Gen Z search on TikTok weekly vs 90% on Google**. For millennials it was **64% vs 94%** — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/09/10/tiktok-search-engine-ads-new-research/).

### Inferences
- In the cases with the strongest recall or brand outcomes, **the viral unit could not be separated from the brand**. "Michael CeraVe" is the brand name. "Eyes. Lips. Face." spells e.l.f. Duo's death only makes sense if you know Duolingo. Ryanair's plane *is* the speaker. Formats that spread without a name (generic water-bottle "restock" videos, generic unboxings) left more room for dupes. This is consistent with SoundOut's finding that brand-name audio is attributed about 9x better, and with System1's finding that characters lift recall.
- The brands that converted views into durable demand also gave fans a **repeatable reason to post again**: Crumbl's weekly flavours, Owala's colourway drops, Pop Mart's blind-box series, Duolingo's daily-lesson tie-in to resurrect Duo. A one-off viral post creates spikes. A recurring trigger makes the brand itself a format.
- **Evidence of a branded-search lift is weak in the public record.** Only the unverified Duolingo newsletter claim (+58% searches) and CeraVe's agency-reported sales and traffic come close. Most "results" are views, impressions and mentions. Treat views as necessary but not sufficient, and expect that brand-specific measurement (branded search, "how did you hear" surveys like Duolingo's) has to be built in-house.
- Pop Mart shows the ceiling: character IP plus social unboxing produced huge revenue, but share of attention decays (Dec 2025) and investors penalise single-asset dependence (Mar 2026).
- Stanley shows a cheap, transferable move: when an organic post about your product goes viral, **a fast reply from the brand account** (Reilly's video) attaches the brand to the creator's moment.

### Gaps
- No independent Google Trends or branded-search series for Duolingo (Feb 2025), Stanley (Nov 2023), Labubu (2025) or Owala could be retrieved. Search budget ran out. Pull these directly from Google Trends.
- No Duolingo-published results or Cannes entry for "Duo is dead" were found. The 38% downloads and 58% search figures are unverified.
- Not covered for lack of search budget: Liquid Death (persona and merch), Netflix "ta-dum" and McDonald's sonic evidence beyond the SoundOut ranking, Labubu's Lisa/BLACKPINK effect, and e.l.f.'s 2024–26 TikTok activity.
- I found no rigorous study comparing brand recall from a brand-owned original sound with recall from a trending sound on TikTok. The MediaScience 2021 finding is vendor-commissioned and old.

## Q2. How do brands keep viral demand from going to copycats when the format, not the brand, is what goes viral?

### Takeaway
Tactics that worked or are documented:
- Building **proprietary, hard-to-copy assets** into the thing that goes viral: a named character, a branded sound, a distinctive colour or shape, a pun on the brand name.
- **Owning the search and comment surfaces** where demand lands: keyword search ads on your own name, brand replies.
- **Verifiable authenticity** (Pop Mart's QR codes and holograms), backed by legal and supply responses.
- **Invite loops** where every share carries the brand (Partiful's branded invite cover).

Where the format is simple and open (for example the 2024–25 "Will you be my Valentine" website trend), cheap clones and DIY templates appear quickly.

**Brand name baked into the joke or the share**
- CeraVe's rumour ("Michael Cera invented CeraVe") carried the brand name in every repost — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/10/02/ogilvy-spread-conspiracy-theory-about-michael-cera-promote-cerave-it-worked).
- Partiful: every invite shows recipients a **branded cover image** and nudges them to install the app. Meme-ready templates come from in-house designers. "Celebrity lookalike contest" flyers carried **QR codes and Partiful links**, which spread through screenshots — [NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/).
- Apple Invites launched in **Feb 2025** as a direct copycat-category entrant (hosting needs iCloud+). Partiful's CEO said it had not seen users leaving for Apple — [NBC Connecticut/CNBC](https://www.nbcconnecticut.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/3547391/).

**Owning search and comments**
- TikTok's **Search Ads Toggle** (launched Oct 2023, initially US) lets advertisers serve In-Feed creative in TikTok search results. Advertisers can add **negative keywords** — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-search-ads-toggle?lang=en-ZA). In **Sep 2024** TikTok added **Search Ads Campaigns with keyword targeting** — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-updates-search-ads-campaigns-with-keyword-targeting/727957/).
- Agency executives said the search-ad launch pushed marketers to put more into **organic TikTok search** (captions, on-screen keywords) — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/with-tiktoks-new-search-ad-toggle-agency-execs-see-marketers-accelerating-organic-search-efforts).
- TikTok's blue, search-linked keywords in comments are chosen by its algorithm. Brands cannot switch them on. The feature was first tested in 2022 — [TechCrunch, Aug 2022 — historical](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/09/tiktoks-latest-test-feature-improve-search-capabilities/embed/).

**Distinctive assets that work without a name**
- System1's fluent-device research: recurring characters lift spontaneous brand recall by up to 25% — [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/research-by-system1-shows-memorable-characters-in-ads-boost-chances-of-profit-by-30).
- SoundOut 2025: without the brand name, correct sonic-logo attribution falls to **18%**. Branded audio cues are reported as about **9x** more effective — [MarComm News](https://marcommnews.com/largest-ever-sonic-brand-tracking-study-reveals-the-best-performing-sonic-logos-of-2025/); [Podcast News Daily](https://www.podcastnewsdaily.com/news/report-brand-name-audio-cues-outperform-music-only-logos/article_7325325b-9d2f-409e-839c-04378322daee.html).
- Examples from the brand cases above: Ryanair's plane face filter — [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/ryanair-social-media-strategy); Scrub Daddy's face-shaped product — [Delco Today](https://delco.today/2022/04/scrub-daddy-tiktok-pay-off/); Owala's limited colourways — [Apartment Therapy](https://www.apartmenttherapy.com/owala-water-bottle-review-37227709).

**Originality enforcement on Instagram (affects reposted or cloned formats)**
- From **April 2024**, when identical copies of content exist, Instagram recommends only the original. Reposts get a label linking to the source. Aggregator accounts that repeatedly repost others' content (reportedly **10+ times in 30 days**) are removed from recommendations — [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240502-instagram-updated-algorithm-prioritizes-original-content); [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy).
- An update reported on **30 Apr 2026** extended this from Reels to photos and carousels. Attributed reposts still count as unoriginal, and non-recommended posts reach only followers — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [SociallyIn](https://sociallyin.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-update-repost/).

### Inferences
- Copycats win when the viral thing is **a mechanic** (runaway button, blind-box reveal, cute bottle) and the brand is **only in the link**. They lose ground when the viral thing contains **an asset only the brand can legally or practically reproduce**: a named character, a trademarked look, a branded sound whose page shows the brand as creator, or an authentication step.
- Pop Mart's playbook (trademark the dupe nickname, sue retail channels, scale supply, add a QR/hologram that is visible on camera) is built for physical goods. The digital equivalent is a **verifiable origin mark inside the experience itself**: a consistent seal, name or end card that viewers learn to look for.
- Owning the name in search is cheap insurance. When comments ask "what's the website", demand goes to whoever ranks for the descriptive query. Keyword search ads on the brand name and the category term block copycats there in a way organic posting alone may not.
- Partiful is the closest structural analogue to a link-sent gift. Its growth loop works because **the received artefact carries the brand to a new person**, independent of any TikTok post.

### Gaps
- I found no published data on how much demand from a viral TikTok format leaked to copycat sites in digital-goods categories.
- I found no authoritative TikTok documentation on the mechanics of brand-owned original sound pages, beyond vendor guides. Broadly, an original sound is credited to the uploading account and its page aggregates every video using it, but I couldn't verify the current 2026 behaviour (credit display, whether business-account originals can be reused by personal accounts).
- Not covered for lack of budget: "pov: I opened the link he sent me"-style reaction formats as brand vehicles, and documented use of video replies to "what's the website" comments.

## Q3. What do TikTok's current rules allow for a small brand posting its own product, and what are the documented effects of disclosure toggles and on-screen URLs on reach?

### Takeaway
A brand may post its own product on its own account. TikTok requires the **content disclosure setting** to be on for any post promoting a brand, product or business:
- **"Your brand"** adds a **"Promotional content"** label.
- **"Branded content"** (paid third-party promotion) adds a **"Paid partnership"** label.

TikTok's help pages state that turning disclosure on **does not affect distribution**. Undisclosed commercial content **may be restricted, removed or made ineligible for the For You feed**, and from the H2 2025 update onward TikTok may also auto-apply the label or reduce visibility. Since the **13 Sep 2025** guideline update, content that sends users to **buy off-platform in markets where TikTok Shop operates has reduced visibility**. QR codes are generally FYF-ineligible. I found **no TikTok document that says a plain on-screen URL by itself triggers ineligibility**. Rules change often, so check the live pages.

### Cited Findings

**Disclosure setting: what it is and what TikTok says about reach**
- TikTok support page "Promoting a brand, product, or service":
  - Creators choose whether they're promoting **their own brand** (label **"Promotional content"**) or **branded content** for a third party (label **"Paid partnership"**).
  - The label **cannot be changed after posting**; fixing a mistake means deleting and reposting.
  - The setting **does not affect the distribution** of the video.
  - Missing disclosure may lead TikTok to **remove or restrict** the video.
  - Source: [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service?lang=en) (current version, read via search extract; verify).
- TikTok's help article "How to turn on the Commercial Content Disclosure setting" says the setting **must** be on when posting content that promotes a brand, product or service, then choose "Your brand" or "Branded content". Search metadata shows it was **last updated January 2026** — [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-turn-on-the-commercial-content-disclosure-setting-in-tiktok?lang=en).
- TikTok's advertiser help page:
  - It cites a TikTok internal study finding **no performance difference** between videos with and without proper disclosure.
  - Disclosure or a "Paid partnership" label does not change how content is recommended.
  - Improperly disclosed commercial content may be **ineligible for the For You feed**, and a successful appeal restores eligibility.
  - Source: [TikTok Business Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-the-commercial-content-disclosure-setting-for-advertisers).
- TikTok Creator Academy says content without proper disclosure **may not be eligible for the FYF**. The toggle is for organic posts; paid ads through **TikTok One** are labelled automatically — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creators/creator-portal/en-us/getting-paid-to-create/creating-branded-content-on-tiktok//).
- Community Guidelines overview: "If you're promoting a product, brand, or business, you must use TikTok's content disclosure setting" — [TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines).
- Third-party note (unverified against TikTok): posts with either disclosure toggle **cannot be set to Private** — [Wildmoka/Backlight help](https://help.wildmoka.backlight.co/hc/en-us/articles/41742868648983-TikTok-branded-content-policy-settings).
- A creator reported an unsponsored unboxing post being auto-flagged as promotional. The source is a TikTok Discover page, so this is anecdotal — [TikTok Discover](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/why-does-tt-say-i-cant-promote-another-brand-or-third-party-on-live-disclosure).

**Enforcement timeline**
- **Community Guidelines update effective 13 Sep 2025** (announced mid-Aug 2025):
  - It refined the commercial content rules, requiring clearer disclosure of sponsored posts and affiliate links, and stated TikTok will **reduce the visibility of content that directs users to "purchase products off-platform in markets where TikTok Shop is available"**.
  - TechCrunch judged the changes "not as substantial", mostly rewording for clarity.
  - Sources: [TechCrunch, 15 Aug 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/); [Marketing4eCommerce](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/en/tiktok-updates-its-community-guidelines/).
- TikTok's "Accounts and Features" page:
  - Content sending users to buy off-platform in TikTok Shop markets has **reduced visibility**.
  - Accounts that post a lot of FYF-ineligible content can themselves become FYF-ineligible and harder to find.
  - Repeated failure to disclose can lead to **temporary posting restrictions or a ban**.
  - Source: [TikTok Community Guidelines – Accounts and Features](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/accounts-features/) (via search extract; verify).
- Wording change in the H2 2025 version of "Regulated Goods, Services, and Commercial Activities":
  - The earlier text said undisclosed commercial content "will be ineligible for the FYF".
  - The newer text reportedly says TikTok "may reduce its visibility or apply the content disclosure setting", so TikTok may now **auto-apply the label** rather than block the post.
  - Sources: [TikTok – Regulated Commercial Activities (2025H2)](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/regulated-commercial-activities?cgversion=2025H2update); commentary at [Social Scale Hub](https://www.socialscalehub.com/academy/tiktok-community-guidelines-september-24-2026) (verify both).
- Further versions: a terms tracker lists the Community Guidelines as last updated **2 May 2026** — [TOS Tracker](https://tostracker.app/document/tiktok-community). A third-party article refers to a **24 Sep 2026** guidelines change — [Social Scale Hub](https://www.socialscalehub.com/academy/tiktok-community-guidelines-september-24-2026). I couldn't verify either against TikTok.
- Automated detection of undisclosed branded content began with a small group of US creators (2025) — [CreatorIQ](https://creatoriq.com/blog/tiktok-enforcement-branded-content-disclosures). Flagged posts get an in-app notification — [Bazaarvoice](https://www.bazaarvoice.com/updates/the-new-rules-of-tiktok-what-brands-and-creators-need-to-know/). Low-quality third-party sites claim mandatory enforcement from **1 Sep 2025** with a 24-hour window to disclose or appeal (unverified).

**FYF eligibility: links, QR codes, originality**
- TikTok's FYF Eligibility Standards:
  - Content with **QR codes is usually FYF-ineligible**, with exceptions such as e-commerce.
  - Unoriginal or low-quality content is ineligible.
  - So is content that "tricks or manipulates users" to inflate engagement.
  - FYF-ineligible content is **not removed**; it can still be found via search and the profile.
  - Source: [TikTok FYF Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards/) (via search extract).
- I found no official TikTok statement that **showing a URL on screen** by itself makes a post ineligible. Third-party claims (for example that repeating the same external link across many videos looks like spam) are unverified vendor assertions — [link.boo](https://link.boo/guides/tiktok-shadowban). Switching to a Business account does not itself change reach — [Poster.ly](https://www.poster.ly/blog/why-is-my-tiktok-not-getting-views).
- Creators see the specific reason in an in-app "not eligible for For You" notice and can appeal from it (third-party guides, consistent with TikTok's appeal language above) — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/how-to-tell-if-tiktok-is-suppressing-your-video).

**Music rules for brand accounts**
- Business accounts are limited to the **Commercial Music Library (CML)**. Mainstream chart music is excluded because its licences exclude commercial use — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok). TikTok also stopped verified brands using popular tracks [historical: 2021 change] — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-changes-rules-on-music-usage-by-businesses/577734/).
- Business accounts **cannot duet, stitch or react** to videos that use regular-library songs, but can with CML sounds — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok).
- Estimates of the CML's size range from **600K+ to 1M+** pre-cleared tracks. Trending CML tracks can be found through Creative Center playlists — [Soundstripe 2026](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/tiktok-music-library-explained); [House of Marketers](https://houseofmarketers.com/tiktoks-royalty-free-music-library-for-ad-content-600k-songs/).

**Instagram parallel**
- Instagram recommends only originals and drops repeat-reposting aggregators from recommendations (Apr 2024; extended to photos and carousels Apr 2026). This matters for brands cross-posting identical content across several accounts — [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240502-instagram-updated-algorithm-prioritizes-original-content); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/).

### Inferences
- A small brand posting its own product should **always turn on "Your brand"**. TikTok's own pages say this has no distribution penalty, and not doing it risks restriction or FYF ineligibility. The "Promotional content" label is the price of eligibility, not a reach tax, at least according to TikTok. Independent tests of that claim weren't found.
- A post that names a website and shows the product **without disclosure** combines two risk factors: undisclosed commercial content, and directing users off-platform to buy where TikTok Shop operates. This is the most plausible explanation for an immediate zero-view "Community Guidelines" restriction. It is inference; TikTok publishes no URL-specific rule.
- With disclosure on, the remaining risk is the **off-platform purchase** visibility reduction (Sep 2025). Whether a URL in a disclosed post counts as "directing users to purchase off-platform" isn't documented. Keeping the purchase path in the bio rather than on screen is the conservative reading.
- QR codes on screen are the riskiest of all (explicitly "usually FYF-ineligible").

### Gaps
- I couldn't read the live TikTok pages because `ads.tiktok.com` and `www.tiktok.com` are blocked by the session's network allowlist. The exact current wording (post–May 2026, and any 24 Sep 2026 change) needs checking by hand.
- I found no independent A/B data on reach with and without the "Promotional content" label, or with and without on-screen URLs.
- I didn't cover YouTube Shorts' "includes paid promotion" disclosure, Instagram's Branded Content tool, or how YouTube treats links in Shorts (search budget exhausted).
- I didn't cover Spark Ads eligibility rules or how disclosure interacts with Spark-boosted organic posts.

## Q4. How do the best brands use a brand account alongside faceless or story accounts and creators?

### Takeaway
The pattern among the strongest cases has three layers:
1. A **brand account with a persona or character** that earns its own reach and replies to the culture (Duolingo, Ryanair, Scrub Daddy, Stanley's president replying to the car-fire video).
2. **Creators doing most of the volume**, either organically around a repeatable trigger (Crumbl reviews, Labubu unboxings) or seeded and paid at scale (CeraVe's 400–450 influencers, pay-per-view clipping at about $1–5 per 1,000 views).
3. **Paid boosting of proven organic posts** (Spark Ads, search ads).

App marketers in 2025–26 added fleets of brand-owned "internal UGC" or slideshow accounts. The evidence for that tactic is almost entirely vendor-reported.

### Cited Findings

**Brand account as character and conversation hub**
- Duolingo grew its account from about 50K to 16M+ followers through an "unhinged" mascot-led persona, prioritising entertainment over app promotion — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot). Brands including Netflix, Baskin Robbins and Cheez-It, and Dua Lipa ("Til' death duo part"), joined in during Duo's "death" — [Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/digital-marketing-ad-tech-news/duolingo-kills-mascot-duo-new-marketing-stunt/2602261).
- Scrub Daddy and Duolingo used **cross-brand crossovers** to swap audiences [historical, 2022] — [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/business/scrub-daddy-tiktok-strategy-duolingo-20220420.html).
- e.l.f. ran a second, Gen Z-specific channel (@elfyeah) alongside the main brand account — [Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/marketers-on-fire-e-l-f-cosmetics-cmo-kory-marchisotto).
- Stanley's president personally replied to a creator's viral post with a new-car offer (Nov 2023) — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/tiktok-stanley-cups-water-bottle-craze/).

**Creators: organic, seeded and paid**
- Crumbl:
  - It said in 2021 it had "never sponsored or compensated creators" [historical; current practice unverified] — [Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/how-crumbl-cookies-tiktok-account-is-driving-revenue/).
  - It **boosted its own organic posts with Spark Ads** (22M reach, +1,500% followers in two months, per TikTok) — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/crumbl-521).
  - By Dec 2024 it ran a **30+ person** social team — [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2024/12/17/crumbl-cookies-have-become-a-hit-among-tween-and-teen-girls/).
- CeraVe seeded a rumour through **400–450 influencers** for about a month before a single paid TV spot — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/10/02/ogilvy-spread-conspiracy-theory-about-michael-cera-promote-cerave-it-worked).
- Scrub Daddy's early growth involved creator Addison Rae. Coverage cites deals with about **25 TikTok influencers** — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90756764/how-to-find-tiktok-influencers-scrub-daddy) [attribution to this specific outlet uncertain].

**Pay-per-view clipping (2025–26)**
- Digiday describes clipping as **marketers paying independent "clippers" to post their clips across social platforms**, typically at **$1–5 per 1,000 views** — [Digiday "WTF is clipping"](https://digiday.com/media/wtf-is-clipping-the-low-lift-creator-strategy-grabbing-advertisers-attention/).
- Critics say clipping rewards outrage, volume and manipulation over substance. Supporters say it is cheaper than traditional creator campaigns — [Digiday "The case for and against clipping"](https://digiday.com/marketing/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/).
- Whop **Content Rewards**: brands set a rate per 1,000 verified views and pay only for approved clips — [Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards).
- Reported rates (vendor and secondary sources):
  - Average about **$1/1K**, with listings from **$0.20 to $6/1K** — [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards).
  - Music campaigns as low as **$0.30/1K**; original on-camera UGC campaigns at the higher end, with one brand-side post citing up to **$75/1K** for UGC — [Content Rewards](https://contentrewards.com/blog/ugc-campaigns-clipping-guide).
  - Whop compares this with roughly **$25 CPM** for traditional ads — [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards).
  - Vyro (MrBeast-backed) about **$3/1K** — [Trends.vc](https://trends.vc/clipping-businesses-pay-per-view-distribution-clip-armies-view-verification/).
- Cluely and Whop are cited as heavy users of clipping — [Leveling Up](https://newsletter.levelingup.com/p/creator-powered-campaigns-are-the-future). One low-authority blog claims about **300M views in 90 days** for Cluely (unverified) — [Ash Media](https://ashmedia.pages.dev/blog/b2b-clipping-strategy).
- View fraud is the main risk. Serious campaigns screen for bots, block view-farm regions and verify views before paying — [Trends.vc](https://trends.vc/clipping-businesses-pay-per-view-distribution-clip-armies-view-verification/).

**Brand-owned faceless and slideshow account fleets**
- Cal AI reportedly began running **"over a dozen" brand-owned TikTok accounts from January 2025**, mixing carousels, repurposed faceless videos and original content as a distribution system — [8x.social](https://www.8x.social/en/blog/cal-ai-multi-account). A vendor calls this **"Internal UGC"**: brand accounts posting content designed to look user-generated, scaling slideshow formats that spike downloads, and A/B-testing modular hook slides — [Stormy AI](https://stormy.ai/blog/cal-ai-tiktok-marketing-playbook). Both are vendor sources with commercial interest; revenue claims are unverified.
- A March 2026 newswire piece describes a "matrix strategy" of 5–10 accounts funnelling to one checkout (general e-commerce) — [WorldNewsWire via Observer-Reporter](https://stocks.observer-reporter.com/observerreporter/article/worldnewswire-2026-3-12-the-faceless-e-commerce-empire-scaling-automated-tiktok-stores-with-ai).
- Constraint: TikTok's FYF standards make content that "tricks or manipulates users" to inflate engagement ineligible, along with unoriginal content. Instagram removes repeat-reposting aggregators from recommendations — [TikTok FYF Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards/); [Gigazine](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240502-instagram-updated-algorithm-prioritizes-original-content).

**The product as the share vehicle**
- Partiful's invite is itself branded media. Every recipient sees a branded cover and an install prompt, and the company has grown without disclosing ad spend — [NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/); [NBC New York/CNBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/).

### Inferences
- The brand account is where **recognition is stored**: persona, character, replies, crossovers. Story accounts, creators and clippers are where **reach is bought or borrowed**. Reach only becomes recognition if the borrowed content carries the brand's distinctive assets. A clipper or story-account post that shows a generic mechanic builds the format, not the brand.
- Spark Ads are the documented bridge. Find organic winners (your own or creators') and pay to extend them, so the boosted post still looks native.
- Multi-account "internal UGC" fleets are a real 2025–26 practice, but they carry platform risk (inauthentic-engagement and unoriginal-content rules on TikTok, aggregator demotion on Instagram), and their claimed results are unaudited. Treat them as a testing lab for hooks, then concentrate winning formats on the brand account and creators.

### Gaps
- Not covered for lack of search budget: TikTok One / Creator Marketplace pricing, TikTok Shop affiliate commission rates and benchmarks, and Spark Ads CPM or lift benchmarks for 2025–26.
- I found no independent study measuring whether clipping or slideshow-fleet views raise branded search or recall compared with brand-account posts.
- Liquid Death (persona, merch, cross-account play) wasn't covered.

## Implications for Ethos

*This section is interpretation, kept separate from the cited facts above. It draws on the Ethos context in the brief and the findings above.*

1. **Make the opening ritual the brand, not the URL.** The "tap to open, sound on" screen, the wax seal or envelope, and the pink "blush" card are Ethos's candidate distinctive assets. Fix them as non-negotiables across all 54 templates and every story frame: same seal shape, same pink, same opening motion. System1's character research and SoundOut's attribution findings suggest consistency matters more than novelty. A recurring couple ("Maya and Leo") can work as a light fluent device if they recur with stable looks and voice.
2. **Own a sound.** Create a short, ownable opening sting for the gift (seal crack plus chime), ideally with a voiced or sung "ethos" in it, because brand-name audio is attributed about 9x better per SoundOut 2025. Post it as original audio from @tryethos so the sound page credits the brand and gathers every reuse. Business accounts can't use chart music, so an owned sound costs nothing in reach terms. Verify how original sounds from business accounts can be reused by personal accounts in 2026.
3. **Stay inside the rules on purpose.**
   - Keep "Your brand" on for every post that shows the product. TikTok says it doesn't affect distribution, while undisclosed promotion risks restriction or auto-labelling.
   - Keep URLs and QR codes off screen (QR codes are explicitly usually FYF-ineligible, and off-platform purchase prompts lose visibility in TikTok Shop markets).
   - Let the visual assets and the spoken or sung name carry recognition, with the purchase path in the bio.
   - Use the in-app ineligibility notice and appeal when a post is restricted.
4. **Capture the "PLEASE TELL ME THE WEBSITE" demand in search, not on screen.** Pin a brand-account comment that names the brand as a search term rather than a link (for example "ethos gift"). Put consistent, searchable words in captions and text overlays ("digital love letter", "ethos gift"). Consider small TikTok Search Ads on "ethos", "tryethos" and category terms so clones can't buy the query. Track Google Trends and Search Console for "tryethos" and "ethos gift" weekly, as the recall metric views can't show. Add a "how did you hear about us" question at checkout, as Duolingo does.
5. **Assume the format will be copied.** The carousel-plus-gift-link format, like the Valentine yes/no sites, is easy to clone and DIY templates already exist. Ethos's moat has to sit inside the received gift:
   - A visible origin mark (seal monogram, "made with ethos" end card).
   - A Partiful-style loop where every recipient sees the brand and a "make one back" prompt.
   - Templates distinctive enough that viewers recognise "an Ethos" on screen.
