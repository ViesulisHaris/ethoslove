# PART A: Marketing science of becoming the remembered brand

## A1. Ehrenberg-Bass: mental and physical availability, distinctive brand assets, category entry points, and how a small brand builds memory structures

### Takeaway
The Ehrenberg-Bass school says brands grow mainly by reaching more category buyers, not by deepening loyalty. To be bought, a brand must come to mind in buying situations (mental availability, built on links to category entry points, or CEPs) and be easy to find and buy (physical availability). Distinctive assets act as shortcuts that trigger the brand, but only once they are both famous and unique. Building them takes a long time and must be done alongside the brand name. I found no published "X years" figure.

### Cited Findings
**Mental and physical availability, growth through reach**
- In Byron Sharp's *How Brands Grow* (2010), mental availability is the likelihood that a brand is noticed or thought of in buying situations. Physical availability is how easily the brand can be found and bought (presence, prominence, relevance). Without physical availability, mental-availability efforts may not pay off fully. Source is a secondary summary; the book itself was not accessible — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/how-brands-grow/summary/)
- Double Jeopardy law: smaller brands have fewer buyers, and those buyers are also somewhat less loyal. Defection rates also follow the double-jeopardy pattern. Secondary summary — [Sharley Substack summary of How Brands Grow Pt 2](https://sharley.substack.com/p/summary-how-brands-grow-part-2-by)
- Small brands: an Ehrenberg-Bass-linked article says tiny brands grow by widening their customer base, which depends on availability, including mental availability. It also cites the Duplication of Purchase law (brands share customers in line with popularity) to argue that a tiny brand need not be "different" to grow — [B&T, "Small but mighty"](https://www.bandt.com.au/small-but-mighty-tiny-brand-advertising-strategies/)

**Category entry points (CEPs)**
- Ehrenberg-Bass defines CEPs as "the building blocks of Mental Availability": the thoughts category buyers have as they move into a category purchase — [Ehrenberg-Bass, Identifying and prioritising CEPs](https://marketingscience.info/research-services/identifying-and-prioritising-category-entry-points/) (PDF: [assets.marketingscience.info](https://assets.marketingscience.info/identifying-and-prioritising-ceps.pdf))
- Romaniuk's brand-health metrics:
  - Mental penetration: the share of category buyers who link the brand to at least one CEP. Mental penetration among non-users matters especially for small brands — [Bigeye, Better Brand Health with Jenni Romaniuk Pt 2](https://www.bigeyeagency.com/insights/better-brand-health-with-jenni-romaniuk-part-2)
  - Network size, plus mental market share (the brand's share of all brand–CEP links) — [Marketing Week](https://marketingweek.com/does-your-brand-measure-up-when-it-comes-to-mental-availability/)
- Small brands are advised to pick one CEP, use it consistently, and broaden as they grow. Agency commentary, not a primary study — [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/small-but-mighty-tiny-brand-advertising-strategies/)

**Distinctive brand assets and the fame/uniqueness grid**
- Romaniuk's *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (OUP, 2018) covers fame and uniqueness metrics, the grid, long-term asset-building strategy, and how to protect assets — [Ehrenberg-Bass book page](https://marketingscience.info/building-distinctive-brand-assets/); [OUP](https://www.oup.com.au/books/higher-education/business-marketing/9780190311506)
- The grid is a 2×2 of fame and uniqueness:
  - Fame: the share of category buyers who think of the brand when they encounter the asset.
  - Uniqueness: whether the asset evokes only your brand rather than competitors.
  - An asset needs both to work as a brand shortcut — [Ehrenberg-Bass, Brands need distinctive assets](https://marketingscience.info/brands-need-distinctive-assets/); [Contagious interview with Romaniuk](https://contagious.com/io/article/jenni-romaniuk-on-distinctive-assets); [ANA, How to manage distinctive brand assets](https://www.ana.net/miccontent/show/id/aa-distinctive-brand-assets)
- New assets start low on both axes. If an asset still fails to register after sustained investment, Ehrenberg-Bass suggests major revision of how it is executed, or switching focus — [Ehrenberg-Bass, Brands of Distinction](https://marketingscience.info/brands-of-distinction)
- Fame should be tested unprompted. Prompted testing can inflate fame scores by roughly 20 points — [Ehrenberg-Bass news: Mumbrella360 talk](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/mumbrella360-ehrenberg-bass-professor-says-youre-poorly-branding-if-youre-focusing-on-assets-over-your-brand)
- Romaniuk separates two phases:
  - Asset building: the asset still needs the brand name alongside it ("co-presentation").
  - Asset using: the asset can stand in for the brand.
  - She calls building "a long process that requires patience" and warns against putting assets ahead of the brand name itself — [Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/mumbrella360-ehrenberg-bass-professor-says-youre-poorly-branding-if-youre-focusing-on-assets-over-your-brand-832843)
- Characters, logos and fonts were the asset types most likely to be both unique and famous in Romaniuk's research. Reported by an IP law firm, which also suggests (as its own speculation) that legal protectability may be linked — [Azrights](https://azrights.com/blog/protecting-brand-identifiers-what-we-can-learn-from-jenni-romaniuk)
- **Conflict:** on which axis matters more, one source says uniqueness, another says fame — [Marketing Architects](https://www.marketingarchitects.com/blog/v41-n3-your-brand-assets-may-be-working-against-you). Treat the weighting as contested. A site attributing specific thresholds to Romaniuk (e.g., fame >60%, uniqueness >70%) could not be verified and is excluded.

**Share of voice and the long/short balance (IPA, Binet & Field)**
- *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA databank, 996 campaigns) points to roughly 60% brand-building and 40% sales activation. Brands whose share of voice exceeds their market share (excess share of voice, ESOV) tend to grow — [Alex Murrell summary](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it); [IPA presentation (2024 PDF)](https://ipa.co.uk/media/14463/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final_030424.pdf)
- Binet says growth speed depends on whether share of voice sits above or below market share, including in B2B — [The Drum (2019)](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2019/05/23/exclusive-look-binet-and-field-s-new-b2b-marketing-research)
- Commentary stresses that 60:40 is an average across categories, not a fixed rule — [WARC, Beyond 60:40](https://warc.com/content/article/event-reports/beyond-6040-putting-effectiveness-in-context/124156); [B&T, "The magic split…"](https://www.bandt.com.au/the-magic-split-between-brand-performance-spending-needs-to-end/)

### Inferences
- For a small brand, the Ehrenberg-Bass logic points to three priorities:
  - Reach lots of potential buyers (not just existing fans).
  - Own a few clearly chosen CEPs (e.g., "anniversary," "long-distance," "last-minute Valentine's") before broadening.
  - Show the same distinctive assets (wax seal, envelope, blush pink) every time, together with the name, for years.
- "Made with Ethos" footers on free gifts work as reach at near-zero cost. Each recipient is a category buyer exposed to name plus assets in a real gifting moment. That is very close to the Ehrenberg-Bass ideal of co-presentation at a CEP.
- Romaniuk's warning about asset "uniqueness" matters here. Wax seals and envelopes are common across the love-letter and invitation category. Unless they are executed in a very specific, consistent way, they risk being famous but not unique, i.e., signalling "love letter" rather than "Ethos."

### Gaps
- No primary source gives a time in years to build a distinctive asset or mental availability for a small brand. The book chapters were not accessible.
- No Ehrenberg-Bass study specific to digital/DTC micro-brands or gift categories was found.
- Sharp's book and Romaniuk's *Better Brand Health* were only accessible via secondary summaries.
- An unverified claim that "CEP breadth explained ~60% of variance in share growth" was found in agency material and excluded.

## A2. Category design ("Play Bigger", the category king) vs. academic evidence on first-mover advantage

### Takeaway
The "category king takes ~76% of category value" claim is the authors' own, unreplicated, survivorship-prone analysis of VC-backed tech companies. The academic record is more sobering:
- Golder & Tellis found nearly half of true pioneers failed, and early leaders who entered later did better.
- Later reviews find first-mover effects depend heavily on how "advantage" is measured.
Category leadership looks more like a product of sustained commitment and execution than of being first.

### Cited Findings
**Play Bigger**
- The book (Ramadan, Peterson, Lochhead, Maney; HarperBusiness, 2016) says the "category king" captures about 76% of the total market capitalisation of its category — [Antoine Buteau book summary](https://www.antoinebuteau.com/play-bigger-book-summary.md)
- Co-author Dave Peterson's Stanford talk describes the basis:
  - 35 category kings representing 76% of the market cap of their categories, from a field of ~8,000 companies.
  - Measured on public market cap and private valuations of VC-backed companies founded 2000–2015 — [Stanford eCorner transcript](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/how-to-be-a-category-king-entire-talk-transcript.pdf); [S2G podcast](https://metacast.app/podcast/the-s2g-podcast/VcrMcEde/becoming-a-category-king-with-the-play-bigger-team/neLz0cyU)
- I found no independent replication of the 76% figure. It comes from a single analysis by the book's authors, who sell category-design consulting — [search synthesis of the above]; see also [IndustryWeek, "Are you different or just better?"](https://www.industryweek.com/growth-strategies/are-you-different-or-just-better)

**Pioneer research**
- Golder & Tellis (1993), "Pioneer Advantage: Marketing Logic or Marketing Legend?", *Journal of Marketing Research* 30(2):158–170:
  - Historical method, ~500 brands in 50 categories.
  - True market pioneers had a 47% failure rate and a mean market share of about 10%.
  - "Early leaders" who entered after the pioneer had a much lower failure rate (one summary cites 8%) and higher shares — [Golder & Tellis paper (Duke course copy)](https://people.duke.edu/~moorman/Marketing-Strategy-Seminar-2015/Session%205/Golder%20and%20Tellis.pdf); [Andrew Clark summary](https://andrewclark.super.site/all-media/pioneer-advantage-marketing-logic-or-marketing-legend)
- Their follow-ups, "First to Market, First to Fail?" (1996) and the book *Will and Vision* (2001), argue that enduring leadership comes from vision, persistence, financial commitment and asset leverage, not from being first — [Publishers Weekly, Will and Vision](https://www.publishersweekly.com/9780071375498)
- Critiques:
  - Survivorship and sample construction. Some databases group "early entrants" with "first movers," and PIMS-based studies can only measure early-entry advantage. Steve Blank argues organisational capability matters more than order of entry — [Steve Blank, "Why pioneers have arrows in their backs"](https://steveblank.com/2010/10/04/why-pioneers-are-the-ones-with-the-arrows-in-their-backs/)
- Counter-evidence: Robinson & Min (2002, JMR 39(1):120) argue that the pioneer's temporary monopoly plus first-mover advantages typically offset its higher survival risk (industrial goods) — [DOI 10.1509/jmkr.39.1.120.18938](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.39.1.120.18938)
- Lieberman & Montgomery (1988) identified three sources of first-mover advantage: technological leadership, pre-emption of assets, and buyer switching costs. Their later review notes that results conflict depending on whether advantage is measured by profit, market share or survival — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/how-first-mover-advantage-once-unquestioned-lost-its-grip-on-entrepreneurs-and-business-researchers/); [Lieberman & Montgomery, "Conundra and Progress"](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/lkcsb_research/3557)
- Practitioner fast-follower case study — [Branding Strategy Insider](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/how-a-fast-follower-brand-overcame-a-first-mover-advantage/)

### Inferences
- "Category creation" is best treated as a positioning and communication discipline: naming the problem and the category, then being consistently linked to it. It is not a guarantee of share.
- The low-switching-cost, low-tech-barrier structure of digital gift pages means none of the three Lieberman–Montgomery first-mover mechanisms is strong here. There is no proprietary tech lead, no scarce assets to pre-empt, and minimal switching costs. Leadership would have to come from memory (mental availability), distribution (Etsy, app store, footer virality) and execution.
- The Golder & Tellis finding that later-entering "early leaders" win is a warning: a well-funded entrant (e.g., a big card or e-gift company) could overtake a small pioneer. The pioneer's best defence is to build memory structures and a brand-plus-legal moat early.

### Gaps
- A widely quoted meta-analysis (VanderWerf & Mahon 1997, reportedly "54 of 66 tests supported first-mover advantage") appeared in search results, but I could not tie it to a verifiable source page. Excluded from findings.
- No sourced fast-follower examples in consumer internet (e.g., Google after earlier search engines, Facebook after Friendster/MySpace) were retrieved this session. These are commonly cited but are unsourced here.
- No academic evidence was found on category creation specifically in consumer apps or gifting.

## A3. Brand name as noun/verb: does it help, and the genericide risk

### Takeaway
Becoming the everyday word for a thing is the ultimate memory win, but it is also how marks die. The legal test is whether the public sees the word as the name of the product type rather than a source.

Owners who:
- supply a generic alternative term,
- use the mark as an adjective, and
- police misuse (Velcro, Xerox)

keep their marks. Owners who let the mark become the product's name (Escalator, Aspirin, Thermos), or who ignore resellers' generic use (Kornspitz, EU), lose them.

Evidence that verb-use *helps* sales is thin and mostly opinion.

### Cited Findings
**How brands lost their marks**
- Escalator: coined and registered in 1900. The US Patent Office later cancelled the registration (1950, *Haughton Elevator Co. v. Seeberger*), noting that even Otis had used "escalator" generically in its own patents — [AGIP, "Escalator: when fame ends a trademark"](https://www.agip.com/News/en/articles/23506)
- Aspirin: Bayer lost US rights after WWI, when its US assets were seized. Sources give 1917 vs 1919 — a date conflict — [Lovemoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/gallerylist/575033/59-brand-names-that-unexpectedly-became-everyday-words); [Consumer Reports/Consumerist](https://www.consumerreports.org/consumerist/15-product-trademarks-that-have-become-victims-of-genericization/)
- Thermos: the company first encouraged everyday use of "thermos" to dominate the market. It then lost in a 1962 US court ruling that the term had become generic — [Lovemoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/gallerylist/575033/59-brand-names-that-unexpectedly-became-everyday-words)

**Brands still fighting**
- Xerox: has policed use through campaigns ("Xerox machine," "photocopy"). It has protected the mark in the US and Canada, but reportedly became generic in Russia, Bulgaria, Portugal and Romania — [Lovemoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/gallerylist/575033/59-brand-names-that-unexpectedly-became-everyday-words)
- Velcro: the 2017 "Don't Say Velcro" music video had the legal team ask people to say "hook and loop fastener."
  - It drew thousands of comments from 150+ countries, and a second video followed.
  - Lawyers noted such campaigns create evidence of policing that can help before a registry or court if the mark is attacked.
  - Velcro still holds ~23 active US registrations including VELCRO — [Law.com Corporate Counsel](https://law.com/corpcounsel/2017/10/12/velcro-fights-misuse-of-trademark-with-dont-say-velcro-campaign); [World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/refreshing-amusing-and-a-genericide-deterrent-the-don-t-say-velcro-video-14671); [World IP Review, second video](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/velcro-issues-second-video-in-fight-against-genericide-16146)

**Courts and verb use**
- US: verb use is not automatically generic use. *Elliott v. Google* (9th Cir.) held that "verb use does not automatically constitute generic use" ("google it" did not kill GOOGLE) — [Mondaq analysis](https://mondaq.co.uk/unitedstates/trademark/738404/&location=articleauthorphototop)

**EU and UK cases**
- EU (CJEU, Kornspitz, C-409/12, 2014): a mark can be revoked where the proprietor failed to encourage sellers to tell customers that the sign is a trade mark, while end customers used it as the product's name. Belgian commentary reads this as a duty of "vigilant conduct" on the owner — [CMS Belgium](https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/revocation-of-successful-trademarks-that-became-common-in-the-trade)
- UK (December 2025, *Dryrobe*): the High Court upheld several DRYROBE registrations against a genericide challenge — [Mondaq, Dryrobe avoids genericide](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/trademark/1722090/dryrobe-avoids-genericide-in-high-court-decision)

**Other recent US cases**
- GRUYERE (4th Cir., 3 March 2023, *Interprofession du Gruyère v. U.S. Dairy Export Council*, 61 F.4th 407): "GRUYERE" held generic for cheese in the US, based on consumer-perception evidence "from any competent source" — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/22-1041/22-1041-2023-03-03.html); [Ladas & Parry](https://ladas.com/?p=11750)
- Booking.com (US Supreme Court, 2020, 8–1): a "generic.com" term is not automatically generic. If consumers see it as a source identifier, it can be registered — [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent_and_Trademark_Office_v._Booking.com_B._V.); [Fish & Richardson](https://www.fr.com/supreme-court-finds-booking-com-protectable-rejects-sweeping-rule-denying-protection-to-generic-com-marks)

**Scholarship on verb use and evidence**
- Shanahan (2016, Academy of Marketing Science) argues the marketing literature has focused on preventing genericisation and overlooked value created by "brand-as-verb" usage. Abstract only — [Kevin Shanahan, academia.edu](https://msstate.academia.edu/KevinShanahan)
- A 2024 BYU Law Review article proposes corpus linguistics to track the drift of a mark toward genericness. It notes that surveys and dictionaries, the methods courts use, are criticised as subjective — [BYU Law Review vol. 50](https://digitalcommons.law.byu.edu/lawreview/vol50/iss1/8)
- Practitioner guidance: use the mark as an adjective followed by the generic noun ("Xerox photocopies," not "Xeroxes") — [Lovemoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/gallerylist/575033/59-brand-names-that-unexpectedly-became-everyday-words)
- Owner conduct (whether the owner contributed to or tolerated generic use) is central to genericide analysis in several jurisdictions. Example: a 2026 Turkish law review — [Mondaq Turkey](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/turkey/trademark/1764456/genericide-in-turkish-trademark-law-legislation-and-judicial-practice)

### Inferences
- Wanting "send an Ethos" to catch on is commercially sensible, but it carries the genericide risk directly. The safer pattern, inferred from Velcro, Xerox and Kornspitz:
  1. Always pair the mark with a generic noun: "an Ethos gift," "an Ethos love letter," "Ethos gift page."
  2. Deliberately popularise a generic category term that competitors can use, e.g., "digital gift page" or "love-letter link." The public then has a word for the category that isn't "Ethos."
  3. Use ® (once registered) or ™ on first/prominent use.
  4. Brief Etsy resellers, affiliates and partners in writing on correct usage (the Kornspitz duty).
  5. Keep a file of policing actions.
- Some playful verb use by customers ("I Ethos'd her") is unlikely to be fatal on its own (*Elliott v. Google*). The company's own copy should still avoid using "Ethos" as a verb or as the generic name of the product.
- "Ethos" is an existing common word, so genericide in the "Kleenex" sense is less likely than for a coined word that fills a naming gap. A dictionary meaning already exists, so the public is less likely to adopt it as the generic name for digital gifts. This is an inference, not a tested legal view.

### Gaps
- No controlled consumer study was found showing that brand-as-verb usage raises recall, preference or sales.
- No 2024–2026 EU court decision revoking a mark for genericness was found. The search found Kornspitz (2014), the UK Dryrobe case (2025) and a non-use revocation (CELEBRITI, T-538/23), which is not genericide.
- EU Trade Mark Regulation art. 12 lets an owner demand that dictionaries mark an entry as a trade mark. I did not verify this in this session; attorney to confirm.

## A4. Naming: common-word name ("Ethos") vs. a distinctive coined name, recall and search, and companies that changed or extended names

### Takeaway
Real words are easier to recall than invented ones. Lerman & Garbarino (2002) found higher recall for word names, while non-words were marginally better recognised. Whether the word related to the product made no reliable recall difference.

The cost of a common word is not memorability but ownability:
- crowded trademark registers,
- weak search/SEO exclusivity,
- shared meaning with many unrelated "Ethos" brands.

Many startups begin on prefixed domains ("try-", "get-") and later buy the exact-match domain or drop descriptors once they scale.

### Cited Findings
**Recall research**
- Lerman & Garbarino (2002), "Recall and recognition of brand names: a comparison of word and nonword name types," *Psychology & Marketing* 19:621–639:
  - Recall was higher for word names than non-word names.
  - Relevant vs irrelevant word names showed no significant recall difference.
  - Recognition was marginally higher for non-words.
  - Tested one hour after ad exposure — [Bohrium paper record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/recall-and-recognition-of-brand-names-a-comparison-of-word-and-nonword-name-types/812456365703299073-7792); [Operative Words commentary](https://www.operativewords.com/blog/2009/04/real-words-make-better-brand-names.html)

**Registrability of common words in the EU**
- Under EU law, the bar is "a minimum degree of distinctive character." A laudatory meaning alone is not fatal: the General Court reversed the refusal of Oatly's slogan — [Lewis Silkin, Oatly](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2021/01/27/oatly-milks-the-euipo-dry-102gpkf)
- But the EUIPO has applied stricter, dictionary-based laudatory refusals recently (LUX, and IMPRESS, T-326/25, March 2026) — [Novagraaf, IMPRESS](https://www.novagraaf.com/en/insights/eu-general-court-confirms-impress-is-a-laudatory-trademark/)

**Crowding: how many ETHOS owners exist**
- US aggregator listings show many separate ETHOS owners with portfolios: Ethos Group Inc., Ethos Inc., Ethos LLC, Ethos Technologies Inc., Ethos AI Inc. — [trademarkdir: Ethos Group Inc.](https://trademarkdir.com/owner/ethos-group-inc); [Ethos, Inc.](https://trademarkdir.com/owner/ethos-inc); [Ethos, LLC](https://trademarkdir.com/owner/ethos-llc); [ETHOS 99619730 (Ethos Technologies)](https://markinton.com/trademark/ethos-99619730); [ETHOS 98552473 (Ethos AI)](https://markinton.com/trademark/ethos-98552473)

**Domain upgrades as companies outgrow names**
- Prefix and descriptor upgrades:
  - Lolly: TryLolly.com → Lolly.com, once proven.
  - Jobber: GetJobber.com → Jobber.com.
  - Front: FrontApp.com → Front.com.
  - Bubble: Bubble.io → Bubble.com (2021, after 1M users).
  - Chime: ChimeBank.com → Chime.com (Oct 2017).
  - Jamba Juice → Jamba (June 2019, dropped "Juice").
  - Knix Wear → Knix.
  - UrbanCompass → Compass.
  - Sources are domain-industry blogs with an interest in exact-match domains — [Peerlist, famous domain upgrades](https://peerlist.io/expiredauctions/articles/famous-companies-that-upgraded-their-domain-names); [SmartBranding, brand upgrades](https://smartbranding.com/tag/brand-upgrade/)
- Uber bought uber.com and Tesla bought tesla.com (from teslamotors.com) — [Namecheap blog](https://www.namecheap.com/blog/exact-match-domains/); [DomainInvesting](https://domaininvesting.com/secure-your-brand-com)
- A 2019 analysis found 908 of 1,587 funded startups (~57%) held their exact-match brand domain — [SmartBranding, 1,587 funded startups](https://smartbranding.com/we-analyzed-1587-funded-startups-and-their-domain-name-choices/)

### Inferences
- "Ethos" is likely an advantage for recall (real word, short, easy to spell in English and Spanish, where "ethos" is also used).
- It is a disadvantage for:
  - Search: a query for "ethos" will surface Ethos life insurance, the rhetoric term, and many other Ethos brands.
  - Exclusivity: crowded registers mean narrower protection.
  - AI-assistant disambiguation: see A5.
- A distinctive modifier makes the name more ownable while keeping the "Ethos" core: a consistent descriptor ("Ethos Love," "Ethos gifts") or a distinctive device (a specific wax-seal mark).
- The tryethos.io → ethos.(something) upgrade path is common, but ethos.com / ethos.io may be held by others. I did not check.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly compares search or recall performance of common-word vs coined names in the digital era. Lerman & Garbarino is lab-based and from 2002.
- I did not check who owns ethos.com, ethos.io, ethos.love or similar domains.
- No sourced example was found of a company renaming specifically to own a category name, apart from descriptor drops (Jamba, Knix).

## A5. Generative engine optimisation: how brands become the name AI assistants give for a category

### Takeaway
AI answers draw heavily on a small set of third-party sources:
- Wikipedia (especially for ChatGPT),
- Reddit and YouTube (especially Google AI Overviews and Perplexity),
- "best X" listicles.

The strongest 2025 correlates of a brand being named are off-site: YouTube mentions and branded web mentions, not domain authority or backlinks. On-page tactics (citing statistics, adding quotations) raised visibility by up to ~40% in the founding GEO paper. All vendor studies are correlational and fast-changing.

### Cited Findings
**Academic: the founding GEO paper**
- Aggarwal et al., "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (Princeton/IIT Delhi; KDD 2024, Barcelona, 25–29 Aug 2024):
  - Optimisation methods boosted source visibility in generative engine responses by up to 40%.
  - "Quotation Addition" and "Statistics Addition" performed best (~30–40%); stylistic methods gave 15–30%.
  - Up to 37% improvement on live Perplexity.ai.
  - Effects varied by domain — [arXiv 2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735); [Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/geo-generative-engine-optimization/)

**Which domains AI assistants cite**
- Ahrefs (June 2025; ~76.7M AI Overviews, 957K ChatGPT prompts, 953.5K Perplexity prompts):

# PART B: Legal protection in the EU and US for a digital-gift web product

## B1. Trademark routes, 2026 fees, timelines and which Nice classes fit

### Takeaway
For a Latvia-based company selling into the EU, US and UK, the usual sequence is:
1. File an EU trade mark (EUTM) first: €850 for one class, €50 for the second, €150 per class after that (e-filing; fee structure unchanged since 2016 and quoted as current in 2026 sources).
2. Then extend to the US (and UK) within the 6-month Paris Convention priority window. This can be done directly at the USPTO ($350 per class base since 18 Jan 2025) or via Madrid (653 CHF basic fee plus per-country fees).

The core classes are probably 9, 42, 38 and 41, with 35 and 45 optional. An attorney must settle the specification.

## B3. Copyright, EU design rights after the 2024/2025 reform, and trade dress / passing off

### Takeaway
Templates, illustrations, animations, code and copy are protected by copyright automatically in the EU if original (the author's own free and creative choices). Copyright protects the specific expression, not the idea.

The EU design reform (Regulation (EU) 2024/2822, phase 1 from 1 May 2025, phase 2 from 1 July 2026) now explicitly covers animated designs and graphical user interfaces:
- Registration: €350 for one design; €125 each for designs 2–10 in a multiple filing; €50 each from the 11th. Up to 25 years in 5-year terms.
- Unregistered EU design: 3 years, anti-copying only.

In the US:
- Copyright registration is needed for statutory damages and attorney's fees, even for foreign works.
- Trade dress for a website look needs proof of secondary meaning and non-functionality, which is hard.
- UK passing off on "get-up" rarely succeeds.

### Cited Findings
**EU copyright**
- Originality is the only requirement: an original expression of the author's free and creative choices (*Cofemel*, *Brompton*). Aesthetic merit and designer intent are irrelevant — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2020/07/functional-articles-may-be-protected-by-copyright); [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/insights/2020/global/cjeu-rules-that-functional-shapes-are-eligible-for-copyright-protection-in-so-far)
- Where technical constraints leave no room for creative freedom, there is no protection (*Brompton*, citing *SAS Institute*). The CJEU frames this through the idea/expression distinction — [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/insights/2020/global/cjeu-rules-that-functional-shapes-are-eligible-for-copyright-protection-in-so-far); [RPC](https://rpclegal.com/thinking/ip/wheely-good-news-for-product-designers)

**US copyright for a foreign (Latvian) owner**
- Foreign works need not be registered to sue for US infringement.
- But timely registration (before infringement, or within 3 months of publication) is required to claim statutory damages and attorney's fees. Without it, only an injunction and actual damages are available — [Dorsey & Whitney](https://www.dorsey.com/trademark-copyright-advertising/insights/dont-get-berned-an-important-limitation-on-enforcement-of-foreign-copyrights-under-u-s-law); [Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/registration-of-foreign-works-is-required-to-collect-statutory-damages); [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/pretrial-practice-discovery/copyright-registration-basics-litigators/)
- Statutory damages can reach up to $150,000 per registered work for wilful infringement — [Donahue](https://donahue.com/resources/publications/copyrights-registered-u-s/)

**EU design reform**
- Timing: phase 1 applied from 1 May 2025; phase 2 from 1 July 2026. Member states must transpose the Directive (EU) 2024/2823 by 9 December 2027 — [HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2025-05/eu-design-reform-begins-key-provisions-of-eu-regulation-2024-2822-effective-from-1-may); [Dreyfus, phase 2](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/08/20/eu-designs-what-has-changed-since-july-1st-2026-phase-2/); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102nchy/eu-design-reform-what-you-need-to-know)
- Naming: Registered Community Designs became Registered EU Designs; Unregistered Community Designs became Unregistered EU Designs — [Dreyfus guide (Jan 2026)](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/05/eu-design-protection-complete-guide-to-the-2025-reform/)
- Scope:
  - "Design" now covers movement, transitions and animation.
  - "Product" expressly includes graphical user interfaces and non-physical products.
  - Animated representations can be filed as MP4 (one dynamic/animated representation per design) — [Dreyfus guide](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/05/eu-design-protection-complete-guide-to-the-2025-reform/); [Simmons & Simmons, gaming](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cme01ixxz001ovew0gte9jba9/opportunities-for-gaming-following-eu-design-rights-reforms)
- Fees:
  - Application fee €350 for a single design.
  - €125 each for the 2nd–10th design in a multiple application; €50 each from the 11th.
  - Registration and publication fees are merged into one, payable within one month of filing.
  - Up to 50 designs per multiple application — [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102nchy/eu-design-reform-what-you-need-to-know); [Mewburn Ellis](https://www.mewburn.com/forward/eu-design-changes); [DLA Piper (Apr 2026)](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/04/eu-design-reform)
- Renewals:
  - 1st renewal rose from €90 to €150; 2nd from €120 to €250.
  - The pre- or post-1 July 2026 schedule applies according to when the EUIPO receives the renewal request — [HGF](https://www.hgf.com/knowledge-hub/legal-updates/re-defining-design-protection-in-the-european-union-what-it-means-for-your-business/); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/eu-design-law-reform-explained-for-businesses)
- Term:
  - Registered: 5 years, renewable in 5-year terms up to 25 years.
  - Unregistered EU design: 3 years from first disclosure, protecting only against copying.
  - Sources disagree on whether first disclosure must occur in the EU — [Dreyfus guide](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/05/eu-design-protection-complete-guide-to-the-2025-reform/)

**Trade dress and passing off**
- US: product design is never inherently distinctive and needs secondary meaning (*Wal-Mart v. Samara*, 529 U.S. 205 (2000)) — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/99-150)
- Courts have begun to entertain website "look and feel" trade dress. But *Blue Nile v. God's Girls* denied it because the website was not shown to be non-functional and distinctive — [Wikipedia, Trade dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_dress)
- UK passing off: get-up is protectable only if goodwill attaches to it and the public recognises it as distinctive. Very few get-up claims succeed — [Wikipedia, Trade dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_dress)

### Inferences
- **Strongest IP for the "look":**
  1. Copyright in each original illustration, animation, template artwork, copy and code (automatic, free, life + 70 years, which is general knowledge and was not verified this session).
  2. Registered EU designs for the most distinctive, signature screens or animations: the wax-seal opening animation, the envelope reveal, the blush card UI.
     - One filing with up to 10 designs costs €350 + 9 × €125 = **€1,475** in official fees.
     - The 3-year unregistered right is a free backstop, but only against proven copying.
- The EU design deferment option (keeping a filing unpublished for up to 30 months under prior practice) could keep upcoming templates confidential. It was not verified for the post-reform rules. Attorney to confirm.
- For the US, registering key template artwork and animations in batches with the US Copyright Office before launch makes DMCA threats credible (statutory damages and fees). Fee amounts were not verified this session.
- "Trade dress" or passing off for the overall pink, wax-seal aesthetic is a weak, expensive, last-resort tool for a young brand without proven consumer recognition.

### Gaps

  - Current US Copyright Office fees (commonly cited $45 Single / $65 Standard application).
  - Group-registration options for many template artworks.
  - The full EU design phase 2 fee table.
  - Whether the deferment rules changed after the reform.

- No source confirmed how *SAS Institute* (C-406/10) applies to app functionality. My understanding that functionality, programming languages and file formats are not protected is general knowledge and should be confirmed by an attorney.

## B4. Enforcement tools and how well they work for small brands

### Takeaway
For a small brand, platform notice-and-takedown is the workhorse:
- DMCA notices to US-based hosts and platforms.
- DSA Article 16 notices to EU hosting services.
- Etsy's IP portal, Apple's App Store dispute form, Google Play's legal form, and TikTok's copyright and trademark forms.

These are free or cheap and often fast, but they generally require a clear, specific right: copyright in identical assets, or a registered trademark for name misuse. Disputed cases bounce back to the parties. UDRP is efficient for lookalike domains ($1,500 WIPO fee; 79% of WIPO cases ended in transfer in 2025), but needs trademark rights.

### Cited Findings
**DMCA (US)**
- After a valid counter-notice, the provider restores content 10–14 business days later unless the complainant files a US lawsuit.
- Knowing material misrepresentation in a notice or counter-notice creates liability under §512(f) — [Digital Media Law Project](https://www.dmlp.org/node/1594); [Revision Legal](https://revisionlegal.com/copyright-law/dmca-counter-notice/)
- Non-US users may be reluctant to counter-notify, because doing so means accepting US jurisdiction — [Revision Legal](https://revisionlegal.com/copyright-law/dmca-counter-notice/)

**EU Digital Services Act**
- Article 16 requires hosting providers, including online platforms, to offer an electronic notice mechanism for illegal content, including IP infringement.
- Notices must be "sufficiently precise and adequately substantiated," and include the notifier's name and email — [Springlex, DSA Art. 16](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-16/); [Presencis, Art. 16](https://presencis.com/regulations/dsa/article-16/)
- Organised rights-holder bodies can become "trusted flaggers," whose notices get priority — [Parltrack DSA dossier](https://parltrack.org/activities/197467/dossier/2020/0361(COD))
- Online platforms must file statements of reasons for moderation decisions in the public DSA Transparency Database (Art. 24(5)) — [European Commission FAQ](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/dsa-transparency-database-questions-and-answers)
- Brand owners are advised to use it to monitor actions and gather evidence — [JD Supra, Brand protection under the DSA](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/brand-protection-and-enforcement-under-6592639)

**Etsy**
- Reports go through the Etsy Reporting Portal. Etsy aims to remove material quickly when a report complies with its policy, and may share the reporter's name and email with the seller.
- Counter-notices are available for copyright reports only.
- Repeat infringers can lose selling privileges.
- Abusive notices can get the reporter's account terminated — [Etsy IP policy](https://etsy.com/legal/ip/); [Etsy DMCA page](https://www.etsy.com/legal/ip-dmca/); [Etsy Help: counter notice](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360040496854-How-to-File-a-DMCA-Counter-Notice)

**Gumroad**
- Search found only a general "Report a creator" route, where staff review the product against the Terms of Service. No dedicated IP form was found — [Gumroad Help](https://gumroad.com/help/article/226-report-a-creator.html)

**Apple App Store**
- Apple's content dispute form forwards the claim to the app provider and asks the parties to work it out.
- The claimant must swear, under penalty of perjury, that they hold or represent the rights. One dispute can cover up to 50 apps.
- If a dispute isn't resolved or a party doesn't respond, the app may be removed — [Apple App Store Content Dispute](https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/dispute-forms/app-store); [AppleInsider forum](https://forums.appleinsider.com/discussion/238356)

**Google Play and TikTok**
- Google Play: copyright and trademark complaints go through Google's legal-issues process — [Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16341334)
- TikTok:
  - Separate online copyright and trademark report forms; incomplete reports may be denied.
  - TikTok Shop listings use a separate IP system — [Red Points, TikTok copyright](https://www.redpoints.com/blog/copyright-infringement-tiktok/); [TikTok ads IP policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-intellectual-property-infringement)

**UDRP (lookalike domains)**
- WIPO fee: $1,500 for 1–5 domains with a single panelist ($1,000 panel / $500 WIPO).
- New in 2026: an expedited one-month service for $4,000 (1–5 domains, same registrant).
- Withdrawal before notification: WIPO keeps $100 — [WIPO fee schedule](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/domain-name-disputes/fees); [WIPO 2026 fee update](https://www.wipo.int/es/web/amc/w/news-domain-name-disputes/2026/news_0005)
- 2025 caseload: a record 6,282 WIPO cases, most of them .com — [WIPO, 2025 record year](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/w/news-domain-name-disputes/2026/news_0001); [Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/01/15/wipo-handles-record-number-of-domain-disputes-in-2025/)
- 2025 outcomes: 79% transferred, 5% denied, 15% settled or terminated (secondary report) — [Dreyfus](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/28/what-do-wipos-record-2025-statistics-reveal-about-the-surge-in-domain-name-disputes/)

### Inferences
- **What works for Ethos-sized brands:**
  - Copyright takedowns where a competitor has lifted Ethos's actual artwork, animations, copy or code, i.e., near-identical copies. These are the most reliable and need no registration in the EU.
  - On Etsy, Gumroad, TikTok and the App Store, the claimant must identify the specific work and the infringing listing.
- **What's weak:** takedowns against "similar style" or "same concept" listings. Platforms will generally decline, or the seller will counter-notify, and the dispute then needs court action that a small brand will rarely fund.
- **Name misuse** (e.g., a seller calling their product "Ethos letter") requires trademark rights. Platforms strongly prefer registered marks with registration numbers, so registration is what unlocks trademark takedowns.
- UDRP needs (a) trademark rights, (b) a confusingly similar domain, (c) no legitimate interest, and (d) bad-faith registration and use. Against "ethos"-containing domains held by unrelated businesses named Ethos, it will usually fail.
- Practical cost of enforcement: platform forms are free. Most cost is staff time plus occasional attorney letters. Litigation, especially cross-border, is disproportionate except for major, persistent copying.

### Gaps
- No platform publishes success rates for small rights holders. Etsy's only published statistics in the results were from 2014. DSA Transparency Database IP statistics for 2025 were not retrieved.
- Gumroad's specific DMCA/IP procedure was not found.
- Typical time-to-takedown figures per platform were not found from reliable sources.

## B5. What copying cannot legally be stopped

### Takeaway
No IP right stops a competitor from selling animated digital love letters, gift pages delivered by link or QR code, interactive mini-game gifts, couples memberships, or a "Made with X" footer. These are ideas, formats, methods and business models.

What can be stopped:
- copying of Ethos's specific original expression (artwork, animation sequences, text, code),
- registered designs (and, for 3 years, copying of unregistered designs),
- use of a confusingly similar name or logo once registered,
- in narrow cases, passing off or unfair competition.

### Cited Findings
- US: copyright protection does not extend to "any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery" — [17 U.S.C. §102(b), Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102)
- EU: the CJEU frames copyright protection through originality and the idea/expression distinction. Elements dictated purely by technical function, with no creative freedom, are unprotected (*Brompton*, citing *SAS Institute*) — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2020/07/functional-articles-may-be-protected-by-copyright); [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/insights/2020/global/cjeu-rules-that-functional-shapes-are-eligible-for-copyright-protection-in-so-far)
- An unregistered EU design protects only against copying, and only for 3 years. Independent creation is a defence — [Dreyfus guide](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/05/eu-design-protection-complete-guide-to-the-2025-reform/)
- US trade dress for website look and feel fails if it is functional or lacks distinctiveness (*Blue Nile*). Product design always needs secondary meaning (*Wal-Mart*) — [Wikipedia, Trade dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_dress); [Cornell LII, Wal-Mart v. Samara](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/99-150)
- Trademarks protect source identifiers, not product concepts. A generic name for a product type cannot be monopolised (e.g., GRUYERE held generic) — [Justia, Gruyère](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca4/22-1041/22-1041-2023-03-03.html)

### Inferences
- Cannot be stopped:
  - The concept of a personalised, animated digital gift page sent by link or QR.
  - Love-letter, bouquet and mini-game formats in general.
  - Generic motifs such as wax seals, envelopes, hearts and pink palettes in general; only Ethos's specific renderings are protectable.
  - Pricing models, memberships, the "free with footer" growth loop, and selling on Etsy or Gumroad.
  - Common descriptive phrases ("digital love letter").
- Terms of service that forbid copying templates bind only users who accept them (contract). They don't bind third parties who see a gift link or reverse-engineer the look without agreeing. They are useful against customers who resell Ethos templates, not against independent competitors. Attorney to confirm enforceability, including any Latvian or EU consumer-law limits.
- The realistic moat is therefore mostly non-legal: memory (mental availability), distribution, speed of new templates, community and quality. Legal tools work as a floor that stops verbatim theft and name confusion.

### Gaps
- No EU, Latvian, UK or US case law specifically on copying of digital greeting or gift-page formats was found.
- Whether EU unfair-competition law (national, e.g., Latvian unfair competition provisions) offers "slavish imitation" protection beyond IP was not researched.

## Implications for Ethos (interpretation, kept separate from the cited facts above)

*Everything in this section is my interpretation of the findings above, applied to the Ethos context provided. It is not established fact and not legal advice.*

**1. Memory is the main moat; legal rights are the floor.**
The evidence (Golder & Tellis; Lieberman & Montgomery; Ehrenberg-Bass) suggests that being first to digital gift pages won't secure leadership by itself, and nothing legal can stop the concept being copied.
- Ethos's job is to become the brand people think of first at a few chosen category entry points: anniversaries, Valentine's, long-distance, "sorry," last-minute gift.
- Do this by maximising reach with the same assets, co-presented with the name, every time. The free-gift "Made with Ethos" footer is the most valuable reach channel Ethos has, because every recipient is a future buyer in a real gifting moment.
- Keep the footer consistent in wording, logo and seal, and make it link to a page that names the category.

**2. Make one asset truly unique, not just famous.**
Wax seals and envelopes are category signals that competitors already use.
- Pick one ownable execution, e.g., a specific monogrammed seal shape and colour, or the exact "blush" pink with a signature opening animation.
- Use it identically everywhere: site, app icon, Etsy banner, TikTok end-cards, footer.
- Test it unprompted for fame and uniqueness once there is enough audience.
- This same asset is what to register as a figurative trademark and as an EU design.

**3. Encourage the habit, protect the word.**
- Promote "send an Ethos gift," "an Ethos love letter."
- Seed and use a generic term competitors can use too, such as "digital gift page" or "love-letter link," so that "Ethos" never becomes the product's name.
- Use ™ now and ® once registered. Write a short usage guide for Etsy listings, affiliates and press.
- Avoid using "Ethos" as a verb in the company's own copy. Customer slang is lower risk.

**4. Name strategy: keep "Ethos," but disambiguate relentlessly.**
"Ethos" is memorable but crowded: Ethos life insurance, Ethos AI, a dictionary word.
- Always pair it with a descriptor in third-party mentions ("Ethos — digital love letters & gift pages") so both humans and AI assistants resolve the right Ethos.
- The tryethos.io domain is fine for now. Plan to move to a stronger exact-match or descriptor domain as the brand grows (Lolly, Jobber and Bubble all did).
- TRYETHOS as a trademark adds little.

**5. Trademark plan (for an attorney to validate).**
- Commission a professional clearance search first, covering EUIPO/TMview, USPTO and WIPO GBD for ETHOS, ETHOS LOVE and phonetic variants. The EU register could not be checked in this research, and Ethos AI's pending US ETHOS filing (Classes 9/42) is a visible risk.
- If clear, file an EUTM, likely as:
  - ETHOS word mark (or ETHOS LOVE if ETHOS is blocked), and
  - the wax-seal logo as a separate figurative mark,
  - in Classes 9, 38, 41 and 42, with 35 optional.
  - Official fees: ~€1,200 per mark for 4 classes.
- Within 6 months, extend to the US (direct USPTO at $350 per class, using ID Manual wording to avoid the $200 surcharge, or via Madrid) and consider the UK.
- Budget for a possible opposition (€320 fee, if Ethos opposes others) and coexistence negotiations.

**6. Lock down the look cheaply.**
- Keep dated source files proving authorship of every illustration, animation and template (EU copyright is automatic).
- Register an EU multiple design covering up to 10 signature screens/animations: about €1,475 official fees, protection up to 25 years. This includes animations, which the 2025 reform now explicitly covers.
- For the US, register key template artwork with the Copyright Office before or within 3 months of publication, so DMCA notices carry statutory-damages weight.

**7. Enforcement playbook with realistic expectations.**
- Use DMCA / DSA Art. 16 / Etsy / Gumroad / App Store / TikTok forms against verbatim copies of Ethos artwork, animation or copy. These work well and cost little.
- Expect "similar style" competitors at $3–60 on Etsy and Gumroad to be untouchable unless they copy actual assets or use a confusing name.
- Use UDRP ($1,500) only for clear bad-faith lookalike domains, after a trademark registers.
- The template-copying clause in the terms of service helps against customers who resell templates, not against competitors.

**8. Get named by AI assistants.**
- Aim for third-party "best digital love letter / gift page" roundups.
- Build an authentic presence where couples ask for gift ideas (Reddit, TikTok, YouTube demos that name "Ethos" with category words).
- Publish a clear, statistic-rich "what is a digital gift page" explainer.
- Keep entity details consistent across the site, App Store, Etsy and press.
- Wikipedia notability comes later, through press coverage.
