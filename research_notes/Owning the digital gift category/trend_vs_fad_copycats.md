# Trend vs fad: originators that survived copycats and those that lost the trend

Status date: 1 November 2026. Material from 2013–2024 is historical and flagged as such. Research method note: most full-text fetches were blocked in this session (Wikipedia, CNBC, Fast Company, KUTV, Yicai, ANB Media did not resolve), so many findings rest on search-result excerpts of the cited pages rather than full reads. Where a figure is reported inconsistently, both versions are given. The web-search budget ran out before a final round on Labubu's UK store suspension, Lindt's Dubai-chocolate volumes and Trader Joe's launch dates, so those are listed as gaps.

---

## Q1. Case timelines: how long did each originator have before credible copies, what did it do in that window, and where is it now?

### Takeaway
Copy windows ranged from roughly zero (Clubhouse: Twitter Spaces launched before Clubhouse peaked) through about two weeks (Lensa) and five months (BeReal's user collapse) to about a year (Dubai chocolate on supermarket shelves). The originators that are still standing (Pop Mart, Squishmallows, Stanley, Wordle under the NYT) owned a named character or brand that sat on the product itself. The ones that faded (Lensa, Clubhouse, BeReal, Gas, fidget spinners, Rainbow Loom) owned a format, a feature or a mechanism. Even the survivors are now fading or under pressure as of late 2026: Pop Mart Q3 2026 revenue is down 45–50% and Crumbl is closing stores.

## Q2. What separated survivors from fads (speed, network effects, owned IP, brand name vs product description, distribution, community, scarcity, quality)?

### Takeaway
The clearest separator was whether the **thing people asked for by name was a proprietary noun** (Labubu, Squishmallow, Stanley, Wordle) or a **description/format** (Dubai chocolate, magic avatars, live audio rooms, dual-camera photo, fidget spinner, rubber-band loom). Second came owned distribution and a steady stream of new releases (Pop Mart series, Squishmallows' 3,000 styles, Stanley colour drops). Network effects did not protect Clubhouse or BeReal, because the copiers already had larger networks.

### Cited Findings
- Owned characters and IP enable scale and licensing: Squishmallows grew to 400M units across 3,000 named styles — [Wikipedia excerpt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squishmallows) — and launched cross-category licensing at 100M units — [The Licensing Letter](https://www.thelicensingletter.com/squishmallows-hits-100-million-sales-launches-licensing-program/embed). Pop Mart's THE MONSTERS franchise brought in RMB 14.16bn in 2025 — [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/intels/2026-03/25/pop-mart-posts-record-results-as-labubu-reliance-weighs)
- But dependence on a single IP is a risk: Pop Mart shares fell ~22% on record FY2025 results over Labubu dependence — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/pop-mart-drops-22-despite-124356349.html); Q3 2026 revenue fell 45–50% — [SCMP via Biztoc](https://biztoc.com/x/98382d2c70444fa9); Pop Mart now notes that non-Labubu products are half of US sales — [Jiemian](https://en.jiemian.com/article/14572087.html)
- Scarcity as a deliberate tool: Stanley wanted "a little bit of scarcity" — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/stanley-cup-hype-rcna132372). Pop Mart's resale mark-ups once exceeded 2,000% (Nomura) and then collapsed as supply rose — [MarketScreener](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/labubu-resale-price-falls-may-be-more-about-supply-than-demand-ce7d5ddadf8ff22c); [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2025/10/23/pop-mart-shares-slide-9-as-enthusiasm-for-labubu-dolls-shows-signs-of-cooling/)
- Format copies by incumbents with distribution: Clubhouse's format was copied by Twitter, Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn — [ThinkInsights](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/clubhouse-after-boom). BeReal lost ~60% of its users within about five months of its peak — [SearchLogistics](https://www.searchlogistics.com/learn/statistics/bereal-statistics/)
- Low-barrier features attract copycats within days: AI-art apps took 8 of the top 100 US download slots within 11 days of Lensa's peak — [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2022/12/13/ai-art-apps-taking-over-app-store-charts/)
- Quality-first restraint: FIX turned down faster expansion to protect the product — [Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/business/retail/fix-dubai-chocolate-nearly-gave-up) — while big chocolate makers and grocers scaled "Dubai chocolate" — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/31/walmart-shake-shack-trader-joes-dubai-chocolate.html)
- Weekly novelty is not enough on its own: Crumbl's AUV fell from $1.84M (2022) to $1.14M (2025), and 2026 sales collapsed — [Local12](https://local12.com/news/nation-world/crumbl-cookies-shuts-dozens-locations-amid-steep-sales-drop-cincinnati-dessert); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/buying-a-franchise/crumbls-sales-are-crumbling-and-its-ceo-says-its-my-fault)
- Rivals with a better product can dislodge even a well-branded leader: Owala's search growth (+60%) versus Stanley's (-50%) in 2025 — [ASI](https://members.asicentral.com/news/strategy/january-2026/owala-finishes-as-fastest-growing-esp-search-term-for-second-year-in-a-row/)

### Inferences
- **Ranked by how much each protected originators in these cases:** (1) a proprietary name or character that is the product (not a description); (2) owned distribution and a direct customer relationship (Pop Mart stores and app, NYT bundle); (3) a release cadence that keeps the brand, not the format, in the feed (Pop Mart series, Squishmallows names, Stanley colourways); (4) a timely exit (Wordle, Gas, BeReal); (5) legal action, which mostly blocks name and logo copying, not format copying (see Q4); (6) network effects, which were weak because incumbents' networks were bigger.
- **Scarcity cuts both ways.** It amplified Stanley and Labubu while demand was hot. Against fast copies (Dubai chocolate), it simply handed unmet demand to copycats. Scarcity works when the copy is visibly not the real thing (a Labubu logo on the foot); it fails when the copy is "good enough" (pistachio-knafeh chocolate).
- Novelty without a distinct named thing (Crumbl's rotating cookies) wears out once copycats and fatigue arrive.

### Gaps
- No controlled data isolates the effect of each factor; this is case comparison, not causal evidence.
- Owala's own revenue and product strategy (the FreeSip lid) was not researched in depth.

## Q3. When the trend's name became generic ("Dubai chocolate", "magic avatars"), how did that hurt the originator, and could it have been prevented?

### Takeaway
"Dubai chocolate" is the textbook case. The world named the category after a place, not after FIX's product ("Can't Get Knafeh of It") or its brand, so the demand FIX created flowed to anyone who used the phrase. German courts have since restricted "Dubai chocolate" to Dubai-made products, but that protects Dubai origin, not FIX. "Magic avatars" became "AI avatars" within days. In both cases the originator's own name was not the word consumers used.

### Cited Findings
- Alani: "The world named it Dubai chocolate" — [The National, Jul 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/food/2026/07/07/fix-founders-on-creating-the-dubai-chocolate-phenomenon-the-world-named-it/). The product's own name was "Can't Get Knafeh of It" — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2540507/creators-of-viral-dubai-chocolate-call-out-copycats-for-damaging-their-brand?amp=1)
- Wikipedia says most legal scholars consider "Dubai chocolate" a generic term in the EU market rather than a geographical indication — [Wikipedia excerpt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_chocolate)
- German courts split, then the appeal court sided with origin. Cologne Regional Court granted injunctions; Frankfurt Regional Court refused one against Lidl, holding that the term alone did not imply origin — [Harte-Bavendamm](https://www.harte-bavendamm.de/en/ip-blog/higher-regional-court-of-cologne-provides-clarity-dubai-chocolate-remains-an-indication-of-origin). The **Higher Regional Court of Cologne, four judgments of 27 June 2025 (6 U 52/25, 6 U 53/25, 6 U 58/25, 6 U 60/25)**, held that "Dubai Chocolate" suggests an incorrect origin when used on non-Dubai products, and that "viral popularity and extensive media coverage do not automatically turn a protected designation into a generic term" — [Harte-Bavendamm](https://www.harte-bavendamm.de/en/ip-blog/higher-regional-court-of-cologne-provides-clarity-dubai-chocolate-remains-an-indication-of-origin); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=a4338266-a82d-48cb-b4ea-7f52b9c0d8e4); [Jüdemann](https://ra-juedemann.de/en/dubai-chocolate-must-originate-from-dubai-olg-koeln-prohibits-misleading-indication-of-origin/); [Yahoo/AFP "German court flips"](https://www.yahoo.com/news/german-court-flips-dubai-chocolate-201223922.html). The claimant was Andreas Wilmers, an importer of chocolate genuinely made in Dubai; sources do not describe him as FIX's importer — [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/german-court-flips-dubai-chocolate-201223922.html). A separate report says an importer took Lindt to court — [Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.de/en/page/dubai-chocolate-importer-takes-lindt-to-court-35702)
- Market adaptation: Lindt changed its pack from "Dubai Chocolate" to "Dubai-Style Chocolate" — [UBC IP blog](https://iplaw.allard.ubc.ca/2025/04/19/sweet-deception-the-intellectual-property-implications-of-dubai-chocolate/). German lawyers advise using "Dubai style" to stay safe — [Harte-Bavendamm](https://www.harte-bavendamm.de/en/ip-blog/higher-regional-court-of-cologne-provides-clarity-dubai-chocolate-remains-an-indication-of-origin); warning letters have been sent over the term — [marken.legal](https://marken.legal/en/warning-letter-dubai-chocolate/)
- Trademark land-grab by others: Canadian applications for "Flair Dubai Chocolate" and "Lindt Dubai Chocolate" were filed, neither by FIX — [UBC IP blog](https://iplaw.allard.ubc.ca/2025/04/19/sweet-deception-the-intellectual-property-implications-of-dubai-chocolate/). One source claims the UAE is not part of the international trademark treaty; this appears doubtful and is unverified here (the UAE is generally reported to have joined the Madrid system in 2021, which was not confirmed in this session)
- Brand-name hijack: impostor FIX sites outrank or sit alongside the real brand in Google, and a £56 "Fix" bar appeared on Etsy — [The National, Sep 2025](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/09/15/dubai-chocolatier-condemns-europes-counterfeit-knafeh-craze); [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/fixdessertchocolatiers.com)
- "Magic avatars": rivals keyword-stuffed app names ("AI avatars") to rank for Lensa's demand — [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2022/12/13/ai-art-apps-taking-over-app-store-charts/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/01/lensa-ai-climbs-the-app-store-charts-as-its-magic-avatars-go-viral)
- By contrast, Wordle's name stayed with its owner: Apple removed clones that used "Wordle" in their name — [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/wordle-new-york-times/)

### Inferences
- **How the generic name hurt FIX:** (a) Retailers with distribution captured the volume (Lindt, Aldi, Lidl, Walmart, Trader Joe's). (b) Search demand for "Dubai chocolate" lands on whoever ranks, including impostor FIX sites. (c) Even FIX's own legal win in spirit (Cologne) protects only "made in Dubai", so a Dubai-made rival is fine. FIX got the cultural credit ("the original") but not the economics.
- **Could it have been prevented?** Partly. FIX could have (1) given the product a short, sayable, ownable name, since "Can't Get Knafeh of It" is a pun and not a handle people repeat; (2) put the FIX name on the product in every viral frame; (3) filed trademarks early in key markets (EU, UK, US) for FIX and a coined product name; (4) secured lookalike domains and an official web store so search had somewhere to land; (5) licensed or co-branded with a large maker ("FIX x …") to ride the retail wave instead of watching it. It could not have stopped the recipe from being copied: recipes and formats are not protectable by trademark.
- The lesson for any trend originator: **the word the audience uses is the asset**. If that word is a description, place or format, the originator has no claim to it.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of FIX filing trademarks for "FIX" or "Dubai chocolate" in the EU/US, or of FIX itself suing anyone. A Fix-styled blog claims the "official German importer of Fix" sued Aldi, Lidl and Lindt, but that site appears unofficial and the court reports name a different importer.
- The final outcome of the Lindt/Lidl proceedings beyond the Cologne appeal judgments was not confirmed.

## Q4. What legal actions did originators take against copycats, and with what results?

### Takeaway
Lawsuits against *format* copying (Crumbl vs Dirty Dough/Crave; Rainbow Loom patent suits; Squishmallows vs Build-A-Bear) mostly ended in settlements, dismissals or narrowed patents, never in a clear knockout. Actions against **counterfeits that copy the name, logo or character** (Pop Mart vs 7-Eleven and Lafufu sellers; Apple removing name-using Wordle clones) worked much better.

WeCool Toys over the '631 patent — [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/9810974/choons-design-llc-v-wecool-toys-inc/); Choon's voluntarily dismissed its Anhetoy case in September 2025 — [Patsnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/litigation/choons-design-v-anhetoy-rubber-band-loom-patent-dispute-patsnap/). Meanwhile sales fell to ~1/10 of peak by 2016 — [Wikipedia excerpt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Loom)
- **Dubai chocolate:** the Cologne appeal court's origin rulings (27 June 2025) were won by an importer of Dubai-made chocolate against Aldi Süd and others, not by FIX — [Harte-Bavendamm](https://www.harte-bavendamm.de/en/ip-blog/higher-regional-court-of-cologne-provides-clarity-dubai-chocolate-remains-an-indication-of-origin); [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/german-court-flips-dubai-chocolate-201223922.html)
- **Stanley:** it is itself a defendant, in lead-seal class actions and in Stanley Black & Decker's 2025 trademark suit, which forced the "Stanley 1913" re-brand — [The Fashion Law](https://thefashionlaw.com/?p=100914); [Wikipedia excerpt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_(drinkware_company))
- **Wordle:** Apple removed clones that used the Wordle name — [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/wordle-new-york-times/)

### Inferences
- **Legal action works on identity, not on ideas.** Trademark and copyright stop look-alike *names, logos and characters* (Labubu, Wordle). Trade dress against a similar-looking product (Squishmallows) or trade secrets against a similar business (Crumbl) is slow, costly and usually settles. Patents on simple consumer mechanisms (Rainbow Loom) get challenged and narrowed, and the fad is over before the case is.
- Legal action did not prevent decline in any case. It protected margin and brand integrity, but it did not create demand.

### Gaps
- No final Federal Circuit/jury outcome for Rainbow Loom was confirmed (one aggregator claims an appellate win; unverified). Settlement terms for Crumbl and Squishmallows are undisclosed. The final judgment and damages in Pop Mart v. 7-Eleven were not found.

## Q5. What did those that survived do to make the brand, not the format, the thing people remember?

### Takeaway
Survivors (1) made a proprietary name or character the object of desire; (2) printed it on the product (the logo on Labubu's foot, "Stanley" on the cup, the Wordle name and grid); (3) multiplied the brand into a collectible range with names and drops; (4) owned the sales channel and the customer relationship; and (5) used collaborations and celebrities to raise the brand's status above the format.

### Cited Findings
- Pop Mart: named characters in blind-box series, with authenticity cues built into the product (nine teeth, peach face, logo on the foot) and anti-piracy packaging — [IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/pop-mart-intensifies-anti-counterfeiting-efforts-labubu-products-fakes-the-rise-and-prices-decline); [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-labubu-craze-cooling-counterfeit-lafufu). Plush became Pop Mart's largest category (RMB 18.71bn, 2025) — [Jing Daily](https://jingdaily.com/intels/2026-03/25/pop-mart-posts-record-results-as-labubu-reliance-weighs). Celebrity-adjacent status ("Labubu with Hermès bag") — [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-06-18/labubu-with-herms-bag-why-are-chinese-designer-toys-taking-the-world-by-storm-going-global-consumer-102331852.html)
- Squishmallows: thousands of named characters (3,000 styles), licensing and big-brand collaborations (McDonald's, PUMA), and new formats for new segments (Squish-a-longs) — [Wikipedia excerpt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squishmallows); [Licensing Source Book 2024](https://issuu.com/maxpublishing/docs/squishmallows_brochure_2024)
- Stanley: colourway drops, Starbucks/Target collaborations, and built-in scarcity — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/stanley-cup-hype-rcna132372); [NBC LA](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/business/money-report/the-limited-edition-stanley-x-starbucks-quencher-is-now-selling-for-hundreds-on-ebay/3304758/); [Frontier Group](https://frontiergroup.org/articles/while-supplies-last-how-marketers-turned-the-humble-reusable-cup-into-a-status-symbol/)
- Wordle: the brand moved into a larger owned ecosystem (NYT Crossword app) and spawned sibling games (Connections) — [Wikipedia excerpt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2591360)
- FIX: kept making brand-led novelty (new named bars, a royal collaboration) and leaned on its "original" status and community — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/dubai-chocolate-queen-sarah-hamouda-launches-new-flavours-and-why-fans-are-going-nuts-over-them-1.500402827); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Fsheikh-hamdan-collaborates-with-viral-dubai-chocolate-brand-for-an-exclusive-flavour-emirati-halwa-1.1726305959352); [Inc. Arabia](https://en.incarabia.com/the-sweet-spot-how-fix-dessert-chocolatier-cofounder-sarah-hammouda-continues-to-build-the-brand-on-her-own-terms-812137.html)
- BeReal under Voodoo: monetising through ads (US, April 2025) and a creator programme rather than relying on format novelty — [SearchLogistics](https://www.searchlogistics.com/learn/statistics/bereal-statistics/); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/marketing/news/bereal-launches-creator-program-to-lure-influencers-and-advertisers-46b4d1fc-2296-4c31-a686-dd21d1066957/)

### Inferences
- The common move is to **make each unit of the product carry the brand into the world**: a cup with "Stanley" on it, a doll with a logo on its foot, a shared Wordle grid that names the game. The product is the ad, and the copy is visibly not the thing.
- **The range beats the single hit.** Survivors turned one viral item into a family of named variants (series, colourways, characters). This keeps the brand in the feed after the format is copied, and gives fans something to collect that copycats do not have.
- Even survivors fade (Pop Mart Q3 2026, Stanley searches -50%). Brand protects margin and buys time; it does not guarantee permanence. A second hit (Pop Mart's non-Labubu IPs, NYT's Connections) is what turns a moment into a company.

### Gaps
- Little direct founder testimony was retrieved on *deliberate* brand-over-format decisions (e.g., Pop Mart's internal strategy, or Stanley's post-Reilly plan).

## Implications for Ethos (interpretation only, not sourced facts)

### Takeaway
Ethos's current viral pattern (brand not on screen, comments begging "PLEASE TELL ME THE WEBSITE", a descriptive trend name "digital gift link") most resembles FIX/Dubai chocolate and Lensa's "magic avatars". It is the failure mode, not the survival mode. Copies should be expected within weeks, not months, because a gift web page is easier to build than a chocolate bar or an AI avatar pipeline, and Etsy/Gumroad sellers and "Letters by Heart" already exist. The goal is not to stop copying, which cannot be done, but to make "Ethos" the word people use and the name every recipient sees.

### Inferences
- **Make every gift an ad for the brand, like Stanley's cup or Labubu's foot logo.** Every gift page a recipient opens should carry a tasteful, persistent Ethos mark and "make your own" path, and the link itself should read as Ethos (tryethos.io/…). Recipients are the distribution. This is the one channel TikTok cannot limit.
- **Own the word, not the format.** "Digital gift" is a description that will go generic, like "Dubai chocolate" or "AI avatars". Consider a coined, sayable product noun (for example, an "Ethos" plus noun or a distinctive term for the format) and use it consistently in captions, on-screen chat text, comments and the site, so that the trend's name points at Ethos. Register EU (EUIPO) and US/UK trademarks for the brand and coined term now. "Ethos" is a common word and likely crowded in some classes, so a clearance check matters. Trademark is the IP that actually worked in these cases (Wordle clone removals, Pop Mart vs 7-Eleven); format, trade-dress and trade-secret suits did not.
- **Put the brand in the story without tripping TikTok's limit.** The fictional chat already shows someone sending a link. Making the URL or the link preview card (Open Graph image and title) visibly Ethos inside the chat is more native than an overlay logo (untested assumption; A/B it). Also pin a comment, use the profile link, and reply to "tell me the website" comments with video replies. Each unanswered comment is demand leaking to whoever ranks for "digital gift link".
- **Secure the landing spots before copycats do.** FIX lost its own name to impostor sites and Etsy listings. Ethos should register lookalike domains, the obvious handles on every platform, and own search results for its name plus the likely trend phrases ("digital gift link", "love letter website", the coined noun). Watch Etsy/Gumroad for listings that use the Ethos name and file takedowns, as Apple's removal of "Wordle"-named clones shows platforms act on names.
- **Release cadence as a moat.** Pop Mart series, Squishmallows' named characters and Stanley's colourways kept the brand ahead of copies. Ethos's 54 templates are an asset: name them like characters, drop new ones on a visible schedule (seasonal, limited-time or "drop" templates), and let the TikTok carousels showcase new drops so the brand, not the generic format, is the news. Crumbl is the warning: rotation without a distinct, recognisable brand experience wears out.
- **Protect what is protectable.** Template art, animations and copy are covered by copyright (as Pop Mart's character copyright was), and in the EU unregistered design rights may cover a template's look. These will not stop a competitor building "a gift page", but they will stop pixel-level clones.
- **Expect the window to be short, and plan for the exit option.** Lensa had about two weeks; Wordle sold within weeks; Gas sold in five months; BeReal sold after a 60% drop for €500M. If the format is attractive to a big platform (greeting-card, messaging or link-in-bio companies), the peak-attention period is also the best moment to talk to them.
- **Scarcity is the wrong lever for Ethos.** In these cases scarcity amplified demand only when the copy was visibly not the real thing. For a digital product that a copycat can reproduce "good enough" overnight, restricting access hands demand to rivals, as with FIX.
- **Measure brand capture, not views.** Track the share of comments, searches and direct visits that use "Ethos" (or the coined noun) versus generic phrases. If 1.3M-view posts produce mostly "what's the website?" and few branded searches, the trend is building the category for everyone.
