# Product-carried virality: how shared artifacts carried the brand, and how brands became the word

*Research date: 1 November 2026. Method note for the report writer: WebFetch was blocked for several key hosts (techcrunch.com, tiffany.com, rosetta.to), and the session's web-search budget ran out before a few last lookups (Spotify Wrapped download uplift, the Wrapped 2024 AI backlash, Linktree vs Instagram in 2018, Lensa). Most findings below therefore come from search-result summaries and quoted snippets, not full-page reads. Figures marked "secondary" come from blogs or aggregators, not company or primary sources. Hotmail (1996–97), Dropbox (2008–10), Venmo's public feed (before 2021) and Calendly's 2021 numbers are historical.*

---

## 1. How exactly did the artifact carry the brand, and which forms worked best (footer, watermark, URL, visual style, packaging)?

### Takeaway
Each case attached the brand to something the user *had* to send for the product to work: an email, a booking link, a bio URL, a payment note, a share card, a box. The most durable forms were (a) brand-bearing URLs and end-of-flow calls to action that the recipient meets while getting value, and (b) visual assets distinctive enough to be recognised without the name (Polaroid's frame, Tiffany's blue, Wrapped's story cards). No controlled study comparing a watermark, a tasteful maker's mark and design-only recognition was found. The only hard evidence on watermarks cuts against them: Instagram demotes visibly watermarked video.

### Cited Findings

**Hotmail: text footer appended to every email (historical, 1996–97)**
- Hotmail went live on 4 July 1996, founded by Sabeer Bhatia and Jack Smith — [Microsoft News Center, 1999 release](https://news.microsoft.com/?p=12288); [Wikipedia: Outlook.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook.com)
- The footer is usually quoted as "PS: I love you. Get your free email at Hotmail". It was a one-line tagline auto-appended to every outgoing message, with a link to sign up — [TechCrunch, 18 Oct 2009](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail)
- Investor Tim Draper is credited with pushing the founders to put a tagline in each message, and the founders at first resisted. Co-founder Jack Smith later disputed the attribution. TechCrunch's review concluded that the evidence indicates Draper had the idea — [TechCrunch, 18 Oct 2009](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail); [TechCrunch, 23 Feb 2009](https://techcrunch.com/2009/02/23/founders-at-work-uncovering-the-truth-behind-a-hotmail-founders-claims/)
- The mechanism: each email reached a new person, was sent by a friend (an implicit endorsement), and ended with a free offer and a link. Secondary accounts describe a "hockey stick" curve soon after the footer was adopted — [themarketingmillennials](https://themarketingmillennials.com/articles/2023-01-09/the-unknown-story-of-how-hotmail-grew-to-12-million-users-in-1-5-years/); [fasterthannormal](https://fasterthannormal.co/businesses/hotmail) (secondary)

**Venmo: the payment note and the social feed**
- Every payment or request needs a note, in text or emoji, saying what it is for. Transaction details *without the amount* went to a news feed seen by the user's network — [Wikipedia: Venmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venmo)
- In 2016 around 30% of approved Venmo transactions included at least one emoji (figure footnoted on Wikipedia, not verified against the primary source) — [Wikipedia: Venmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venmo)
- Transactions were public by default, and Privacy International reported that setup did not make it clear how to change this — [Privacy International](https://www.privacyinternational.org/examples/2692/venmo-app-default-public-setting-exposes-transaction-histories); [Axios, 22 Jul 2018](https://www.axios.com/2018/07/22/venmo-privacy-policy-user-financial-transactions)
- The founders were Andrew Kortina and Iqram Magdon-Ismail. The name combines Latin "vendere" (to sell) with "mo" (mobile) — [Wikipedia: Venmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venmo)
- Secondary analyses credit word of mouth among college students, driven by the feed and by group-expense splitting, as the low-cost acquisition channel — [AppSamurai](https://appsamurai.com/blog/mobile-app-success-story-how-venmo-did-it/) (secondary)

**Calendly: the booking link and booking page**
- Every booking needs two people, so every link sent shows the product to a recipient, who uses it to book — [OpenView](https://openviewpartners.com/blog/saas-product-viral-loop/)
- When branding is on, Calendly's banner shows on booking pages, routing forms, the confirmation-page email field and the invitee email — [Calendly Help Center](https://help.calendly.com/hc/en-us/articles/35448706343319-How-to-turn-off-Calendly-branding-on-your-scheduling-page)
- The default URL format (calendly.com/username) also carries the brand on every link (based on the product's URL structure; no source counts URL-driven acquisition separately).

**Linktree: the bio URL and the page footer**
- Free pages carry a Linktree footer and a Linktree logo on the page's QR code — [Linktree Help Center](https://intercom.help/linktree-ff524ba1864c/en/articles/5434182-how-to-remove-the-linktree-logo)
- The founders are Alex Zaccaria, Anthony Zaccaria and Nick Humphreys. It started as a side project of their agency, Bolster — [Wikipedia: Linktree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linktree)

**Canva: the output is not watermarked; the paywall is**
- Canva's free designs are not marked as such. Instead, *premium* elements show criss-cross watermarks until they are bought or the user upgrades to Pro or Teams — [Canva Help: watermarks](https://www.canva.com/help/watermarks-design/)
- A third-party guide says free Canva websites carry a "Designed with Canva" badge that Pro removes. No official Canva page confirming this was found — [Krumzi](https://www.krumzi.com/blog/canva-watermark-explained) (secondary)

**Spotify Wrapped: design-led share cards, timed once a year**
- It began in 2015 as "Year in Music", with personal shareable cards, and was rebranded "Wrapped" in December 2016. It has run every December since — [Wikipedia: Spotify Wrapped (via Wikiwand)](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Spotify_Wrapped); [Uproxx](https://uproxx.com/music/how-long-has-spotify-wrapped-been-around)
- In 2019 Wrapped moved to a format modelled on social-media Stories, called the most pivotal change in the feature's history — [SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1731101/spotify-wrapped-history-untold-story-creator-no-credit)
- Spotify's own count of 2025 shares includes in-app native shares, screenshots and downloads. Screenshots are counted as shares, so the artifact travels even without a share button — [TechCrunch, 4 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-wrapped-campaign-hit-200m-engaged-users-in-24-hours-a-19-yoy-increase)
- Duolingo copied the format: its Year in Review started as an email in 2019 and moved in-app in 2020. The team said that "condensing your Duolingo stats on a shareable card that worked like an image would be the most efficient" — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes)

**Polaroid: the white frame as the brand**
- Polaroid's US registrations describe its "Border Logo" as "one rectangle inside another with a larger border below the inner rectangle" — [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/cameras/instant-cameras/how-iconic-is-polaroids-square-format-courts-to-decide-as-years-long-dispute-between-camera-makers-heats-up)
- EU: the EUIPO mark is a rectangular border around a grey area, with the inside not claimed. In cancellation proceedings Polaroid conceded the marks were "a rather simple geometric form" but argued the public "associate them with Polaroid", which is a claim of acquired distinctiveness — [Kilburn & Strode](https://www.kilburnstrode.com/Knowledge/Technical-Tips/EUIPO-Polaroid-trade-marks)
- Japan: the JPO examiner refused the Polaroid frame mark for lacking inherent distinctiveness. The Appeal Board reversed this and granted it in class 40 (photo printing) — [Marks IP Law blog](https://blog.marks-iplaw.jp/2018/08/26/is-polaroid-photo-frame-trademarked/)

**Tiffany & Co.: the package is the brand inside the gift**
- Tiffany was founded in 1837 by Charles Lewis Tiffany and John B. Young. The shade is a private custom Pantone colour (PMS 1837, named for the founding year). Tiffany registered it as a colour trademark in 1998 — [Wikipedia: Tiffany Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_Blue); [CNN Style](https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/tiffany--co-blue)
- Sources conflict on when the colour was standardised with Pantone (CNN gives 2001) and on its origin: the 1845 Blue Book catalogue cover versus the box packaging of the 1886 engagement ring — [CNN Style](https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/tiffany--co-blue); [Wikipedia: Tiffany Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_Blue)
- Charles Lewis Tiffany reportedly refused to sell the boxes on their own, so a box could only be had with a purchase — [Tiffany: Blue Box story](https://www.tiffany.com/world-of-tiffany/blue-box-story/); [CNN Style](https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/tiffany--co-blue)
- In Canada a non-traditional registration protects the blue-and-white combination of box and ribbon. Protection covers the colour as applied to packaging, not robin's-egg blue in general — [BCF case study](https://www.bcf.ca/en/thought-leadership/case-studies/tiffany-and-co-s-blue-box-story-of-an-iconic-trademark/); [Fashion Law Journal](https://fashionlawjournal.com/the-story-of-the-iconic-tiffanys-blue-color/)

**Typeform, Loom, Shopify, Substack, CapCut, Dropbox: footers, end screens and outros**
- Typeform shows "Powered by Typeform" on the loading screen and a "Create a typeform" button after submission on Free and Basic accounts — [Typeform Help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029262372)
- Loom: viewers of a shared video see Loom branding, and a "Make a Loom" call to action at the end pushes viewers to become creators — [Product Fruits](https://productfruits.substack.com/p/how-loom-went-from-zero-to-being); [GrowthDeck](https://growthdeck.beehiiv.com/p/loom) (secondary)
- Shopify adds a "Powered by Shopify" footer link to themes by default (via `{{ powered_by_link }}`). Merchants can delete it for free in the theme editor — [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/how-can-i-remove-the-powered-by-shopify-link-from-my-stores-footer/301716); [EcommerceCEO](https://www.ecommerceceo.com/how-to-remove-powered-by-shopify/) (secondary)
- CapCut's watermark normally comes from a template's ending clip (outro) or a logo layer built into the template. The official fix is to delete the outro segment before export — [CapCut Help](https://www.capcut.com/help/remove-watermark-when-exporting-video)
- Dropbox (historical, 2008–10) used a two-sided referral reward of extra storage, modelled on PayPal's cash refer-a-friend scheme — [Viral Loops](https://viral-loops.com/blog/dropbox-grew-3900-simple-referral-program/); [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/blog/dropbox-referral-program/) (secondary)

**Evidence on watermarks themselves**
- In February 2021 Instagram changed Reels ranking to make content "visibly recycled from other apps (i.e. contains logos or watermarks)" less discoverable in the Reels tab and Explore. It cited surveys showing a "less satisfying" experience. Such posts still reached followers — [Tubefilter, 10 Feb 2021](https://tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-will-now-limit-the-reach-of-re-posts-from-tiktok-within-its-reels/594803)

### Inferences
- The forms that scaled share three traits. The brand sits *where the recipient receives value* (the booking page, the inbox, the bio click). The recipient is one action from becoming a user (sign-up link, "Make a Loom", "Create a typeform"). And the sender cannot easily strip it on the free tier.
- Design-only recognition (Polaroid frame, Tiffany blue, Wrapped cards) is the most robust to removal and to platform penalties, because nothing can be cropped out. It takes years of consistent use, though, and is legally fragile: Polaroid has spent nearly a decade defending a "simple geometric form".
- Overt watermarks carry a growing distribution penalty on social platforms (Instagram 2021). A maker's mark inside the artifact, or a share card designed to be screenshotted, avoids that penalty in a way a corner logo does not.
- Tiffany's rule that boxes could not be bought alone shows packaging that signals *authentic purchase*. The brand adds value to the gift instead of advertising on it, the opposite of Hotmail's footer.

### Gaps
- No A/B test or study was found comparing a watermark, a subtle maker's mark and design-only recognition for acquisition.
- The exact Hotmail footer at launch is disputed. One secondary account says the founders first left out "PS: I love you". I could not check this against a primary source.
- The New York Sun's 1906 description of the Tiffany box and Tiffany's official box history were not retrieved (tiffany.com was blocked).

## 2. Did the company deliberately push its name as a noun or verb, or did it happen organically? What was the exact copy?

### Takeaway
In most cases the verb or generic use emerged organically from heavy daily use ("Venmo me", "I have a 9am Zoom", "link in bio", "my Wrapped"). What companies controlled deliberately was the *call to action inside the artifact*, which gave people the word: "Get your free email at Hotmail", "Create a typeform", "Make a Loom". Dictionary recognition followed category dominance (Zoom). Trademark lawyers judged genericide risk to be real but manageable.

### Cited Findings
- **Hotmail.** The copy, "PS: I love you. Get your free email at Hotmail", used the brand as a *place* ("at Hotmail") with a direct imperative ("Get"), not as a verb — [TechCrunch, 2009](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail)
- **Venmo.** "Just Venmo me" is described in commentary as how a generation moves money. No source tied the phrase to a Venmo marketing campaign, so it appears to be organic — [yespress: Venmo](https://yespress.io/venmo) (low-quality source; treat as cultural observation only)
- **Zoom (2020).** Commentators noted "Zoom" used as an adjective ("I'm hosting a Zoom trivia night"), a verb ("I'm Zooming") and a noun ("I have a 9 am Zoom") — [Mondaq, 2021](https://mondaq.com/canada/trademark/1085802/zooming-in-on-generic-trademarks)
- **Zoom.** Merriam-Webster now carries a separate verb entry for Zoom: "to use the Zoom service to hold or participate in online meetings with enabled video support". Its etymology calls it a derivative of the trademarked name. When the entry was added was not found — [Merriam-Webster](https://merriam-webster.com/dictionary/zoom)
- **Zoom genericide debate.** Law360 quoted trademark professor Alexandra J. Roberts: "This sudden popularity and market dominance can be how it starts", but also "Not every story of genericide proceeds along the same path" — [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/1277334/as-zoom-booms-is-its-trademark-at-risk-of-genericide)
- **Zoom.** Under the precedent from the Google trademark case, verb use alone does not make a mark generic if it still identifies the source — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/zoom-verge-becoming-generic-mark)
- **Zoom's defences.** Zoom applied for further word marks (ZOOM ROOMS, ONZOOM, ZOOM FOR YOU) and for its blue logo — [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=2800537)
- **Typeform.** The end-of-form button reads "Create a typeform", using the brand as a lowercase common noun in the product's own UI — [Typeform Help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029262372)
- **Loom.** The call to action reads "Make a Loom", the brand as a countable noun for the artifact itself — [Product Fruits](https://productfruits.substack.com/p/how-loom-went-from-zero-to-being) (secondary)
- **Linktree.** "Link in bio" became the category phrase. Coverage in 2023 framed Linktree (then "over 35 million registered users") as the default — [Allwork.space, Apr 2023](https://allwork.space/2023/04/digest-instagram-threatens-linktree-by-giving-users-the-ability-to-add-additional-links-to-bios/)
- **Spotify Wrapped.** Secondary sources describe users sharing results without prompting, which makes December a recurring viral event — [Parle Magazine, Dec 2025](https://parlemag.com/2025/12/how-spotify-wrapped-became-biggest-free-marketing-campaign) (secondary)
- **Canva.** A search found no dictionary entry or reputable source documenting "Canva" as a verb ("I'll Canva it"). A Perkins quote ("Design should be a verb...") appears only on one unreliable site — see Gaps.

### Inferences
- The pattern behind the verbs: a short, distinctive, pronounceable name (Venmo, Zoom, Canva) used dozens of times inside a social exchange. Generic English words (Ethos is one) rarely become verbs, because they are ambiguous in speech and in search.
- The deliberate lever that is documented is naming the artifact in the call to action ("a typeform", "a Loom"). That teaches recipients a noun for the thing they just received, which is a precondition for "send me a ___".
- Two neighbouring facts suggest the "Wrapped" name worked as a product noun ("my Wrapped") more than as a verb. Rivals avoided the word (Apple uses "Replay"), while Duolingo's blog describes its own copy as "a shareable card".

### Gaps
- No primary source on whether Venmo ever used "Venmo me" in its own copy or ads.
- When Merriam-Webster added the Zoom verb sense, and whether Oxford has done the same, was not verified.
- No reliable evidence that "Canva" is used as a verb.
- No primary source on whether Linktree deliberately promoted "link in bio" as a phrase. The 2018 episode in which Instagram flagged Linktree links could not be researched (search budget exhausted).

## 3. How did paid tiers that remove branding work, what did removal cost in exposure, and what stayed even on paid tiers?

### Takeaway
Removing branding is a standard paid feature in prosumer SaaS: Calendly, Linktree, Typeform and CapCut all charge for it. Usually it sits at a mid or upper tier, not the cheapest. Shopify is the notable counter-example, letting anyone delete the footer for free. Companies keep exposure on paid tiers through other channels: brand URLs (calendly.com/…, linktr.ee/…), app-only flows (Venmo), and native share cards (Wrapped). No company was found publishing the exposure cost of removal.

### Cited Findings
- **Calendly.** Branding can be turned off only on Standard, Teams, Enterprise and the legacy Professional plan. Free plans cannot remove it. Popup embeds also need `branding: false` in the embed code, otherwise the banner stays — [Calendly Help Center](https://help.calendly.com/hc/en-us/articles/35448706343319-How-to-turn-off-Calendly-branding-on-your-scheduling-page); [Calendly Community](https://community.calendly.com/asked-answered-79/disabling-the-powered-by-calendly-banner-4970)
- **Linktree.** The footer logo and the QR-code logo can be removed only on Pro or Premium — [Linktree Help Center](https://intercom.help/linktree-ff524ba1864c/en/articles/5434182-how-to-remove-the-linktree-logo)
- **Linktree pricing (third-party, conflicting).** One source says branding removal needs Pro at $12/month billed annually or $15 monthly, and that the cheaper Starter tier does not remove it — [UniLink](https://app.unilink.us/blog/linktree-pricing-2026). Another says Starter does — [Linkie](https://linkie.bio/blog/is-linktree-free). Both are competitors.
- **Linktree.** The URL stays linktr.ee/… on every plan, so the brand travels in the bio link even when the footer is gone (based on the product's URL structure; not separately sourced).
- **Typeform.** Removing branding needs Plus or higher. Basic, although paid, keeps both "Powered by Typeform" and the post-submission "Create a typeform" button. Removing branding also hides that sign-up button — [Typeform Help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029262372)
- **Typeform pricing (third-party, conflicting).** Basic is about $28/month billed yearly. Plus is about $56/month billed yearly ($79 monthly) per [Splitforms](https://splitforms.com/reviews/typeform), or $59/month per [DEV Community](https://dev.to/allenarduino/how-to-remove-form-builder-branding-without-paying-59month-2e69)
- **Typeform.** Community threads show friction over branding-driven upgrades, for example a thread titled "being forced to upgrade, unhappy" — [Typeform Community](https://community.typeform.com/share-your-typeform-6/being-forced-to-upgrade-unhappy-6689)
- **Shopify.** Shopify does not prohibit removing "Powered by Shopify", and it can be done without a plan change. Some guides argue it adds trust and recommend keeping it, and removing it does not affect SEO — [EcommerceCEO](https://www.ecommerceceo.com/how-to-remove-powered-by-shopify/); [Nudgify](https://www.nudgify.com/how-to-remove-powered-by-shopify/) (secondary)
- **CapCut.** The template outro watermark can be deleted on the timeline. For template-embedded logos, CapCut's help points to "the no-watermark export path shown by the current template and account", and says cropping is not an equivalent fix — [CapCut Help](https://www.capcut.com/help/remove-watermark-when-exporting-video); [CapCut Help: export without watermark](https://www.capcut.com/help/video-without-watermark)
- **Canva.** Canva does not charge to remove a mark from the user's own output. It charges to remove watermarks on premium *stock* elements (Pro, or per-element purchase; one guide cites about $1 per element) — [Canva Help](https://www.canva.com/help/watermarks-design/); [Canva Help: premium elements](https://www.canva.com/en_in/help/premium-elements/)
- **Venmo (privacy as "branding" exposure).** In July 2021 Venmo removed the global public feed. The friends feed became the only social feed, but transactions stayed public by default, still visible on profile pages and via the public API — [The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/mobile-payments/venmo-drops-global-social-feed-that-allowed-users-to-see-strangers-payments--1250580); [EFF, Jul 2021](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/07/venmo-takes-another-step-toward-privacy); [The Week](https://theweek.com/tech/1002823/youll-no-longer-be-able-to-see-strangers-payments-on-venmo)

### Inferences
- Gating removal at a *mid* tier (Typeform's Plus, Linktree's Pro, Calendly's Standard), not the cheapest, is a deliberate choice. Every low-tier paying customer keeps advertising.
- Typeform shows the trade-off openly: buying "no branding" also switches off the product's own acquisition button. This is the clearest documented case where removing branding means removing the recruit call to action.
- The paid-tier brand survives mainly in *URLs* and the *recipient flow*, not in visible logos. Calendly's and Linktree's domains still appear in every shared link after removal.
- Venmo shows the reputational ceiling. Exposure that users experience as surveillance turns into a privacy story (Axios 2018, EFF 2021) and forces a rollback.

### Gaps
- No company disclosed what share of users pay to remove branding, or the acquisition lost when they do.
- Current Linktree and Typeform prices were conflicting and not verified on the official pricing pages.
- Whether Calendly's paid-tier booking URL still shows calendly.com, and whether any branding stays in invitee emails after removal, was not confirmed.

## 4. What are the quantified effects: signups or share of growth from the artifact, and any published K-factor?

### Takeaway
The best-documented attribution figures are Calendly (about 70% of signups via the invitee loop, per its then-CPO), Substack (the network drives more than 50% of new free and more than 30% of new paid subscriptions, per company posts), Wrapped (200M engaged users and 500M+ shares in the first day in 2025) and Dropbox (100k to about 4M users in 15 months, historical). No company in scope published a K-factor.

### Cited Findings

**Hotmail (historical)**
- About 12 million users at the Microsoft acquisition (about $400M, usually dated December 1997) is the most repeated figure, but no primary source was found — [fasterthannormal](https://fasterthannormal.co/businesses/hotmail); [TechCrunch, 2009](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail) (secondary)
- One report instead says Hotmail gained 8.5 million users within 18 months of launch — [DNA India](https://dnaindia.com/business/report-meet-sabeer-bhatia-indian-whose-18-month-old-company-was-bought-by-microsoft-for-rs-3358-crore-3103527/amp). This conflicts with the 12M figure.
- Secondary accounts say Hotmail had 750,000 users by Labor Day 1996 and 1 million within about six months — [themarketingmillennials](https://themarketingmillennials.com/articles/2023-01-09/the-unknown-story-of-how-hotmail-grew-to-12-million-users-in-1-5-years/); [fasterthannormal](https://fasterthannormal.co/businesses/hotmail) (secondary, unverified)
- Microsoft reported more than 30 million active members by July 1999 — [Microsoft News Center](https://news.microsoft.com/?p=12288)

**Calendly**
- Then-CPO Annie Pearl, on Lenny's Podcast: "seventy percent of our signups come through that viral loop" — [Lenny's Podcast transcript (rosetta.to)](https://rosetta.to/u/lennyspodcast/behind-the-scenes-of-calendly-s-rapid-growth-annie-pearl-cpo); [podcast summary](https://podcasts-distilled-hugo-content.pages.dev/lennys-podcast-product-career-growth/behind-the-scenes-of-calendlys-rapid-growth-annie-pearl-cpo/)
- January 2021 (historical): Calendly raised $350M from OpenView and ICONIQ at a valuation above $3B, with about 10M monthly users. It had been profitable for years (TechCabal says since 2016), had raised only $550k before, and had about $70M subscription revenue in 2020. TechCrunch also reported a 1,180% growth figure, metric unclear in the snippet — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2100684); [TechCabal, 27 Jan 2021](https://techcabal.com/2021/01/27/tope-awotonas-calendly-raises-350m/)

**Spotify Wrapped**
- 2021: nearly 60 million shares of Wrapped stories and cards — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2449677)
- 2022: more than 156 million users engaged — [TIME](https://time.com/6340656)
- 2023: 227.4M+ users engaged (+44.2% YoY) and 50.3M+ shared (+32.9% YoY), company-reported in Spotify's Cannes Lions entry — [Cannes Lions / Love the Work](https://lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/wrapped-experience-1637034)
- 2025: 200M engaged users within 24 hours, against 62 hours to reach the same mark in 2024. Media reported this as a "19% YoY increase". ## 5. When platforms or copycats cloned the feature, what kept the original's brand strong, or not?

### Takeaway
Results were mixed and depended on two things: whether the clone was *bundled* into something users already had, and whether the original's artifact or experience was *better to share*. Linktree survived Instagram's native five links (April 2023) on analytics and convenience. Wrapped kept its cultural lead over Apple Music Replay (2019–) on share design, despite a weak 2024. Zoom kept the verb and the brand, but its growth flattened to about 4% a year as bundled rivals took over.

### Inferences
- **Linktree** survived the platform clone because the clone gave the *capability* (more links) but not the *job*: an editable hub with analytics and monetisation. Linktree's brand also rides on its own domain, which Instagram cannot absorb.
- **Wrapped** kept its lead because its share artifact (bold, stories-format cards, a fixed annual drop) is more shareable than Replay's always-on data view. Rivals copied the data but not the ritual. Even a bad year (2024) did not transfer the cultural moment, and the 2025 rebound was a return to the original formula.
- **Zoom** shows the limit of becoming a verb. The verb survived, and the brand is in the dictionary. But once bundled substitutes reached workplaces, the self-serve segment, where "just send a Zoom link" virality lives, flattened (+1.4%), while enterprise sales carried growth.
- **Polaroid** shows that a design-only distinctive asset can be cloned almost exactly, and that defending it is slow and uncertain (nine years and counting). Legal protection is weaker than habit and recognition.

- The Polaroid v. Fujifilm trial outcome (if any after August 2025) was not confirmed.
- The specifics of the Spotify Wrapped 2024 backlash (AI podcast, design criticism) were not retrieved.

## Implications for Ethos (interpretation, not findings)

*Everything below is the researcher's interpretation, applying the findings above to the Ethos context supplied in the brief. It is not sourced fact.*

1. **The link preview card is Ethos's Hotmail footer, and the best-placed asset it has.** It appears in the recipient's chat (and in group chats) *before* the gift is opened. It is present on free and paid gifts alike, and the sender cannot strip it. It works like Calendly's and Linktree's domains, which keep carrying the brand after paid removal. The sealed envelope plus recipient's name plus TRYETHOS.IO should be treated as the core distinctive asset (the Tiffany-box role) and kept visually constant across templates, so that it becomes recognisable without the name.
2. **Paid gifts currently remove branding at the highest-emotion, highest-intent moment.** Calendly (70% of signups via invitees) and Typeform show that the recipient's end-of-flow screen is where acquisition happens. Typeform's paid removal also kills its "Create a typeform" button. Ethos already keeps "make one" / "send one back" on paid gifts, which is good, but these buttons name nothing. Consider a tasteful maker's mark on paid end screens, such as a seal or envelope motif, or "sealed with Ethos" in small type. That is distinct from the advert-like "Made with Ethos" footer, and it could be tested as an opt-out instead of being removed by default. Gate full removal at a higher tier, as Typeform (Plus) and Linktree (Pro) do, rather than with any payment.
3. **Aim for the Tiffany effect, not the Hotmail effect, on paid gifts.** In gifting, a visible "free tool" footer can make a gift feel cheap, which is probably why senders pay to remove it. Tiffany's box shows that branding can *add* perceived value when it signals that the gift was bought and cared for. The design goal is a mark that makes the gift look more premium (a wax-seal style, consistent across templates), not an ad.
4. **"Ethos" is unlikely to become a verb on its own, so give people a noun for the artifact.** It is a common English word, ambiguous in speech and in search, and the domain carries a "try" prefix. The documented lever is naming the artifact in the call to action ("Create a typeform", "Make a Loom"). Consider copy like "make an Ethos" or "send one back" combined with a consistent object word (the "envelope" or "letter"), so recipients learn what to ask for. Check that search for the chosen phrase actually leads to tryethos.io.
5. **Growth is TikTok carousels where the name often can't appear, so lean on design-only recognition.** Instagram (2021) demotes visibly watermarked video, which suggests overlays are a poor bet. Following Polaroid and Wrapped, make the sealed envelope and the "tap to open, sound on" gate visually unmistakable, and show them in carousel content. A viewer who later receives one in a chat should then recognise "one of those" without needing the brand name.
6. **Build a share card for the recipient, and design it to be screenshotted.** Wrapped counts screenshots as shares (500M+ on day one in 2025). Duolingo raised share rates by adding a flattering stat to its card. A recipient's "I got one" story-format card (envelope, name, a line from the gift) could carry the distinctive design onto Instagram and TikTok without an overt watermark. This would complement the seasonal peaks (Valentine's, Mother's Day) that could serve as Ethos's Wrapped-style rituals.
7. **Measure the loop the way Calendly did.** Tag free and paid gift links separately, and track recipient opens, "make one" taps, recipient-to-sender conversion and secondary sends (an internal K-factor). This would put a number on what removing branding from paid gifts costs, which none of the companies studied published.
8. **Expect clones and platform risk, and defend through the job, not the feature.** Linktree survived Instagram's native links on convenience, analytics and its own domain. Wrapped survived Replay on share design and ritual. Zoom's self-serve virality flattened once bundled rivals arrived. If messaging apps, Apple, Meta or Canva ship "animated gift card" features, Ethos's defence is a recipient experience and a recognisable envelope that people specifically ask for.
