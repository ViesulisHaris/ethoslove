**The hook is a paradox, not a plot point.** The two at the top are one sentence with two halves that
fight each other — moved on / neither let go, loved you / let you go. They name nobody, explain
nothing, and every person watching can put their own life in them. The middle of the table is plot:
a fiancé, a misunderstanding, five years. Interesting, but you have to care about *those* people
first, and that costs a million views. The bottom is plain sadness with nothing pulling against it,
and it does a thirtieth of the top.

**Our line, in that shape:** "POV: he packed everything except you." Six words, two halves, no names.

Three more things from their 3.6M post:

- **The caption is a question and a part number.** `should she call him? | part 1`. Not a joke, not a
  summary — a question the comments answer and a promise there is more.
- **The hashtags are genre, not reach:** `#lovestory #romance #part1`. No #fyp.
- **Saves beat comments 33 to 1** — 30.5K saves, 920 comments, 12.2K shares on 3.6M. People keep
  these. The top comment, at 31K likes, is a moral argument about what the couple are doing, and one
  of the most-liked replies under it is "we all know it's scripted but we choose to act like it was
  real". The audience does not need it to be true. It needs to be consistent.

/ "im not ok". 03.12: "6 hours" / "dont say the number" / "sorry" / "come here" / "im outside".

Nobody is leaving because they stopped loving each other — he got in somewhere and she is the one
telling him to go, so the comments have nothing to fight about except whether she went downstairs.
The gift is deliberately small: a bus stop, a receipt from the Chinese. Nobody makes a keepsake out of
the holidays.
