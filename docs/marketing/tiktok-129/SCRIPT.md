## Why this one

Awe + tenderness, the highest-arousal pair for sharing. An 84-year-old succeeding at the interaction removes the "will they manage it" objection for every mum, dad and nan gift, and "Your grandad would have loved this" is grief handled gently, which is the line that gets sent to siblings.

## The slides

1. The stop. "its a link, just press it" and her full-sentence reply "Hello love. It says open the card. Do I press it" — the voice is the joke and the warmth at once.
2. The turn. "Oh my goodness" / "It opened like a real card" / "There is a cake stood up in it" / "I've lit them. They're all lit."
3. Proof. The card open and lit: "Dear Nan," on the lid in handwriting, 84 on the topper, signed Ellie.
4. She sells it. "Blow on it?" / "Ellie I look ridiculous" / "They've gone out." / "How does it know" — the microphone as magic, seen by someone who doesn't take it for granted.
5. Proof two. The letter, open, addressed to her.
6. "Your grandad would have loved this." / "He'd have blown on the phone all day." / "he would" / "I'm going to show Margaret." ❤️. Sadness → warmth in four lines; Margaret is the laugh that lets people share it.
7. The save. "for everyone asking about nan's card": template, what she does, "nan managed it", 15 min, code LOVE, "save this for ur nan. and ring her".
