## Why this hook

"only open it if we ever break up" is the most frightening sentence in the batch and it is not a
threat — it is an insurance policy, written by somebody who was too shy to say the thing out loud
while things were fine. The viewer spends slide 1 certain this ends badly. It does not, and the
correction happens inside slide 3, which is where a dread hook has to break.

- **Four bubbles on slide 1.** The instruction, the condition, her question, his answer. Nothing else.
- **"im opening it" / "mia" / "im opening it"** is the whole of slide 2 and it is funnier than any
  joke we could write, because everybody knows she was always going to open it.
- **"it says if ur reading this i already lost"** is the screenshot.
- **The reason is the payoff:** he wanted her to know what he would say if he lost her, while he still
  had her. Nobody is leaving anybody. Nothing to argue about.
