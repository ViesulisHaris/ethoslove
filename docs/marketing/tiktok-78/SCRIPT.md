## Why it is built this way

- **The opening argument is the most relatable one there is.** Every couple has a fight about which
  date counts — the first date, the day you made it official, the day you met — and nobody has ever
  won it. It is low stakes, which the slow shape needs, and it is the best comment engine we have ever
  written: the comments will not be about the story, they will be people stating their own rule.
- **He is wrong in the funniest direction.** He is not forgetting the anniversary, he is being
  *pedantic* about it, which keeps him likeable while the viewer takes her side. A boy who forgets is
  a villain; a boy who insists it is tomorrow is a bit.
- **"It's tomorrow" is the phrase to repeat,** and the last bubble resolves it: by the time she has
  read the whole thing it is past midnight, so he is finally right, and the two words she opened with
  come back from him. The loop is the joke.
- **365 against 391 is the number to check.** She counts from the first date. He counts from the day he
  first saw her, twenty-six days earlier, which is the whole confession and he never says a feeling
  out loud. The maths holds if anyone checks it: 18 Sept 2025 → 14 Oct 2026 is 391 days, and 14 Oct
  2025 → 14 Oct 2026 is 365.
- **"You always start at the end" plants the thing nobody has read.** It is also a joke at her expense
  that she immediately proves ("yes" / "no"), so the withheld page costs no tension to set up.
- **Midnight does the work of a grand gesture.** No car park, no flight, no money. The only thing that
  happens is the clock, which is free and which every viewer's boyfriend also has access to.

Her: Noa. Him: the one who counts from the wrong day. Five chat slides, four gift frames, one to close.
One timestamp at the top and one on the last slide, nothing in between, so the five chat slides read
as one conversation being scrolled.
