# Brand the wrapping, not the gift

Ethos can show its name to every recipient and every buyer without adding a tap, because the moments with the widest reach are steps everyone already takes. These are the chat preview before the link is opened, the "Tap to open" press that browsers require before any sound can play, the checkout and the TikTok post. Branding those costs code, not friction, and runs into almost nothing. Today the gift page drops the "Ethos" site name from its preview because of how Next.js merges metadata. The tap-to-open gate shows an unlabelled seal and plays no sound. Receipts and the Apple Pay sheet will name another business until Ethos has its own Stripe account. The moves that do run into trouble sit inside the gift and after it. A hallmark on paid gifts goes against the Terms and against a Spanish pay-panel promise of "sin la marca de Ethos" (without the Ethos brand), and no badge product reviewed keeps its mark on after payment. Email reminders to recipients need their own unticked consent under ePrivacy rules and Spain's LSSI. A recipient share card adds a fifth action to an end screen that already has four. The evidence favours branding the wrapper. Sonic logos that include the brand name are attributed about nine times better than music alone. Wallet-first checkouts carry Stripe's largest reported lifts. Moonpig takes about 40% of its orders within a week of a reminder. Gift research, meanwhile, finds that presentation which feels corporate or overdone can make close partners judge the giver as less thoughtful. Ethos should therefore ship in four waves. In November, before the Christmas push: the preview fix, measurement, a Spanish copy fix and Ethos's own Stripe account. By mid-December: the name and one shared seal-crack chime on every opening, plus a calendar reminder that stores nothing. In January, ahead of Valentine's: a named reply button and, for new purchases only and with a free "hide" switch, a paid-gift hallmark. Last, as tests: the share card and a sung "Ethos". TikTok runs alongside all four, with the disclosure label on, no web address or QR code, an owned sound titled "Ethos" and a pinned search term. A copycat can rebuild the envelope in a weekend. It cannot legally reuse the name, the seal-and-name lockup or the sound, and it cannot inherit the occasion dates Ethos holds.

## Four steps every user already takes can carry the name

The constraint the owner set (no new step in making, buying or opening a gift) turns out to be a useful filter rather than a handicap, because Ethos's flows already contain several steps nobody can skip. A recipient always sees a preview card in the chat. They always make one tap to open, because the tap is what lets the page play sound. A signed-in sender of a free gift reaches a shareable link in about three taps after the editor. A paid sender always passes through a payment sheet that names a merchant ([share-screen.tsx](../src/components/editor/share-screen.tsx); [sound-gate.tsx](../src/components/gift/sound-gate.tsx)). The name can ride on each of those steps for free. Anything that needs a new screen, field or decision has to pass a stricter test.

Where the name appears today is lopsided. **86% of gift opens are on free gifts that carry the "Made with Ethos" footer**, and recipients open on iPhone 68% of the time ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). The other 14% are paid gifts, and inside a paid gift the word "Ethos" appears nowhere. All the recipient sees is an unlabelled wax-seal icon on the gate and loading screens, which is hidden from screen readers and has no word attached ([logo.tsx](../src/components/shared/logo.tsx); [publish.ts](../src/lib/gift/publish.ts)). Those are the most impressive gifts in the catalogue: on premium templates **about 60% of people who start a gift pay**, against 4.5% for the free Letter ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). The price of that gap shows up in search. About 99% of Google clicks in late September and October came from people typing "tryethos" or a misspelling of it, and Google Lens queries such as "what website is that?" show viewers trying to recover a name they were never shown ([SEO-ORGANIC.md](../docs/marketing/SEO-ORGANIC.md)).

The research on gifts and brand marks points to one placement rule: put the brand on the wrapping and on the steps around the gift, not over what the sender made. Wrapping lifts recipients' feelings about a gift through mood, even when the wrapping is plain ([ScienceBlogs on Howard 1992](https://scienceblogs.com/grrlscientist/2009/12/15/the-psychology-behind-wrapping-2)). Between friends, though, neat presentation raises expectations the gift may then miss ([Rixom, Mas & Rixom 2020](https://myscp.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jcpy.1140)). Recipients read overpackaging as a sign that the giver was *less* thoughtful, even though givers believe the opposite ([Shi, Chen & Yin 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S002243592400068X)). Handmade gifts win because they seem to "contain love", and mainly for loved ones ([Fuchs, Schreier & van Osselaer 2015](https://www.wu.ac.at/fileadmin/wu/d/i/mm/paper/2015_CF_MS_SO_The_Handmade_Effect_Whats_Love_Got_to_Do_with_It.pdf)). No study tests a visible maker's brand on a personal digital gift, so the case for restraint is an inference from these four papers, not a measured result. Media habits point the same way. Netflix members press Skip Intro **about 136 million times a day**, because long title sequences they have seen before get skipped ([Netflix](https://about.netflix.com/news/looking-back-on-the-origin-of-skip-intro-five-years-later)). The roughly three-second "ta-dum" sting is short enough to sit inside the start of something people want, and it became loved ([Marketplace](https://marketplace.org/2022/01/03/the-strategy-behind-sonic-logos-like-netflixs-startup-chime)). Brand lines that people keep also tend to give the sender something. In a 111-person experiment, "Sent from my iPhone" softened readers' judgement of typos ([Carr & Stefaniak 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257828481_Sent_from_My_iPhone_The_Medium_and_Message_as_Cues_of_Sender_Professionalism_in_Mobile_Telephony)). Superhuman said in 2024 that its default "Sent via Superhuman" line still drove **more than 30% of site traffic** ([Deciphr podcast summary](https://www.deciphr.ai/podcast/how-i-built-825m-email-revolution-called-superhuman--rahul-vohra)).

Memory research adds one requirement: the mark must travel *with the name* until it is famous. A new distinctive asset such as a seal or a chime is useless as a shortcut until people know it belongs to one brand. Jenni Romaniuk of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute says that while an asset is being built it must be shown alongside the brand name ([Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/mumbrella360-ehrenberg-bass-professor-says-youre-poorly-branding-if-youre-focusing-on-assets-over-your-brand-832843); [Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/brands-need-distinctive-assets/)). Envelopes and wax seals are category signals that rivals already use. An unlabelled seal teaches recipients the category, not Ethos. ## The preview and the tap to open reach every recipient before the surprise

### The link preview lost its site name to a metadata merge, not a decision

The chat card is the first thing every recipient sees, and today it names everyone except Ethos. Its title is the sender's own title or "{name}, someone made you something 💌". Its description is "Open it with your sound on.", and the image is a sealed envelope with "for Maya / someone made you something" ([page.tsx](<../src/app/[currency]/[locale]/(gift)/g/[shortId]/page.tsx>); [card-art.ts](../src/lib/gift/card-art.ts)). The image dropped the web address on 16 October, partly because the owner found the old card "bland" and partly because "the chat prints the domain under it anyway" ([VIRAL-FORMULA.md](../docs/marketing/VIRAL-FORMULA.md); [card-art.ts](../src/lib/gift/card-art.ts)). The missing **og:site_name** was never decided by anyone. The root layout defines `siteName: "Ethos"`, but the gift page sets its own `openGraph` object, and Next.js replaces every `openGraph` field from the layout when a page does that ([layout.tsx](<../src/app/[currency]/[locale]/layout.tsx>); [Next.js generate-metadata docs](../node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/03-api-reference/04-functions/generate-metadata.md)). For the same reason, the "· Ethos" title template reaches the browser tab but not the preview title. Putting `siteName` back on the gift page is one line of code. It reaches every recipient, names no template and spoils nothing.

The **link preview** mockup goes further, and the evidence supports most of it. Adding "An Ethos gift." in front of "Open it with your sound on." puts the name beside the domain the chat already prints, which is the co-presentation Romaniuk calls for. It also leaves the title, and with it the surprise, untouched. "An Ethos gift" is the right form of words: it pairs the brand with a generic noun, the usage Evite's press kit asks writers to follow and the safeguard against the brand becoming a common noun ([Evite Press Kit](https://partners.evitecdn.com/Press%20Kit/Press%20Kit/Evite_PressKit_02.2019.pdf)). There is a trust angle too. Search Console shows queries such as "is this website safe" and "will it hack me" landing on the site, and two scam checkers are the only external sites linking to it ([SEO-ORGANIC.md](../docs/marketing/SEO-ORGANIC.md)). Kaspersky warned in December 2025 that phishing sites target last-minute digital-gift buyers ([Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.com/about/press-releases/the-future-of-gift-shopping-half-of-holiday-shoppers-plan-to-give-digital-presents-this-christmas-kaspersky-new-report-reveals)). A consistent name on the card gives a wary recipient something to recognise. No study measures whether a brand word in a preview raises or lowers open rates, so this needs a test, not an assumption.

The card lockup (a small seal-and-name mark on the envelope, still with no web address) conflicts with a past decision. On 16 October the first TikTok post showing "tryethos.io" got **0 views**, and the team set the rule "Nothing that names the site on screen. No link card, no domain, no 'tryethos' in the chat." The rule mattered because the TikTok slides draw the real preview card ([VIRAL-FORMULA.md](../docs/marketing/VIRAL-FORMULA.md)). The mockup's own fix is sound: the slides are rendered by a separate script, so they can use an unbranded card while real chats get the lockup. Any change to the card image also needs a new version hash in its URL so the chat apps fetch it again. The image has to stay well under the roughly 300 KB above which WhatsApp is known to drop previews ([card.jpg route](<../src/app/g/[shortId]/card.jpg/route.tsx>)). Paid senders were sold "all from you", but the preview sits in the chat app rather than inside the gift, and the domain already shows there, so the conflict is mild. The Terms already say a preview "can show the gift's title or the recipient's name" ([legal.ts](../src/content/legal.ts)), and adding a brand word discloses nothing personal. What nobody knows is how iMessage, WhatsApp and Instagram's direct messages actually display og:site_name next to the domain. That calls for a device check before launch.

### The tap to open is the one gesture browsers already demand

Browsers block audio until the user interacts with the page. In Chrome, an audio context created before a gesture starts suspended and must be resumed from a touch or click ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay/)). iOS Safari is stricter and wants playback started inside the tap handler itself ([Chromium issue 41387757](https://issues.chromium.org/issues/41387757)). Ethos's gate exists for exactly this reason. The code calls it "the single user gesture browsers need before audio can play. Also the emotional threshold." ([sound-gate.tsx](../src/components/gift/sound-gate.tsx)). On The Letter and about 30 other templates with the classic cover, the gate shows the unlabelled seal, "Leo → Maya", "Someone made this for you, Maya…" and "Tap to open". A loading screen of **at least 1.6 seconds** follows the tap ([GiftRenderer.tsx](../src/templates/_shared/GiftRenderer.tsx); [looks.ts](../src/templates/_shared/covers/looks.ts)). The gate's tap plays nothing ([gift-experience.tsx](../src/components/gift/gift-experience.tsx)). Decorated covers skip the gate. Their tap plays a paper or ribbon sound and then a three-note synthesised chime about 0.6–0.8 seconds later ([Cover.tsx](../src/templates/_shared/covers/Cover.tsx); [sounds.ts](../src/templates/_shared/collage/sounds.ts)). So Ethos already has half a sonic logo and plays it on only some gifts.

The **tap-to-open** mockup adds the wordmark under the seal and plays a 1.5-second seal crack and chime on the existing tap, timed with the seal opening. Both cost zero taps and zero seconds, because they fill a wait that already exists. The sound evidence supports keeping it short and simple. Practitioners recommend sonic logos of 1–3 seconds ([Sonic Minds](https://sonicmindsagency.com/what-defines-a-great-sonic-logo/)). In a 2025 test of 0.5-, 1- and 2-second cues, shorter cues distracted less and longer ones were easier to identify, and the authors chose one second ([arXiv 2507.04238](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04238)). A 2026 study of 90 commercial sonic logos found that simple, hummable structures are recalled best ([Cambridge repository preprint](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/eb68da1f-6ea7-4829-be41-d7da46f26aff/content)). Most of the recognition gain from repetition comes by the third hearing ([Pramana et al. 2024](https://journal.ipb.ac.id/jcs/article/download/57484/29475)). Older earcon guidelines blame loudness, not length, for annoyance ([Brewster-style guidelines](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228607856_Experimentally_derived_guidelines_for_the_creation_of_earcons)). The decisive finding for branding is SoundOut's 2025 tracking study. Sonic logos that include the brand name were **about 9 times better at correct attribution**, and without the name, accuracy fell to **18%** ([MarComm News](https://marcommnews.com/largest-ever-sonic-brand-tracking-study-reveals-the-best-performing-sonic-logos-of-2025/)). A wordless chime will not teach anyone "Ethos" on its own. Paired with the visible wordmark on the same screen, it will, because the eye supplies the name the ear lacks.

The conflicts are technical and solvable, with one copy problem. Ethos's audio code fades library music in when the template loads, warns that "a bell on top of one adds up past full scale and clips", remembers the recipient's mute choice and allows only one voice at a time ([use-gift-audio.ts](../src/templates/_shared/hooks/use-gift-audio.ts); [sound-floor.ts](../src/templates/_shared/sound-floor.ts)). A chime shorter than the 1.6-second loading wait finishes before the music starts. It must still respect a remembered mute, and it should sit below the music's level. One risk hides here: if the chime annoys someone into muting, that mute would also silence the sender's song and voice note, so the chime has to stay gentle. iOS's silent switch has not been tested with these synthesised sounds. The sound should not play in the sender's editor previews, because the sender, not the recipient, hears it dozens of times. Two placement fixes complete the move. The gate appears only on classic covers, so the wordmark must also go on the decorated covers' "For Maya / Tap to open" screen, or a large share of recipients will miss it. Nobody has measured how gifts split between the two cover types ([gift-experience.tsx](../src/components/gift/gift-experience.tsx)). And because the gate also appears on paid gifts, the Spanish pay-panel line "Sin la marca de Ethos, todo de tu parte" and the made-with-Ethos page's "va sin la marca" become plainly untrue once a name sits under the seal ([es.json](../messages/es.json)). The English versions promise only "no footer". The Spanish lines should match the editor's own "Quitar el pie «Hecho con Ethos»" before the wordmark ships. The mockup's sung "Ethos" is the risky variant. Nobody has studied a spoken brand name at the start of a love letter, and the gift research above counsels restraint. It belongs in a late A/B test, not the first release.

## A paid gift's last screen is where Ethos's own promises bind

On a paid gift today, the end screen shows a heart, "The end. For now.", "Made by Leo for Maya", a reaction button, "Send one back to Leo", "The first reply is free", "Keep this gift" and "Watch again", with no Ethos anywhere ([EndScreen.tsx](../src/templates/_shared/EndScreen.tsx)). The **end of a paid gift** mockup makes four changes. It adds a "Sealed with Ethos" hallmark under the credit, renames the reply button "Send Leo an Ethos gift back", relabels the keep link "Keep it, and a reminder before Leo's birthday" and adds "Share that you got one". The end deserves care because the peak-end rule says people remember an experience by its peak and its ending ([Wikipedia: Peak–end rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule)). The same screen is also where the brand's two strongest growth precedents and its sharpest legal conflict meet.

The named reply button is the best-supported change, and it costs no step. Hotmail grew on a footer that turned every email into an invitation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail/)). Typeform ends free forms with "Create a typeform" ([Typeform Help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029262372)), and Loom ends shared videos with "Make a Loom" ([Product Fruits](https://productfruits.substack.com/p/how-loom-went-from-zero-to-being)). Each teaches the recipient a word next to an action they may want. Typeform's growth, by one analyst's account, came 80% through word of mouth and product virality, much of it untrackable "direct" traffic ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/typeforms-viral-growth-and-its-disruption)). On real gift pages the reply button always reads "Send one back to {sender}". The more general "Make one for someone you love" appears only in demos, so renaming that one reaches almost no recipients ([gift-experience.tsx](../src/components/gift/gift-experience.tsx); [i18n.ts](../src/templates/_shared/i18n.ts)). The live string also names the button in the Terms ("the 'send one back' button at its end") and in launch test 7.10, so both need updating ([legal.ts](../src/content/legal.ts); [LAUNCH.md](../docs/LAUNCH.md)). Spanish is the trap. The natural short form, "Hazle un Ethos a Leo", uses the brand as a common noun. That is the pattern that cost Thermos its mark ([Lovemoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/gallerylist/575033/59-brand-names-that-unexpectedly-became-everyday-words)), and under the EU's *Kornspitz* ruling a mark can be revoked when its owner lets it be used generically ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/revocation-of-successful-trademarks-that-became-common-in-the-trade)). The Spanish string should keep "un regalo" in it. The baseline is low and muddied: replies run at 2.3% of free gifts and 0.5% of premium ones, but premium recipients met a price when replying ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)).

The hallmark is where the evidence turns against the mockup. Every "made with" badge product reviewed shows the badge by default and removes it on payment, including Lovable, v0, Framer, Webflow and Carrd ([Framer Help](https://www.framer.com/help/articles/how-do-i-remove-the-made-in-framer-badge-from-my-website/); [Webflow Help](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961378233235-Webflow-branding); [Carrd docs](https://carrd.com/docs/pro/plans)). Shopify lets any store remove its badge for free ([Pixel Union](https://support.pixelunion.net/hc/en-us/articles/25206667974803-How-do-I-remove-Powered-by-Shopify-from-the-footer)). None keeps a mark on after payment. When v0's badge stayed on for paying users through a bug, they complained publicly ([Vercel Community](https://community.vercel.com/t/built-with-v0-badge-showing-in-premium/33610)). Ethos's own documents promise the same thing many times over. The Terms list "removing the footer" as a paid extra and give unlocks "no footer". They say the footer "may be removed only by unlocking the template" and that a refund brings it back. Any change that affects something paid for "in more than a minor way" requires advance notice and a free 30-day exit with a proportionate refund ([legal.ts](../src/content/legal.ts)). The pay panel says "No Ethos footer, all from you", and in Spanish the broader "Sin la marca de Ethos" ([en.json](../messages/en.json); [es.json](../messages/es.json)). A non-linked hallmark that never says "Made with" is technically not the footer. In Spanish it is still "la marca", and buyers will read the spirit, not the definition.

The reach arithmetic decides the order. A paid-gift hallmark adds the brand to at most the **14% of opens** that are paid, and once the wordmark sits under the seal on the gate and covers, those recipients will already have seen "Ethos" at the threshold. Its marginal value is a second exposure at the end. Its marginal risk is the highest of any move in this report. The defensible version applies only to gifts bought after the Terms and pay-panel copy change, so no existing contract changes, and stays visibly distinct from the footer (no link, no "Made with"). It should also come with a free "Hide the Ethos seal" switch in the Extras section where the footer switch already lives ([extras.tsx](../src/components/editor/sections/extras.tsx)). Default-on but removable is how Superhuman's signature, Apple's restored Mac startup chime and Shopify's badge all work ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/27/my-six-months-with-30-month-email-service-superhuman/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2020/06/23/macos-big-sur-brings-back-startup-chime/)). It adds no step for the sender who keeps the default and gives a way out to the sender who bought "all from you". The planned but unbuilt M11.2 text link, "or make one for someone else" under Replay on paid gifts, is a gentler route to the same reach ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). The mockup's keep-link label stays within the March reminders plan (M6), which put reminder capture "never on the recipient's end screen": the label advertises a reminder, but the form stays on the separate keep page ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md); [EndScreen.tsx](../src/templates/_shared/EndScreen.tsx)).

## Reminders and shares belong to the recipient, so they must cost the recipient nothing

### The calendar entry is a brand placement that needs no consent

Moonpig shows why occasion dates are the moat a copycat cannot clone overnight. It held **113 million reminders** at 30 April 2026 ([Moonpig FY26 factsheet](https://www.moonpig.group/media/0tqhyd35/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-full-year-results-investor-factsheet.pdf)), and **nearly 40% of its orders arrive within seven days of a reminder** ([Moonpig FY25 results](https://www.moonpig.group/media/ylcfmqgj/moonpig-group-plc-fy25-full-year-results-announcementpdf.pdf)). Moonpig captures those dates from logged-in *buyers*, by hand or from their phone calendar. In the US it offers 40% off for setting three reminders and emails a week and three days before each date ([Moonpig help](https://help.moonpig.com/en/articles/318961-how-do-reminders-work); [Moonpig US](https://www.moonpig.com/us/add-reminder)). No leader was found asking a card's or gift's recipient for their own dates, so there is no benchmark for what Ethos proposes. Ethos already has Moonpig's sender-side mechanism. The share screen's reminder card saves this gift's occasion for next year with one tap. Every publishing sender is signed in, because free guests sign in to publish and paid guests come back signed in, so every sender sees it ([remind-next-year.tsx](../src/components/reminders/remind-next-year.tsx); [publish-sheet.tsx](../src/components/editor/publish-sheet.tsx)). The plan set a 5–15% opt-in target to be "read in early November", and the baseline table still records "Reminder opt-in: 0" ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). That reading is due now, and it should come before more is built on the recipient side.

The recipient side is today Ethos's heaviest flow. "Keep this gift" leaves the gift for a separate page. That page requires an email, offers unticked reminder boxes (Leo's birthday, our anniversary, Valentine's) with month and day menus, and sends a confirmation email before anything is kept ([keep-form.tsx](../src/components/gift/keep-form.tsx); [keep.ts](../src/lib/keep/keep.ts)). It takes two screens, at least three taps and two emails, and nobody has measured how many finish. The **keep-and-remind sheet** mockup puts "Add to my calendar — No account. We store nothing." first and email second. Ordering it that way is the strongest idea in the set. If the calendar file (.ics) is built in the browser from the month and day the recipient picks, the date never reaches Ethos's server and Ethos never sends anything, so no consent question arises. That is an inference, and no regulator has addressed calendar files. The event can still carry the brand a year later: an entry titled "Leo's birthday", with a seven-day alert and the note "Send Leo an Ethos gift: tryethos.io", lands at the exact moment Moonpig's reminders do, without an inbox. The costs are real. Ethos cannot measure anything past the tap. iOS Safari offers to add .ics files to Calendar, but iOS Chrome may not, and the Google Calendar app cannot import .ics files directly, so Android users need Google's own link format ([Marketo Nation thread](https://nation.marketo.com/thread/27884); [UNISON guide](https://learning.unison.org.uk/how-to-add-an-icalendar-file-to-your-calendar/)). "We store nothing" must also be literally true, which rules out putting the date in an Ethos URL that request logs would record. The date entry itself should be day and month only, as typed fields or short selects rather than a calendar picker, which is what the US Web Design System recommends for memorable dates ([USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/memorable-date/)).

The email half of the mockup has a consent flaw. Its single checkbox reads "Email me the gift's link and one reminder a week before. Nothing else." That bundles a service the recipient asked for (the link) with marketing (a nudge to send a gift). A recipient who never bought anything is not a "customer", so the ePrivacy soft opt-in does not cover them, and email to them needs prior consent ([Presencis on Art 13](https://presencis.com/regulations/eprivacy-directive/article-13/); [Presencis on soft opt-in](https://presencis.com/regulations/eprivacy-directive/definitions/soft-opt-in/)). In the UK regulator's view, a message that "actively promotes or encourages" use of a service is marketing, and any promotional element turns the whole message into marketing ([DPO Centre](https://www.dpocentre.com/marketing-emails-the-fine-line-between-a-service-and-sales-message/)). The EDPB requires consent by clear affirmative action, never pre-ticked, and granular across purposes ([EDPB consent summary](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-04/edpb-summary-consent_en.pdf)). Spain's data authority says LSSI Article 21 "no admite un consentimiento tácito" (does not allow tacit consent) ([AEPD report 2018-0164](https://www.aepd.es/es/documento/2018-0164.pdf)). Since 5 February 2026, UK electronic-marketing (PECR) fines can reach £17.5m or 4% of global turnover ([MFMac](https://www.mfmac.com/insights/data-protection/data-use-and-access-act-2025-increased-maximum-fines-for-cookies-and-direct-marketing-practices/)). Today's keep page actually gets this right, with the email for the link and a separate unticked box for each reminder. The redesign should keep that split, with "Email me the link" as the button and "Also email me a week before Leo's birthday" as its own box. Saving Leo's birthday also makes Ethos a controller of third-party data. That brings a GDPR Article 14 notice to Leo, plausibly given through the sender's own privacy notice, along with first name, day and month only, and deletion on unsubscribe ([GDPR Art 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/14/data.htm); [MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). The Terms promise confirmation by email and "no other emails and no newsletters", so double opt-in stays ([legal.ts](../src/content/legal.ts)). A November 2025 EU court ruling (*Inteligo Media*) read the soft opt-in broadly enough to cover free freemium accounts ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102luvt/ecj-rules-on-soft-opt-in-for-freemium-newsletters-no-separate-gdpr-legal-basis-r/)). It might one day cover a recipient who sends a free reply, but that is untested, and consent is the safe basis. Latvia's implementing law, which governs Ethos, has not been checked against these readings.

One more keep path costs nothing and is half-built. After a gift ends on iOS Safari, a banner already tells recipients to tap Share and then "Add to Home Screen", and the icon is already named "Ethos". But the site manifest's start URL is "/", so the icon opens the homepage instead of the gift ([home-screen-tip.tsx](../src/components/gift/home-screen-tip.tsx); [manifest.ts](../src/app/manifest.ts)). The per-gift manifest planned as M11.3 would make that icon a branded keepsake on the recipient's phone that actually opens the gift ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). No measured install rates exist, so treat it as a cheap fix, not a growth bet. Wallet passes, iOS web push and SMS can wait. Passes need Apple signing certificates and a server, push requires a Home Screen install first, and SMS counts as "electronic mail" under the same consent rule ([Apple developer docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/walletpasses/building-a-pass); [WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/); [Article 29 WP opinion WP90](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2004/wp90_fr.pdf)).

### The share card is the only move that adds an action

No recipient share action exists today. The end screen offers a reaction (which goes privately to the sender), send one back, keep and replay ([EndScreen.tsx](../src/templates/_shared/EndScreen.tsx)). The best case for adding one is Spotify Wrapped: **more than 500 million shares on day one in 2025**, a count that includes screenshots and downloads, from cards built at Instagram Stories size ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day); [AOL/CNET](https://www.aol.com/articles/spotify-wrapped-2025-wraps-more-213100251.html)). The technical path works on the mobile web. Sharing image files through the phone's share sheet is supported in iOS Safari 14–15 onward and Chrome for Android 76 onward, but iOS cannot share text and files together ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/share.json); [Stack Overflow archive](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/76076190/sharing-an-image-using-the-webshare-api-in-ios-is-failing)). Posting straight to Instagram Stories needs native app code, so "Share to Stories" cannot be promised from a web page ([social_story_share docs](https://pub.dev/documentation/social_story_share/0.2.1/)). The **recipient share card** mockup ("FROM LEO / someone made me something / Ethos / SEALED WITH LOVE", no address, no QR) is more cautious than the evidence requires. The documented penalties target video carrying another platform's watermark when it is recommended to strangers: Instagram since February 2021, and TikTok's rule that reposts with another platform's logo are ineligible for its For You feed ([GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/newscomm-47671.php); [Slidy Creator](https://slidycreator.com/blog/tiktok-not-eligible-for-recommendation/)). A Story goes to followers and is not ranked for strangers. The real reason to leave the address off is the 16 October rule, if these images might ever reach TikTok slides. Against that, people already search "tryethos", and a plain "Ethos" sends viewers into a search shared with an insurer, an AI company and a journaling app ([Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/ethos-99619730); [Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/ethos-98552473)). Test the card with and without a small "tryethos.io". The privacy defaults matter more than the brand. The gift link is framed as "a private letter" and can be password-protected, so the card must never show the gift itself, should leave out the sender's name unless the recipient adds it, and should not be offered on password-protected gifts ([legal.ts](../src/content/legal.ts)). Because this is a fifth action on an ending people will remember, it ships last, as a test with reaction and reply rates as guardrails.

## One name where money and attention change hands

### Checkout: the leak is trust, and the fix is an account, not a design

Checkout is the only screen where branding and conversion clearly pull in the same direction. The code already heads Stripe's hosted page "Ethos" with the site icon and adds `ETHOS` to the statement ([line-items.ts](../src/lib/stripe/line-items.ts)). Receipts and the Apple Pay sheet still show the shared account's name, and on 23 September the live page read **"Pay SECUORA"** ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md); [LAUNCH.md](../docs/LAUNCH.md)). The statement reads in the "MIGLA* ETHOS" style, the account is shared with secuora.net and other businesses, and the checkout footer may show another business's terms URL ([STRIPE.md](../docs/STRIPE.md)). The faster one-tap wallet path drops the "Ethos" branding entirely ([line-items.ts](../src/lib/stripe/line-items.ts)). The losses are large. In the 30 days to 26 October, **only about half of everyone who clicked pay finished at Stripe (476 → 233)** ([PROFITABLE-ADS.md](../docs/marketing/PROFITABLE-ADS.md)). That is a different count from the 82% of *started* checkouts paid by 23 September ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)), and the docs do not say which checkout code was live. In the October Meta ad test, every panel group asked for reviews, a company name, a refund line and payment logos ([PROFITABLE-ADS.md](../docs/marketing/PROFITABLE-ADS.md)). Baymard finds that **19% of shoppers who abandon** do so because they "didn't trust the site with my credit card information", and that trust at the payment step comes mainly from reducing ambiguity, not from badges ([Baymard list](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate); [Baymard payment UX](https://baymard.com/blog/payment-ux)). A buyer who comes from Ethos and sees SECUORA is exactly that kind of ambiguity.

The header override cannot finish the job, because Stripe's `display_name` changes only the top of the checkout page, while "your business name still appears in terms, receipts, and other places" ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/customization/appearance)). The statement prefix must reflect the business name, the website or the legal name, and Stripe gives opaque descriptors as a cause of disputes ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/statement-descriptors); [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/why-do-customers-see-statement-descriptors-that-don-t-match-what-i-ve-set-in-stripe)). Disputes are the after-sale cost. Chargebacks911's 2026 report puts "friendly fraud" (customers disputing purchases they made) at **43.8% of chargebacks** ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/nearly-44-of-chargebacks-are-labeled-friendly-fraud-finds-a-chargebacks911-report/)), and 79% of consumers in Ethoca's survey had reported an unrecognised transaction to their bank ([Ethoca](https://www.ethoca.com/blog/transaction-clarity-helps-reduce-disputes-credit-card-chargebacks)). The planned fix, M14, is an Ethos-only Stripe account for SIA "MIGLAI" with the public name "Ethos", the descriptor "TRYETHOS.IO", a verified Apple Pay domain and wallets switched on. It also cures a legal exposure. If the shared account belongs to another company, the checkout names a different trader from the one in the Terms, which the CRD's trader-identity rule, Art 6(1)(b), forbids ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). The plan says to switch "before a big push, not during one", which puts it in November.

The **checkout** mockup's layout is mostly right, with four corrections. Putting the wallet first follows Stripe's advice and its largest reported effects, all vendor-reported and not mutually consistent. Stripe reports +22.3% conversion from offering Apple Pay, a 10% lift in one A/B test, roughly double when Apple Pay is surfaced early, and Wish doubling conversion by defaulting to it ([Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/testing-the-conversion-impact-of-50-plus-global-payment-methods); [Stripe Sessions 2024](https://stripe.com/sessions/2024/a-look-under-stripes-hood-using-data-to-demystify-payments); [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/apple-pay/best-practices)). Wallets also handle EU strong customer authentication with the phone's own biometrics, US cards fall outside it, and EU card payments of €30 or less are often exempt ([Stripe SCA guide](https://stripe.com/guides/strong-customer-authentication); [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/3d-secure/strong-customer-authentication-exemptions)). The first correction concerns the withdrawal tickbox, which the mockup draws *below* the Apple Pay button. EU law lets a trader unlock digital content at once only with the consumer's *prior* express consent and acknowledgement that the right to withdraw is lost, confirmed afterwards on a durable medium. The Commission accepts both given together on the purchase page through a positive action ([Commission Notice OJ C 525/2021](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AC%3A2021%3A525%3AFULL)). The code already makes the wallet button appear only after the box is ticked ([one-tap-pay.tsx](../src/components/editor/one-tap-pay.tsx)), so the box belongs above the buttons. Second, only the words on the button count. The Commission accepts "buy now" and "pay now", and the EU Court ruled in *Fuhrmann-2* that context cannot rescue unclear wording ([Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/new-guidance-from-brussels-on-consumer-rights-directive/); [SCL](https://www.scl.org/12571-cjeu-rules-on-requirements-for-obligation-to-pay-button/)). Wallet wording comes from Stripe's preset button types such as "buy", so the price should sit beside the wallet button, and the card path's button must read "Pay €7.99" itself ([Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/elements/express-checkout-element/accept-a-payment)). Third, "Sold by Ethos Love" does not match the Terms, which name the trader as SIA "MIGLAI" ([legal.ts](../src/content/legal.ts)). "Ethos · sold by SIA MIGLAI, Latvia" keeps one brand and the correct trader. Fourth, the promise of "one name" on the page, the wallet sheet and the statement holds only after M14.

The mockup's reassurance lines are well chosen. "No account" answers Baymard's 19% forced-account abandonment ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/reduce-cart-abandonment)). "We fix it or refund you" matches the planned FAQ wording, and "Receipt goes to you, not to Maya" protects the surprise ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). Two compliance edges remain. When Stripe refuses the tickbox, the code falls back to checkout without it, and that buyer keeps the 14-day right to withdraw ([STRIPE.md](../docs/STRIPE.md)). For such buyers the EU withdrawal button applies from 19 June 2026: a clearly labelled function with a confirmation step and **no login required** ([outvio](https://outvio.com/blog/eu-withdrawal-button/); [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/pitfalls-for-e-commerce-how-the-new-eu-withdrawal-button-widerrufsbutton-wi-102ms91)). The planned "Withdraw from a purchase" link from /account therefore has to work without signing in. In-app browsers are the other edge. The code never offers wallets inside Instagram's or Facebook's browsers, Stripe's Link does not work inside the in-app browsers (webviews) that open links from social apps, and TikTok's in-app browser, which opens every bio and comment link, is untested ([one-tap-pay.tsx](../src/components/editor/one-tap-pay.tsx); [Stripe docs](https://docs.stripe.com/elements/express-checkout-element)). There the card path is the main path and needs equal care. Counsel could also confirm whether the EU tickbox can be hidden from US buyers, which would save them a tap.

### TikTok: give the name a sound and a search term, and test the rest

On TikTok the September hits reached 1.3M and 984.8K views and drew comments like "PLEASE TELL ME THE WEBSITE". Then the post showing "tryethos.io" got zero views, and support cited an unnamed Community Guidelines violation ([VIRAL-FORMULA.md](../docs/marketing/VIRAL-FORMULA.md)). TikTok's own pages say the "Your brand" disclosure setting **does not affect distribution**, while undisclosed promotion may be restricted ([TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service?lang=en)). Since September 2025 TikTok has cut the reach of posts that send users to buy off-platform where TikTok Shop operates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/)), and QR codes are usually ineligible for the For You feed ([TikTok FYF Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards/)). No TikTok document says a brand name or a plain URL on screen limits a disclosed post. The **TikTok post** mockup follows the conservative reading. It turns on "Promotional content", shows no address, credits an original sound "Ethos · the seal opening" and pins a comment: "it's ours. search 'ethos gift' to make one". The owned sound costs nothing in reach, because business accounts are already limited to TikTok's Commercial Music Library ([Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok)). Its title carries the name, which SoundOut's 18%-without-a-name finding shows a wordless chime cannot do. The same chime then plays in every gift, so posts and gifts reinforce each other. The precedent is e.l.f., whose commissioned song spelled out its name and passed a billion views in its first week ([Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/marketers-on-fire-e-l-f-cosmetics-cmo-kory-marchisotto)).

Three things in the mockup need attention. The post comes from "@maya.and.leo" with the caption "he made me something". If that account is a persona Ethos runs, the repo's own legal note applies: posts written "as her" are testimonials from a person who doesn't exist under the FTC's endorsement rules and the EU's unfair-practices rules, and the label alone does not cure that ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). Product posts should come from @tryethos or from real couples who have given permission. Second, the pinned search term should be the one demand already uses. People type "tryethos", while "ethos" alone collides with other brands, and nobody knows what TikTok search returns for "ethos gift". Pin each term on alternate posts and compare brand clicks in Search Console over the following 48 hours. TikTok's keyword search ads on brand terms are cheap insurance against clones buying that demand ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-updates-search-ads-campaigns-with-keyword-targeting/727957/)). Third, once the gate shows "Ethos", every post that films a gift opening will put the name on screen, which the 16 October rule forbids. The 17 October evidence points to missing disclosure, not the domain, as the cause, but nobody has tested it. One matched pair of disclosed posts, identical except for the visible wordmark, settles the question before the rule hardens into folklore.

## Ship in four waves, and let guardrail metrics veto the rest

The order follows from three tests applied together: reach (every recipient before paid recipients only), conflict (none, then copy, then the Terms) and testability. The table sets the waves against the calendar, with Christmas and then Valentine's 2027 as the peaks the plan already targets.

| Wave | When | Moves | Clear first | Primary metric | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2–20 Nov | Restore og:site_name; A/B "An Ethos gift." in the description; Spanish "sin la marca" lines changed to "sin el pie"; add ref tags to end-screen buttons and measure end-screen reach; read the sender reminder opt-in; start M14; trademark clearance search for ETHOS and the seal lockup | Device check of previews in iMessage, WhatsApp and Instagram | Share of published gifts opened within 48 h | Time to open |
| 1b | before the Christmas push | Switch to the M14 account (one name on page, wallet sheet, receipt, statement) | Entity verification; Apple Pay domain | Pay click → paid (baseline ~49%) | "Unrecognised" disputes; refunds |

| 3 | January, live before Valentine's | Terms, pay-panel and button-name updates; "Send Leo an Ethos gift back" on all gifts; hallmark on new paid gifts with a free hide switch | Terms s.2, s.6, s.14, refunds and the reply-button clause; launch test 7.10; Spanish wording | Replies started per open; new makers from paid-gift recipients | Pay sheet → pay on paid templates (20%); refunds; hide-switch rate |

The volumes force a discipline on testing. By my arithmetic, detecting a 20% relative lift on the 2.3% free-gift reply rate takes about **18,000 recipients per arm** at conventional power (95% confidence, 80% power). The 13,959 recipients counted by 23 September ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)) would not fill one arm. Reply rate and brand search can only be directional readings outside the peaks. Gift completion, end-screen reach and mute rate have far higher base rates, so they should serve as the go/no-go signals for the opening changes. Checkout is the most testable screen. About 476 pay clicks a month at a ~49% finish rate means a 10-point change shows up within a month on either side of the account switch. The paid hallmark is the hardest. Seeing a 3-point drop from the 20% pay rate on paid templates needs about 2,600 pay-sheet views per arm, which only Valentine's volume supplies. That is one more reason to run it as a cohort test then, rather than ship it on faith. The events to read most of this already exist in Ethos's cookieless analytics: `keep_tapped`, `reply_started`, `reminder_saved` and view progress ([track.ts](../src/lib/analytics/track.ts)).

Each wave also lays down a protectable asset, which is how this work defends against copycats. The format cannot be owned: US copyright excludes "any idea, procedure, process, system" ([17 U.S.C. §102(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102)), and the envelope-link gift already exists at SendLove, MiMomento and others ([sendlove.io](https://sendlove.io/template/love-letter); [MiMomento](https://www.mimomento.app/blog/best-digital-gift-page-makers-2026)). The identity can. Since 1 May 2025, EU designs explicitly cover animations and transitions ([Dreyfus](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/05/eu-design-protection-complete-guide-to-the-2025-reform/)), so the seal opening with its chime can be registered for €350 as a first design ([Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102nchy/eu-design-reform-what-you-need-to-know)). The seal-and-name lockup on the preview, the gate and the hallmark becomes a figurative mark worth filing, at about €1,200 per EU mark across four classes ([EUTMR fee annex](https://www.ippt.eu/legal-texts/eu-trade-mark-regulation/eu-trade-mark-regulation-200)), once a clearance search has checked the ETHOS applications already pending in the US ([Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/ethos-98552473)). Consistent placement does for Ethos what Pop Mart's hologram and QR code did for Labubu. It gives viewers an origin mark they learn to look for, and a clone cannot copy it without infringing ([MarqVision](https://wf.marqvision.com/blog/labubu-vs-lafufu)).

## Conclusion

The no-friction rule reorders the playbook rather than limiting it. The instinct is to start with a permanent mark inside paid gifts, because that is where the brand is most visibly missing. But once the name sits on the preview and under the seal at the moment of opening, paid recipients see it anyway, without Ethos breaking a promise it sold. The hallmark then becomes a small second exposure on 14% of opens, carrying the largest legal and trust risk of any move, so it belongs last and behind a switch. The two largest losses the evidence uncovered are not branding problems at all. Half of pay clicks never complete, and that leak sits beside a payment page and wallet sheet that name another company. And nobody knows how many recipients reach the end screen where replies, keeps and shares live. Fixing the account and the measurement comes before fine-tuning the hallmark wording.

The most original idea in the set is the calendar entry. It puts "Send Leo an Ethos gift" into a recipient's own calendar, timed to the week before the occasion, which is exactly when Moonpig's reminders sell. Ethos stores nothing, needs no consent and adds no step for anyone who skips it. Ethos will not be able to see whether it works, beyond the tap. That trade is worth making, because the alternative, email reminders to people who never bought anything, is the most heavily regulated move here. The open uncertainties are concrete and cheap to settle: how each chat app shows a site name, whether a sung brand name feels intimate or intrusive, whether a disclosed TikTok post can show the word "Ethos", and whether Latvia's e-marketing law reads consent the way Spain's does. Each can be answered with one device check, one A/B test, one matched post pair and one lawyer's email before Valentine's 2027.
