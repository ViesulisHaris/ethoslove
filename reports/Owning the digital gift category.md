# Own the name, not the format

The online businesses that became the name people remember for their category (Moonpig, Evite, Partiful, Calendly, Linktree, Wordle, Labubu) did not get there by being first or by protecting the format in law. They won because every unit people passed along carried a proprietary name to someone new, buyers asked for that name rather than a description, and each recipient became a customer the company could reach again: Calendly credits its booking-link loop with **about 70% of signups**, and Moonpig holds **70% of UK online cards** on top of **113 million occasion reminders**. The originators that lost trends they started, FIX with "Dubai chocolate" and Lensa with "magic avatars", lost because the world named the trend with a description anyone could use. Copycat defence follows the same logic: law reliably stops copies of a name, a logo or specific artwork (Wordle-named clones pulled from the App Store, Pop Mart's early wins against fake Labubus) but never the idea, and lawsuits over formats (Squishmallows, Crumbl, Rainbow Loom) settled while sales fell. Ethos is on the losing side of that line today. Its September TikTok hits filled the comments with "PLEASE TELL ME THE WEBSITE", yet TikTok held back the one post that showed the domain, a paying sender's gift never shows the word "Ethos", about 99% of Google clicks come from people typing the brand, and Ethos appears in none of the category searches or roundups where a dozen look-alike makers charging $0–9.90 already sit. The fix starts with the gift itself, the one channel TikTok cannot throttle: a permanent, premium Ethos mark and a named call to action on every gift, paid or free, plus a word and a sound that point only to Ethos and Moonpig-style capture of each recipient's occasion dates. Under that goes a cheap legal floor (trademark clearance and an EU filing, an EU design registration for the signature animations, a takedown routine) for assets that will, in the end, be protected mostly by memory.

## Every category winner made the thing people shared carry its name

The best-documented growth loops in consumer internet share one mechanism. The brand sits exactly where a new person gets value, and that person is one tap from becoming a user. Hotmail added "PS: I love you. Get your free email at Hotmail" to the end of every outgoing email ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/18/ps-i-love-you-get-your-free-email-at-hotmail)). It had more than **30 million active members by July 1999** ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/?p=12288)). Calendly works because every booking needs two people. Its then-CPO, Annie Pearl, said "**seventy percent of our signups come through that viral loop**" ([Lenny's Podcast transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/lennyspodcast/behind-the-scenes-of-calendly-s-rapid-growth-annie-pearl-cpo)). Calendly raised only $550k before a $350M round at a valuation above **$3B** in 2021 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2021/01/27/tope-awotonas-calendly-raises-350m/)). Typeform ends every free form with a "Create a typeform" button ([Typeform Help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029262372)). Loom ends shared videos with "Make a Loom" ([Product Fruits](https://productfruits.substack.com/p/how-loom-went-from-zero-to-being)) and had more than 25 million users when Atlassian agreed to buy it for about **$975M** ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/atlassian-to-acquire-video-messaging-platform-loom-for-approx.-$975-mln-in-cash-stock)). None of these companies published a viral coefficient. Calendly's 70% is the closest public figure to "recipients become senders", and it comes from a product the recipient *must* use in order to get value.

The gifting and greetings analogues push the mechanism further: they capture the recipient as a person. Every Partiful invite shows guests a branded cover ([NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/partiful-marketing-strategy/)). Guests RSVP with a name, phone number and texted code ([Party.pro](https://party.pro/partiful/)), and hosts never get those contacts ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/05/23/partiful-founders-startup-raises-series-a/)), so the guest network belongs to Partiful. Sensor Tower estimated its monthly users rose **400% year on year in Q1 2025** ([CNBC via NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/?amp=1)). Moonpig, the clearest category owner in the set, grew its share of UK online cards from 60% in 2019 to **70% in 2023** ([Moonpig Group](https://www.moonpig.group/investors/investment-case/)). The engine behind it is the date of the next occasion. In its year to April 2026 Moonpig held **113 million reminders**. Around **40% of orders were placed within seven days of a reminder**, and 1.2 million Plus members placed about a quarter of Moonpig orders, on revenue of £373.0m at a 28% EBITDA margin ([Moonpig FY26 results](https://www.moonpig.group/media/moonpig-group-plc-fy26-results-announcement.html)). Management says it consistently earns **over 90% of revenue from existing customers** ([Moonpig H1 FY26 script](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/moonpig-presentation-script-moonpig-group-plc-fy26-half-year-results-presentation-script-final-ce7d51d2da81f423)). One card captures one date, and that date pulls the next purchase.

How the brand appears on the gift matters as much as whether it appears, and the cases split into two models. Evite treats branding as a tax. Free invitations show banner ads to guests, and Premium sells "an ad-free guest experience" ([Evite FAQ](https://evite.com/cms_proxy/61c4debfe6329744f06c60e1/648cd3dda08b27df921dc7b4_evite_faq.pdf)). Close to 10% of users pay ([US Chamber](https://www.uschamber.com/co/good-company/the-leap/evite-revamps-business-model-amid-pandemic)), rising later to **1 in 5 sending premium invitations, which made up two-thirds of revenue** ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/retail-deals/2023/11/30/a-brand-new-evite-invites-subscribers)). But Evite sells the removal of third-party ads, not of its own name. The other model is branding as a badge. Hallmark's crown has been a registered mark since 1950 ([Company-Histories](https://www.company-histories.com/Hallmark-Cards-Inc-Company-History1.html)). Paperless Post built skeuomorphic envelopes meant to evoke ripping open a real one ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/15/paperless-post-20m-august-capital/)) and has sent more than **650 million invitations** ([How I Built This](https://wondery.com/shows/how-i-built-this/episode/10386-paperless-post-james-and-alexa-hirschfeld/?queryID=6eecc1c8fd4cb3ddd1bfcecd6266d469)). Tiffany's founder reportedly refused to sell the blue boxes on their own, so the box proved a real purchase ([CNN Style](https://edition.cnn.com/style/article/tiffany--co-blue)). Software companies that do charge for removal gate it at a mid tier, not the cheapest: Calendly at Standard ([Calendly Help](https://help.calendly.com/hc/en-us/articles/35448706343319-How-to-turn-off-Calendly-branding-on-your-scheduling-page)), Linktree at Pro ([Linktree Help](https://intercom.help/linktree-ff524ba1864c/en/articles/5434182-how-to-remove-the-linktree-logo)), Typeform at Plus. Typeform's documentation shows the cost openly: **buying "no branding" also switches off the "Create a typeform" recruitment button** ([Typeform Help](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029262372)). Overt watermarks also carry a growing penalty on social platforms. Since February 2021 Instagram has made Reels with other apps' logos or watermarks less discoverable ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/)). Spotify Wrapped shows the alternative: a share card designed so well that Spotify counts screenshots as shares. That added up to **500M+ shares on the first day of Wrapped 2025** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)).

The two collapses in greetings show that reach is not ownership. In 1999 Blue Mountain held an estimated **65% of the online greeting-card market** with about 9 million monthly visitors ([Next TV](https://www.nexttv.com/news/excitehome-buys-blue-mt-eyeballs-382256)). Excite@Home paid **$780M** for it ([InternetNews](https://www.internetnews.com/it-management/excitehome-buys-blue-mountain-arts-for-780-million/)), and American Greetings bought it two years later for **$35M** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mountain_Arts)). Its huge user base had produced "not one cent" of e-card sales ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/19991201/15696.html)). Cameo sold a high-ticket novelty with no built-in repeat occasion, and its last round was a cramdown about **90% below its $1B peak** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/30/cameo-app-unicorn-silicon-valley-startup-celebrity-influencer-social-media/)). Owning a channel or a moment without a paid, recurring job left nothing to defend.

## The word people say decides who collects the demand

FIX Dessert Chocolatier created one of the biggest viral food products of the decade and captured little of the category it started. Its pistachio-and-kataifi bar was called "Can't Get Knafeh of It" ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2540507/creators-of-viral-dubai-chocolate-call-out-copycats-for-damaging-their-brand?amp=1)). FIX said it had no website, no store and no authorised resellers ([Yahoo UK](https://uk.style.yahoo.com/being-scammed-viral-dubai-chocolate-113100220.html)). As its co-founder put it in July 2026, "**The world named it Dubai chocolate**" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/food/2026/07/07/fix-founders-on-creating-the-dubai-chocolate-phenomenon-the-world-named-it/)). By May 2025 Walmart, Shake Shack and Trader Joe's were selling it ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/31/walmart-shake-shack-trader-joes-dubai-chocolate.html)). A search for FIX's own name returned impostor sites, and an Etsy listing sold a "Fix" bar for **£56** ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2025/09/15/dubai-chocolatier-condemns-europes-counterfeit-knafeh-craze)). The German court victory over the term came from a different importer and protects Dubai *origin*, not FIX ([Harte-Bavendamm](https://www.harte-bavendamm.de/en/ip-blog/higher-regional-court-of-cologne-provides-clarity-dubai-chocolate-remains-an-indication-of-origin)). Lensa's "Magic Avatars" lost the same way, only faster. Within about **two weeks** of its peak, AI photo editors held the top three US App Store spots, and rivals had stuffed "AI avatars" into their app names to catch Lensa's traffic ([iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2022/12/13/ai-art-apps-taking-over-app-store-charts/)). Lensa's 2023 revenue fell **58%** ([Business of Apps](https://www.businessofapps.com/data/lensa-ai-statistics)).

The originators that held on longer owned a proprietary noun and printed it on the product. Every genuine Labubu carries a holographic Pop Mart sticker and a QR code that can be checked on camera ([MarqVision](https://wf.marqvision.com/blog/labubu-vs-lafufu)). Squishmallows turned one plush into **400 million units across 3,000 named styles** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squishmallows)). Wordle's shared grid named the game in every post, and Apple removed clones that used the Wordle name ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/wordle-new-york-times/)).

The table also shows a limit. A proprietary name buys years instead of weeks, but it does not make a company permanent. Pop Mart, Stanley and Crumbl (whose 2026 sales collapse forced dozens of closures, per [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/buying-a-franchise/crumbls-sales-are-crumbling-and-its-ceo-says-its-my-fault)) all faded once novelty passed. What separated a moment from a company was a second hit, such as NYT's Connections or Pop Mart's non-Labubu lines. Where the format was easy to copy, the originators that captured the most value often sold at the peak: Wordle within weeks, and BeReal for **€500M** even after its users fell from 15M to 6M ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/15/deal-dive-bereal-got-its-best-case-scenario-exit); [SearchLogistics](https://www.searchlogistics.com/learn/statistics/bereal-statistics/)).

Marketing science explains why the name has to travel with the thing. In the Ehrenberg-Bass school, brands grow mainly by reaching more category buyers. A brand gets bought when it comes to mind at "category entry points", the situations that lead into a purchase, such as an anniversary, a long-distance relationship or a last-minute Valentine's gift ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/research-services/identifying-and-prioritising-category-entry-points/)). Distinctive assets such as a logo, a colour or a character only work as shortcuts once they are both **famous and unique to one brand** ([Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/brands-need-distinctive-assets/)). Jenni Romaniuk warns that while an asset is being built it must be shown *with* the brand name, a process she calls "long" and requiring "patience" ([Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/mumbrella360-ehrenberg-bass-professor-says-youre-poorly-branding-if-youre-focusing-on-assets-over-your-brand-832843)). Recall research makes the same point. Recurring brand characters lifted spontaneous brand recall by **up to 25%** in System1 and Lumen's work ([LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/research-by-system1-shows-memorable-characters-in-ads-boost-chances-of-profit-by-30)). Sonic logos that include the brand name were about **9 times better at correct attribution**, and without the name accuracy fell to **18%** ([MarComm News](https://marcommnews.com/largest-ever-sonic-brand-tracking-study-reveals-the-best-performing-sonic-logos-of-2025/); [Podcast News Daily](https://www.podcastnewsdaily.com/news/report-brand-name-audio-cues-outperform-music-only-logos/article_7325325b-9d2f-409e-839c-04378322daee.html)). The TikTok cases with the best brand outcomes fit this. In each, the viral unit could not be separated from the name. e.l.f.'s commissioned song spelled out "Eyes. Lips. Face." and passed **1 billion views in its first week** ([Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/marketers-on-fire-e-l-f-cosmetics-cmo-kory-marchisotto)). CeraVe seeded "Michael Cera invented CeraVe" through **400–450 influencers** ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/10/02/ogilvy-spread-conspiracy-theory-about-michael-cera-promote-cerave-it-worked)). Duolingo's "dead owl" stunt took daily mentions from about 11,000 to **59,900+** ([Meltwater](https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/duolingo-dead-mascot-campaign)).

The deliberate lever these companies controlled was the noun in the call to action, not the verb in culture. "Create a typeform" and "Make a Loom" teach every recipient a word for the thing they just received. "Venmo me" and "send a Partiful" appear to have emerged on their own ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e7c8f1d8d5a05e35)), while Evite's press kit tells writers to say "an Evite invitation", not "an Evite" ([Evite Press Kit](https://partners.evitecdn.com/Press%20Kit/Press%20Kit/Evite_PressKit_02.2019.pdf)). That caution is sensible. Thermos lost its mark in 1962 after encouraging everyday use of the word ([Lovemoney](https://www.lovemoney.com/gallerylist/575033/59-brand-names-that-unexpectedly-became-everyday-words)), and Escalator was cancelled after even Otis used the word generically ([AGIP](https://www.agip.com/News/en/articles/23506)). In the EU, *Kornspitz* lets a mark be revoked when the owner fails to make resellers present it as a trademark ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/bel/legal-updates/revocation-of-successful-trademarks-that-became-common-in-the-trade)). Velcro's "Don't Say Velcro" video was explicitly a way to create a record of policing ([World IP Review](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/refreshing-amusing-and-a-genericide-deterrent-the-don-t-say-velcro-video-14671)). In the US, verb use alone does not make a mark generic (*Elliott v. Google*, [Mondaq](https://mondaq.co.uk/unitedstates/trademark/738404/&location=articleauthorphototop)). As for the name itself, real words such as "Ethos" are recalled better than invented ones ([Lerman & Garbarino 2002](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/recall-and-recognition-of-brand-names-a-comparison-of-word-and-nonword-name-types/812456365703299073-7792)). Their cost is ownership, not memory: crowded trademark registers and search results shared with unrelated brands.

## Law stops copies of identity, never copies of the idea

Being first is not itself a moat. In Golder and Tellis's study of about 500 brands in 50 categories, true market pioneers had a **47% failure rate** and a mean market share of about 10%, while "early leaders" who entered later did far better ([Golder & Tellis 1993](https://people.duke.edu/~moorman/Marketing-Strategy-Seminar-2015/Session%205/Golder%20and%20Tellis.pdf)). The popular claim that a "category king" takes 76% of its category's value comes from a single unreplicated analysis by consultants who sell category design ([Stanford eCorner transcript](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/how-to-be-a-category-king-entire-talk-transcript.pdf)). Lieberman and Montgomery's three sources of first-mover advantage are technology leadership, pre-empting scarce assets and buyer switching costs ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/how-first-mover-advantage-once-unquestioned-lost-its-grip-on-entrepreneurs-and-business-researchers/)). A gift page sent as a link has almost none of them.

The idea of a personalised, animated gift page sent by link cannot be owned. US copyright excludes "any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept" ([17 U.S.C. §102(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102)), and EU copyright protects only original expression. The digital-gift space proves the point every day. The Valentine's "runaway No button" website exists as free open-source code ([GitHub](https://github.com/pandaboi1/Valentine)). Link-sent bouquets spread across a sprawl of unrelated "digibouquet" sites ([digibouquet.net](https://digibouquet.net/); [digibouquet.cc](https://digibouquet.cc/)), and competitors publish "alternatives to" pages that harvest the originals' traffic ([Attached](https://www.attachedapp.com/attached-blog/link-bouquet-alternatives-6-websites-like-link-bouquet-to-send-a-bouquet-by-link)). Lawsuits against similar-looking or similar-tasting products rarely produce a knockout. Jazwares' trade-dress suit against Build-A-Bear's Squishmallow look-alikes ended in a dismissal with undisclosed terms and no ruling on the merits ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/squishmallow-maker-ends-trade-dress-suit-against-build-a-bear)). Crumbl was denied an injunction against Dirty Dough and settled ([Franchise Times](https://www.franchisetimes.com/franchise_news/dirty-dough-crumbl-reach-settlement-regarding-trade-secrets/article_c13aa9f4-8883-11ee-9388-cbdefc9a7cb9.html)). Rainbow Loom had 7 of 11 challenged patent claims fall in review ([Patentology](https://blog.patentology.com.au/2014/08/rainbow-loom-patent-claims-rejected-for.html)) while sales sank to about a tenth of peak ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Loom)).

Actions against copies of *identity*, meaning a name, a logo or specific artwork, work much better. Pop Mart's counsel reports that a court found Pop Mart likely to win its trademark and copyright case against counterfeit Labubus sold through 7-Eleven ([Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/publications/trademark-copyright-protection-victory-against-counterfeit-labubus/)). Cameo, a common English word, won a preliminary injunction that stopped OpenAI calling a Sora feature "Cameo", because the court found the mark suggestive rather than descriptive ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/federal-court-rules-that-openai-must-stop-using-the-term-cameo-124559072.html)). For a digital product, EU law changed in a useful way. Since the design reform took effect on 1 May 2025, EU designs expressly cover **animation, transitions and graphical user interfaces**, filed as MP4 ([Dreyfus](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/05/eu-design-protection-complete-guide-to-the-2025-reform/)). Official fees are €350 for one design and €125 each for designs 2–10 in a multiple filing ([Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102nchy/eu-design-reform-what-you-need-to-know)). The free unregistered EU design right lasts only three years and protects only against proven copying. In the US, a foreign owner can sue without registering a copyright. But statutory damages and attorney's fees, up to **$150,000 per work** for wilful infringement, require registration before infringement or within three months of publication ([Dorsey & Whitney](https://www.dorsey.com/trademark-copyright-advertising/insights/dont-get-berned-an-important-limitation-on-enforcement-of-foreign-copyrights-under-u-s-law); [Donahue](https://donahue.com/resources/publications/copyrights-registered-u-s/)). US trade dress for a product's look needs proof of acquired recognition (*Wal-Mart v. Samara*, [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/99-150)), which a young brand does not have. In practice, enforcement for a small brand means platform notices: DMCA notices, EU Digital Services Act Article 16 notices ([Springlex](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-16/)) and Etsy's reporting portal ([Etsy IP policy](https://etsy.com/legal/ip/)). These are free and fast against near-identical copies, and weak against "same style" listings. For look-alike domains, the UDRP costs **$1,500**, and **79%** of WIPO cases in 2025 ended in transfer ([WIPO fees](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/amc/domain-name-disputes/fees); [Dreyfus](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/01/28/what-do-wipos-record-2025-statistics-reveal-about-the-surge-in-domain-name-disputes/)), but it requires trademark rights first.

When a large platform copied a category leader, the leader survived if the platform copied the capability but not the job. Apple Invites launched in February 2025, but hosting requires a paid iCloud+ subscription ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/04/apple-launches-app-for-party-invitations-in-recurring-revenue-push-.html)). Two months later Partiful's CEO said, "We haven't really seen any users that have been leaving" ([CNBC via NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/meet-partiful-the-gen-z-party-planning-staple-thats-taking-on-apple/6231135/?amp=1)). Instagram added five bio links in April 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/18/instagram-takes-on-linktree-and-others-with-support-for-up-to-5-links-in-bio/amp)), yet Linktree went from 35M to **50M+ users** by May 2024 ([Yahoo/TechCrunch](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/linktree-surpasses-50m-users-rolls-131957288.html)). Wrapped kept its cultural lead over Apple Music Replay on share design ([SoundGuys](https://www.soundguys.com/spotify-wrapped-vs-apple-music-replay-149834/)). Zoom kept the verb, but once bundled rivals reached workplaces its self-serve segment grew just **1.4%** in the quarter to July 2025 ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001585521/000158552125000138/zm-20250731ex991.htm)). A copy is most dangerous when it is free, preinstalled and cross-platform. A leader is safest when its recipient experience works for anyone with a link and its brand rides on its own domain.

## Ethos is running the Dubai-chocolate playbook today

The demand is real. Two September posts reached **1.3M and 984.8K views**, and the top comment on one was "PLEASE TELL ME THE WEBSITE" ([VIRAL-FORMULA.md](../docs/marketing/VIRAL-FORMULA.md)). From 6 to 23 September Ethos made **706 paid purchases worth €5,332**, and one hit was measured at about **€1,900 in 48 hours** ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). But the conversion from views to brand runs entirely through memory. Of 7.4K Google clicks from 18 September to 22 October, about **99% came from people typing the brand** ("tryethos", "tryethos. io", "try ethos" and typos). Google Lens queries such as "what website is that?" show viewers trying to recover a name they were never shown ([SEO-ORGANIC.md](../docs/marketing/SEO-ORGANIC.md)). Brand clicks spike on hit days (1,651 on 21 September) and fall back to 23–90 a day. Late-October posts stalled at about 500 views ([VIRAL-FORMULA.md](../docs/marketing/VIRAL-FORMULA.md)). Like FIX's bar, the thing going viral is a format ("my boyfriend made me a website") that viewers cannot attach to a name.

The brand is missing from the TikTok posts, and Ethos has partly chosen that. On 16 October the first post showing "tryethos.io" got **0 views**, and TikTok support said its visibility was limited for a Community Guidelines violation. The rule since then has been: no site name on screen, and the brand only in the bio and comments ([VIRAL-FORMULA.md](../docs/marketing/VIRAL-FORMULA.md)). What caused the restriction is an inference. TikTok's own pages say the "Your brand" disclosure setting **does not affect distribution**, and that undisclosed promotion can be restricted ([TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/promoting-a-brand-product-or-service?lang=en)). The September 2025 guidelines reduce the visibility of content that sends users to buy off-platform where TikTok Shop operates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/15/tiktoks-new-guidelines-add-subtle-changes-for-live-creators-ai-content-and-more/)), and QR codes are usually ineligible for the For You feed ([TikTok FYF Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards/)). No TikTok document found says a plain on-screen URL alone triggers a restriction. Undisclosed promotion that also named an off-platform shop is the more plausible cause. Either way, on TikTok the name can travel only as a spoken word, a search term, a sound or a recognisable look.

The brand is also missing from the gift itself, which matters more, because the gift is the channel TikTok cannot limit. The words "Made with Ethos" appear only on free gifts and free replies. Any unlock removes them ([publish.ts](../src/lib/gift/publish.ts); [Watermark.tsx](../src/templates/_shared/Watermark.tsx)), and the terms of service promise "no footer" with every unlock ([legal.ts](../src/content/legal.ts)). The chat preview card is a sealed envelope that, since 16 October, deliberately carries no web address ([card-art.ts](../src/lib/gift/card-art.ts)). Its title reads "{name}, someone made you something". The sound gate and loading screens show an unlabelled wax-seal logo, and the end screen says "Made by {sender} for {recipient}". So a recipient of a premium Birthday Cinema, Bouquet or Pop-up Card, the templates where about **60% of starters pay** against **4.5%** for the free Letter ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)), meets the most impressive version of the product with no name to remember. The paid "no footer" extra is Evite's tax model applied to Ethos's own name. And Ethos has not yet built Typeform's partial protection, a "send one back" call to action that names the brand.

Ethos is also missing wherever new buyers search. In Google results on 24 October, gift cards owned "digital gift". Single-page niche sites won "digital gift for boyfriend", "online love letter" and "digital bouquet", and Ethos ranked for no non-brand phrase it checked ([SEO-ORGANIC.md](../docs/marketing/SEO-ORGANIC.md)). The 2026 roundups that AI assistants draw on are written by competitors that rank themselves first, and none names Ethos: LovePanda ([lovepanda.co](https://lovepanda.co/blog/best-digital-gift-website)), MiMomento ([mimomento.app](https://www.mimomento.app/blog/best-digital-gift-page-makers-2026)) and Letters by Heart ([lettersbyheart.com](https://lettersbyheart.com/blog/best-website-to-make-love-letters-online-comparison)). The only sites linking to tryethos.io are two scam checkers ([SEO-ORGANIC.md](../docs/marketing/SEO-ORGANIC.md)). On one non-Google engine, a search for "tryethos.io" puts Gridinsoft's **"60/100 Trust Score"** page first ([Gridinsoft](https://gridinsoft.com/online-virus-scanner/url/tryethos-io)). The handles are split: Pinterest @tryethos belongs to someone else ([replies-and-pins.md](../docs/marketing/etsy/replies-and-pins.md)), Instagram @tryethos did not exist on 19 September ([brand.ts](../src/config/brand.ts)), and tryethos.app is an unrelated journaling app that appears under brand searches. In the Meta ad test, every panel group asked for **reviews, a company name, a refund line and payment logos** ([PROFITABLE-ADS.md](../docs/marketing/PROFITABLE-ADS.md)), and as of 23 September checkout read "Pay SECUORA" ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). That puts a third name in front of buyers at the moment they have to trust the site.

The competitive field shows how short the window is. Close equivalents already exist. SendLove sends a free "instant envelope link" ([sendlove.io](https://sendlove.io/template/love-letter)). MiMomento offers scratch-to-reveal, counters and a song for **$2.99** ([MiMomento](https://www.mimomento.app/blog/best-digital-gift-page-makers-2026)). AIskyla markets itself as free with "no watermark" ([aiskyla.com](https://aiskyla.com/create/love-letter)). Etsy and Gumroad sell love-letter and birthday "websites" for $2–60 ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1857223924/customizable-website-digital-love-letter); [Gumroad](https://elementorcraft.gumroad.com/l/rgvvuw)), and TikTok discover pages for "gift website for boyfriend" feature Joygram, Love Capsule and Canva sellers ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/gift-website-ideas-for-boyfriend)). The research found no evidence that any of them copied Ethos, because the format was already common. That is exactly the problem: a copy needs no copying. What Ethos has that they lack is breadth (54 animated templates), voice notes, a real song, scheduling, the free reply and Spanish. Those differences only become a brand if recipients see them attached to a name. The name itself is a mixed asset. "Ethos" is short and easy to recall in English and Spanish, but in the US Ethos AI has a pending ETHOS application in Classes 9, 36, 42 and 45, filed in May 2024 ([Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/ethos-98552473)). Ethos Technologies, the life insurer, filed new ETHOS applications in 2026 ([Markinton](https://markinton.com/trademark/ethos-99619730)). And the EU register has not been checked at all.

## A playbook to make "Ethos" the word recipients ask for

### Put a permanent, premium Ethos mark inside every gift

The single highest-leverage change is to stop removing the name from paid gifts while still selling what buyers actually want removed: the ad-like pill. Keep "no footer" in every unlock, as the terms promise, and add a small, permanent maker's mark that works as a badge rather than an ad. It could be a monogrammed wax seal with the word "Ethos" on the sound gate, the same seal beside "Made by {sender} for {recipient}" on the end screen, and the wordmark pressed into the seal on the chat preview envelope. Paperless Post's envelopes and Tiffany's box show that branding which signals a real, cared-for purchase adds perceived value to a gift. Moving the mark onto the seal also follows Romaniuk's rule that a new asset must be co-presented with the name until it is famous. That matters because envelopes and seals are category signals that competitors already use. If buyers object, gate full removal at the "Everything" tier or offer it as an opt-out, as Typeform and Linktree gate removal at mid tiers. Apply the change only to new purchases, with the offer described clearly, because the current terms promise "no footer". Next, rename the call to action, the lever Typeform and Loom documented: "Send {sender} an Ethos gift back" rather than an unnamed "send one back". Pairing the mark with a generic noun teaches recipients a word without inviting genericide. A recipient-side share card ("I got one", with envelope, name and a line from the gift) built for screenshots would carry the look onto Instagram and TikTok without a watermark that platforms demote, as Wrapped and Duolingo's percentile card do. Finally, measure what none of the analogues published. Tag free and paid links separately and track recipient opens, "make one back" taps and recipient-to-sender conversion. That internal viral coefficient is the only way to price what removing the brand costs.

### Give TikTok viewers a word and a sound instead of a URL

Because TikTok will not carry the domain, Ethos needs assets TikTok cannot strip. Every live gift already opens on a "Sound on" gate, which makes it a natural home for a sonic logo. Ethos should commission a two-second opening sting (seal crack, chime, ideally a sung or spoken "Ethos") and play it in every gift and every post. It should be published as original audio from @tryethos so that the sound page credits the brand. Brand-name audio is attributed about nine times better than music alone. Business accounts are already limited to TikTok's Commercial Music Library ([Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/why-can-i-only-use-commercial-sounds-on-tiktok)), so an owned sound costs nothing in reach. Every post that shows the product should carry the "Your brand" disclosure, with no URLs or QR codes on screen. The brand should appear as a search term ("ethos gift") in a pinned comment and in captions, and @tryethos should answer "what's the website" comments with video replies. Stanley's president showed how much a fast brand reply to someone else's viral moment can be worth ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/tiktok-stanley-cups-water-bottle-craze/)). TikTok's keyword search ads ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-updates-search-ads-campaigns-with-keyword-targeting/727957/)) on "ethos", "tryethos" and the category phrases are cheap insurance against clones buying that demand. Treat @tryethos as the place where recognition is stored: persona, recurring characters, the owned sound, and Spark-boosted winners, the way Crumbl boosted its own organic posts ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/inspiration/crumbl-521)). Story accounts are a lab for testing hooks, not the brand. The repo's own legal note flags that posts written "as her" are fictional testimonials under FTC and EU unfair-practice rules ([MONETIZATION.md](../docs/MONETIZATION.md)). Cameo's 30-state settlement over undisclosed endorsements shows the risk is not theoretical ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/30/cameo-app-unicorn-silicon-valley-startup-celebrity-influencer-social-media/)). If Ethos pays clippers at the reported **$1–5 per 1,000 views** ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/wtf-is-clipping-the-low-lift-creator-strategy-grabbing-advertisers-attention/)), it should pay only for clips that show the seal and say the name. Reach is cheap. Attaching it to the brand is the scarce part.

### Turn each recipient into a reminder record

Partiful and Moonpig own the recipient, not the template, and that is the moat a copycat cannot clone overnight. When a recipient taps "Keep this gift", Ethos should ask for that person's own occasion dates (their partner's birthday, their anniversary) and send a reminder a week before. Moonpig gets about 40% of its orders within that window. "Ethos for Two" maps onto Moonpig Plus, but renewals must stay transparent and easy to cancel. Jacquie Lawson's auto-renewals earned it an **F grade** and a stream of complaints ([USA Complaints](https://usacomplaints.com/company/jacquie-lawson/)), and Ethos, as an EU company, faces consumer-law limits as well. Between occasions, the brand stays in the feed through range rather than repetition, the move that kept Pop Mart, Squishmallows and Stanley ahead of copies. Name the 54 templates like characters, release new ones on a visible seasonal schedule, and treat Valentine's and Mother's Day the way Spotify treats December. Crumbl's 2026 collapse is the warning: rotation without a recognisable brand experience wears out.

### Become the answer search engines and AI assistants give

AI assistants lean heavily on third-party sources. Wikipedia is the most-cited domain for ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/top-10-most-cited-domains-ai-assistants)), and among 75,000 brands, YouTube mentions correlated most strongly with being named, at about **0.737** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations)). Adding statistics and quotations raised a source's visibility in generative answers by up to **40%** in the founding GEO study ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)). Ethos's realistic route has four parts. First, a fair, cited comparison page in a niche where every existing roundup is self-serving. Second, the directory listings already drafted, on Product Hunt, AlternativeTo and PeerPush, where Letters by Heart and DigiFlower already appear ([LINK-KIT.md](../docs/marketing/LINK-KIT.md)). Third, one or two editorial gift-guide mentions before Christmas and Valentine's. Fourth, YouTube demos that say "Ethos" next to category words. Every third-party mention should use the same descriptor, such as "Ethos, animated digital gift pages", so that people and models can tell this Ethos from the insurer, the AI company and the dictionary word. The same pages answer the trust gaps the ad panel raised: a named company, a refund line, reviews from Etsy and Product Hunt, and a checkout that reads "Ethos". Spanish may be an open lane. On a non-Google engine, "regalo digital para mi novio" returned editorial lists but no makers. That is a hypothesis to check on Google before investing.

### Lay a legal floor before Valentine's 2027

The legal goal is modest: stop near-identical theft and name confusion, which the cases show law can do, not stop the format, which it cannot. Start with a professional clearance search of the EU register, TMview, the USPTO and WIPO for ETHOS, ETHOS LOVE and phonetic variants. The EU picture is the biggest unknown in this research. TRYETHOS adds little as a mark, because "try" is a non-distinctive prefix. If the search is clear, file an EU trademark for the word mark and, separately, for the monogrammed seal in Classes 9, 38, 41 and 42. Official fees are **€850 + €50 + €150 + €150 = €1,200 per mark** ([EUTMR fee annex](https://www.ippt.eu/legal-texts/eu-trade-mark-regulation/eu-trade-mark-regulation-200)), and an unopposed mark typically registers in 4–6 months ([Briffa](https://www.briffa.com/blog/how-long-is-the-trade-mark-registration-process/)). Class 38 already has the accepted term "transmission of greeting cards online" ([UK IPO](https://www.ipo.gov.uk/t-tmj/tm-journals/2025-032/UK00004241196.html)). Extend to the US within the six-month priority window at **$350 per class**, using the USPTO's ID Manual wording to avoid surcharges ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/usptos-significant-restructuring-trademark-fees-takes-effect-january-18-2025)). File one EU multiple design covering up to ten signature animations (the seal opening, the envelope reveal, the sound gate) for about **€1,475** in official fees. Register key template artwork with the US Copyright Office within three months of publication, and keep dated source files for everything. Claim @tryethos on Instagram, Threads, X (which the site's metadata already names), Snapchat and Reddit, and check tryethos.com and close variants with a registrar. Then run a standing takedown routine against verbatim copies and Ethos-named listings on Etsy, Gumroad, TikTok and the App Store. Pair it with a one-page usage guide for Etsy listings, affiliates and press ("an Ethos gift", never "an Ethos") to meet the *Kornspitz* duty. Expect "similar style" competitors at $3–60 to stay out of reach. All of this is research, not legal advice, and an EU/US trademark attorney should confirm the specifications.

## Conclusion

The evidence changes what "can't be copied" should mean for Ethos. No one can be stopped from building an animated gift page, and in this niche the copies already exist. The thing a rival cannot copy is a name millions of recipients have seen at the moment they were moved, attached to dates Ethos holds and to a sound and seal they recognise. That makes the received gift, not the TikTok post, Ethos's real brand channel. TikTok's crackdown on on-screen domains raises the stakes: when the content cannot carry the name, the product has to. Today's set-up does the reverse, hiding the name on exactly the paid gifts that impress most. The cases also suggest Ethos's most dangerous competitor is not LovePanda or a future Apple feature but a generic phrase. If the world learns to ask for "a digital gift link" or "a website gift", the demand will flow to whoever ranks for it, as it did for FIX. The copy windows in these cases ran from weeks to a year, and the season from Christmas to Valentine's 2027 is the next big gifting peak. So the practical test for the next 100 days is simple to state: track the share of comments, searches and recipients who say "Ethos" rather than a description. If that share does not rise, the virality is building the category for everyone else, and the peak-attention moment that Wordle and BeReal sold into is the time to weigh Ethos's options.

The main uncertainties are worth stating plainly. No company in these cases published a viral coefficient. No test has compared a tasteful maker's mark with a footer or with no branding at all. The EU trademark register for ETHOS was not checked. And what caused TikTok's 16 October restriction is an inference. Each of these can be resolved cheaply: by instrumenting Ethos's own links, by A/B-testing the paid-gift mark, by commissioning one clearance search, and by running the bio-link test the team already planned.
